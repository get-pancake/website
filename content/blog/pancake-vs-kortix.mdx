Kortix calls itself "the open-source AI command center for your company." Founders researching that category also run into [Pancake](/), and the two get compared. They shouldn't be confused.

Kortix is infrastructure: you build your own agent workforce on a shared machine and point it at any process. Pancake is a GTM platform focused on finding customers. It spots people showing buying signals and starts the conversations.

**TL;DR:** Kortix is open-source infrastructure for technical teams who want to build custom agent systems on a shared Linux machine. Pancake is hosted and works out of the box: it finds buyers and opens conversations from your own account. If you have engineers and want to automate many processes on your own stack, Kortix is a serious option. If you are a founder or small B2B team that needs more conversations with buyers, and you don't want to configure Docker containers to get them, Pancake fits.

---

## Why They Get Compared

Kortix states its thesis as "76% agents, 24% humans": a company where agents do most of the execution and humans verify and steer. That is a claim about the whole company.

Pancake goes deep on one part of the company: go-to-market. Its agents find buyers and start conversations. Tell Pancake who you want to reach, and it builds a Play to find them.

So the overlap is smaller than search results suggest. The real difference is scope, and who each product is built for.

---

## What Each Platform Is Actually Building

Kortix is infrastructure. The product is an open-source shared machine (Linux Ubuntu) where every agent runs in the same environment, shares the same filesystem, the same databases, the same credentials. You define agents as markdown files with identities, permissions, tools, and activation rules. Kortix provides the runtime — bash, package managers, Docker containers, cron/webhook triggers, 3,000+ integrations — and you assemble the workforce. The open-source model means you can self-host everything on your own servers.

Pancake is a finished product. You add your website and it builds its GTM Brain: your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections in one shared memory. Its agents watch buying signals, such as people posting about a keyword you pick or companies hiring for roles that match your buyer. New leads arrive each morning with the reason each was picked. You approve them in the app or from Slack, and outreach runs under your name. Every lead gets a personal message in your voice, and you can edit any of them before it goes out. It costs [$99/month per workspace](/pricing), with everything included.

## Where Pancake Fits Better

### No engineering work to start

The most significant practical difference between the two platforms is the time from signup to agents doing useful work.

With Kortix, getting a full agent workforce running requires meaningful engineering work. You write agent configurations in markdown, define activation rules, set up the integrations your agents will use, test execution in the Linux environment, and iterate. For a founder with an engineering background, this is tractable. For a solo founder without one, it is a significant barrier.

Pancake's setup starts with your website, which it reads to learn who buys from you. There are no agent definitions to write and no Linux environment to test. If your team has no engineer, that difference decides the choice.

The gap is time. A month spent writing and testing agent definitions is a month without outreach.

### The agents come with their jobs defined

Kortix gives you the ability to define agents for every function. That ability is valuable — but it means you are making every decision about how those agents should work, what they should have access to, and how they should hand off to each other.

Pancake's workflow is already built. You say who you want to reach, and Pancake builds a Play to find them. Each search runs Discover, Enrich and Qualify, and every lead arrives with why they fit. Outreach follows a set sequence: a light warm-up, then up to three messages. The first message asks one light question tied to the signal instead of pitching. A reply stops the sequence. If you'd rather steer from Claude, ChatGPT or Codex, connect it through Pancake's MCP server and run your Plays from there.

For founders who want to own the agent design decisions, Kortix's model is more appropriate. For founders who want prospecting done without the architecture work, Pancake's ready-made workflow is the shorter path.

### A focused product is easier to judge

Kortix's positioning is broad: "Take process-heavy companies and turn them into AI-operated ones." That is a migration from an existing state. Its value depends on which processes you rebuild and how well you build them.

Pancake is built around one outcome, so you can judge it on one question: do the leads fit your buyer, and does the stated signal make sense for each? [See how Pancake finds your buyers](/).

## Where Kortix Has Genuine Advantages

### Full control of the stack

For technical teams that want to own every layer of their agent infrastructure, Kortix's open-source model is a genuine advantage. You can inspect the code, modify the agent runtime, add integrations the platform does not support natively, and deploy on your own infrastructure.

That control matters if your company handles sensitive data you cannot send through third-party systems, or if your workflows require custom technical implementations that a managed SaaS cannot accommodate.

### Self-hosting for data-sensitive operations

Kortix can run entirely on your own servers. For regulated industries — healthcare, finance, legal — where data residency requirements are strict, that option can be a compliance requirement rather than a preference.

Pancake is hosted by design, so if on-premise deployment is a requirement for your situation, Kortix is the relevant option.

### Engineering-native agent design

If your team has engineering bandwidth and wants agents that are deeply integrated with your technical systems — running scripts, managing infrastructure, orchestrating DevOps workflows — Kortix's Linux-native environment is the right fit. Agents with bash access and Docker can do things that managed SaaS agents are not designed for.

The agent-as-code model also fits teams that already use version control for configuration. Kortix's cron and webhook definitions are git-versionable — which means agent configuration follows the same review process as the rest of the codebase.

## The Decision Framework

The right platform depends on the answers to three questions.

**What do you need agents to do?** If the answer is prospecting, meaning finding buyers and starting conversations, Pancake does that out of the box. If it's many internal processes across engineering, operations and data, Kortix gives you the environment to build them.

**Do you have engineering bandwidth for infrastructure work?** If yes, Kortix's flexibility is a real asset. If no, Pancake asks for none.

**Do you need to own the infrastructure?** If data residency or compliance requires on-premise deployment, Kortix is the platform that supports it. If a hosted service works for you, Pancake removes the infrastructure work entirely.

Most solo founders and small B2B teams whose bottleneck is pipeline will find Pancake closer to what they need. Technical teams automating an existing business, or working under compliance and infrastructure requirements, will get more from Kortix's flexibility. Pancake's proof is easier to check yourself. The 3-day free trial, which needs a credit card to start, runs on your own website and your own market. Look at the leads it picks and the signal behind each one, and decide whether those are people you'd want to talk to.

## Who Should Pick Which

**Choose Pancake if:**
- You are a founder or small B2B team and your bottleneck is finding buyers
- You have no engineering capacity to spare on agent infrastructure
- You want leads each morning with the signal that picked each one, and outreach that runs from your own account
- You'd rather approve leads than design workflows

**Choose Kortix if:**
- You have engineering bandwidth and want full control over the agent stack
- You need self-hosted deployment for compliance or data residency reasons
- You are migrating an existing process-heavy business to AI-operated workflows
- You want agents that can run bash scripts, manage infrastructure, and work in a shared Linux environment

The two rarely collide in practice. Kortix answers "how do we give our team a shared AI infrastructure to run processes on?" Pancake answers "who should we be talking to this week, and how do we start?"
