**TL;DR:** Viktor is a strong general AI employee for Slack and Microsoft Teams. The closest like-for-like alternatives are Lindy and Anthropic's Claude Tag. Dust and Glean fit teams that mostly need answers from company knowledge. Zapier Agents and Relevance AI fit teams that want to build their own agents. If you wanted Viktor to bring in customers, a focused tool like Pancake handles that one job, from buying signal to first message.

We checked every feature and price below on each vendor's own site in September 2026.

## What Viktor does well

Viktor calls itself an AI employee. It lives in Slack and Microsoft Teams and connects to more than 3,200 tools, including HubSpot, Salesforce, Linear, Notion, Stripe and GitHub. You mention it in a thread. It does the work in its own cloud environment, where it can run code and build reports, dashboards and internal tools.

It keeps what it learns about your company. You can show it a task once with a screen recording, and it writes the steps down as a skill your team can review. Scheduled tasks handle repeat work. It can also post a weekly briefing to a channel without being asked.

It asks before anything you can't undo. A customer email, for example, waits in the Slack thread until someone on the team approves it.

On price, the $100 of starting credits needs no card. Billing is per workspace, not per seat, and every plan includes every integration.

For a team that wants one generalist to take work from anyone, Viktor is a sound pick. Our [Viktor vs Pancake side-by-side](/blog/viktor-vs-pancake) lays out where it wins.

## Why people look for Viktor alternatives

Five reasons come up when teams compare.

- **Credit spend.** Every task uses credits, and long multi-tool jobs use more. Viktor's own blog publishes a guide to cutting credit costs, and it offers a cheaper model tier to stretch them. Teams that want a fixed monthly number compare per-seat or flat plans.
- **Security paperwork.** Viktor lists SOC 2 Type 1, with Type 2 and ISO 27001 in progress. Some procurement teams need Type 2 today.
- **An AI vendor you already pay for.** If your company runs on Claude Team or Enterprise, Claude Tag brings a shared @Claude into Slack without a second contract.
- **Knowledge over tasks.** Some teams mostly want correct answers from their docs, tickets and wikis. Search-first tools are built for that.
- **One outcome that matters most.** A generalist spreads its attention across every function. When the goal is one number, such as new customers, a specialist can own it end to end.

## How we picked these alternatives

Each tool below had to meet three tests. It does work from a chat surface or runs agents on a schedule. It is live and sold in September 2026. It has a public way to start, self-serve or demo. We left out plain chatbots and workflow builders with no agent layer. The list starts with the closest replacements and ends with narrower tools.

---

## 1. Lindy: the closest like-for-like swap

Lindy sells itself as an AI teammate for the whole team. It answers in Slack channels and DMs. It also works in Gmail and iMessage, and it joins Zoom, Meet and Teams calls as a named participant. It connects to more than 1,000 tools and supports MCP. Its memory lives in plain files you can open and edit, so you can see what it knows. Recurring work runs on a schedule, such as a morning brief or a Monday report.

**Pricing (September 2026):** Plus costs $29.99 per seat per month, Pro $99.99 and Max $199.99, with more credits at each step. All seats sit on one workspace bill and share a credit pool. There is no free plan. Teammates who join through Slack get their first week free.

**How it differs from Viktor:** the shape is similar and the billing is not. Viktor charges per workspace, with no fee per person. Lindy charges per seat, so cost follows headcount. Lindy's integration catalog is smaller, and it reaches into personal channels like email and iMessage.

**Pick it if** you like Viktor's model and want a second quote, or a teammate that also works in your inbox.

---

## 2. Claude Tag: a shared @Claude in Slack

Anthropic launched Claude Tag in beta on June 23, 2026. It puts one shared Claude in each Slack channel, and anyone in the channel can tag it. It builds context from the channel over time. It can schedule tasks for itself and work on a project over hours or days. You connect it to the tools, data and codebases you choose. With ambient behavior turned on, it posts updates before you ask.

**Pricing (September 2026):** Claude Tag comes with the Claude Team and Enterprise plans. Team standard seats cost $20 per month billed annually, or $25 billed monthly. Enterprise costs $20 per seat plus usage at API rates. The individual Pro and Max plans don't include it.

**How it differs from Viktor:** it runs in Slack only for now, with no Teams version yet. You pay per seat inside a plan many companies already buy. Viktor has the larger ready-made integration catalog.

**Pick it if** your company already works in Claude and wants one shared AI teammate in Slack.

---

## 3. Dust: agents built on your company's knowledge

Dust lets a team build, share and run agents on top of its own knowledge and tools. You choose from more than 20 models, including GPT, Claude, Gemini, Mistral and DeepSeek. Agents connect to Slack, Notion, GitHub, Google Drive and dozens of other sources, or to any tool through MCP. Dust is SOC 2 Type II certified and offers data residency in the US or the EU.

**Pricing (September 2026, listed in euros):** a free tier with 500 lifetime credits. Pro costs €24 per user per month billed annually (€30 monthly) and includes 8,000 credits. Max costs €120 billed annually (€150 monthly). Credits reset each month and don't roll over.

**How it differs from Viktor:** someone on your team designs the agents. Viktor is one ready-made employee you talk to. Dust gives you more say over which model runs and where your data lives.

**Pick it if** you have a person who will own building agents for sales, support or engineering, and data location matters to you.

---

## 4. Glean: enterprise search with agents on top

Glean is best known for enterprise search and now runs an agent platform on the same index of company data. You can publish agents to Slack, where people trigger them in DMs or channels through Glean in Slack. One limit to know: write actions that need interactive approval in the Glean web app don't complete from Slack.

**Pricing:** not public. Glean sells through demos and sales-led contracts.

**How it differs from Viktor:** Glean is built for large companies with many systems and strict permissions. Viktor is self-serve and starts with free credits.

**Pick it if** you run a large company and your main problem is finding the right answer across many systems.

---

## 5. Zapier Agents: agents across 9,000+ apps

Zapier Agents are AI teammates built on Zapier's catalog of more than 9,000 apps. You give an agent company knowledge and a job, and it runs on command or on a trigger. They work in Slack too. Zapier's templates include a helpdesk agent that answers new questions in a Slack channel.

**Pricing (September 2026):** a free plan with 400 agent activities a month. Pro starts at about $33 a month billed annually, with 1,500 activities. Agent activities are billed apart from regular Zap tasks.

**How it differs from Viktor:** you define each agent's trigger and job up front. Viktor takes open-ended requests in a thread. Zapier wins on app coverage and on a free plan with a monthly allowance.

**Pick it if** your team already runs on Zaps and wants its agents in the same place.

---

## 6. Relevance AI: build your own agent team

Relevance AI is a platform for building specialist agents for sales, customer success, marketing, support, operations and HR. Its sales templates include an outbound prospector and a research-and-enrichment agent. Slack is one of its many integrations.

**Pricing (September 2026):** Pro costs $19 per month billed annually ($29 monthly), with 2,500 actions and $20 of model credits. Team costs $234 per month billed annually ($349 monthly). The free plan is closed to new signups.

**How it differs from Viktor:** Relevance is a builder. You assemble the agents, tools and triggers yourself. Viktor is one employee that plans the steps on its own.

**Pick it if** you want several narrow agents, sales agents included, and you have time to build and tune them.

---

## 7. Slackbot: the AI already in your Slack plan

Every paid Slack plan includes AI summaries, recaps, AI search and workflow generation. Business+ ($15 per user per month billed annually, $18 monthly) adds Slackbot as a personal AI agent that understands your conversations, files and projects. Enterprise+ adds search across connected apps and systems. Salesforce's Agentforce agents can also run in Slack once you connect a Salesforce account.

**How it differs from Viktor:** Slackbot works mostly with what's already inside Slack. Viktor acts across thousands of outside tools.

**Pick it if** your team mostly wants to catch up and search, and you'd rather not add a vendor.

## 8. Pancake: when the real job is finding customers

Some teams look at a general AI employee when what they need is more customers. Pancake is an AI GTM team for founders and small B2B companies, built for that one job. Its agents find people who show buying signals and run outreach to them. It doesn't handle engineering, finance or customer support work, so it won't replace Viktor across the company.

Here is how it runs:

- **Setup.** You add your website. Pancake learns your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections into one shared memory called the GTM Brain. It keeps learning from what works.
- **Signals.** Agents watch six kinds of buying signals: posts on a topic you choose, engagement with a competitor's posts, engagement with a voice your buyers follow, engagement with your own posts, open roles that match your buyer, and job posts that name a tool you replace.
- **Leads.** New leads arrive each morning, each with the signal that picked it. Every new lead also lands in Slack, and you approve it there or in the app.
- **Outreach.** It runs from your own account on the professional network your buyers use: a profile visit, a like on a recent post, an invite with no note, then up to three messages. The first message makes no pitch. It asks one light question tied to the signal.
- **Articles.** Pancake also writes articles built to show up in Google and AI answers, and you approve each one.

**Pricing (September 2026):** $99 per month flat, every agent included, with no seats and no usage billing. The 3-day free trial needs a card to start. Details are on the [pricing page](/pricing).

**How it differs from Viktor:** Viktor takes on a wide range of tasks as people hand them over. Pancake runs one customer-acquisition loop every day, and you approve leads and articles along the way. Viktor bills by credits. Pancake's price doesn't change with how much it does.

**Pick it if** you are a founder or a small B2B team without a sales team, and the goal is more conversations with buyers. Know the limits: Pancake doesn't send email, sync to a CRM or promise meetings.

## Which Viktor alternative should you choose?

Start from the job you hired Viktor for.

- **"One AI employee for the whole team, billed per seat."** Lindy.
- **"We already pay for Claude."** Claude Tag.
- **"Answer questions from our docs and systems."** Dust if you want to build the agents yourself. Glean if you are a large company with many systems.
- **"Automate workflows across hundreds of apps."** Zapier Agents.
- **"Build our own team of narrow agents."** Relevance AI.
- **"Catch up and search inside Slack."** Slackbot.
- **"Find customers and start conversations with them."** Pancake.

Staying on Viktor is a fair answer too. If your team uses it across many functions and the credit spend makes sense, switching may cost more than it saves.
