Kompany and Pancake both come up when founders look for AI that does real work inside a business. The overlap ends there. Kompany is an open-source engine: you give it a directive from the command line, and AI agents build and run a new venture around it. [Pancake](/) is an AI GTM team for a company that already exists. It finds people showing buying signals and runs outreach to them.

If you're a developer who wants to spin up an AI-operated business from a terminal, Kompany is built for you. If you already sell something and need more conversations with buyers, Pancake is the closer fit.

Here's what each one does.

## TL;DR

Kompany is an open-source CLI tool. It gives a project an AI CEO, a routing layer and specialist agents, runs on your own LLM subscription, and builds AI-native ventures from a one-line directive. Pancake is a hosted product for founders and small B2B companies. Its agents watch for buying signals, bring you leads each morning, run outreach from your own account and write articles for Google and AI search. Kompany is for builders starting from zero. Pancake is for companies that already have something to sell.

## What is Kompany?

Kompany (runkompany.com) is an open-source autonomous AI company engine. You send it a one-line directive in natural language — "research the best monetization model for a newsletter about Python tooling" — and an AI CEO classifies it, creates a project with phases and IDs, and routes execution to specialized agents that complete the work: research, building, publishing, whatever the directive requires.

Every irreversible action — a deploy, a payment, a public post — stops at an approval gate. You give a one-word approval and the agent proceeds. Everything else runs autonomously within a per-task budget cap. A persistent ledger records every decision and dollar spent.

The technical setup is intentional: `uvx kompany onboard --yes` installs in under 90 seconds. You bring your own API key from Anthropic, OpenAI, or another supported provider. Kompany then uses Claude Code CLI, Codex CLI, or opencode as the execution layer — running multi-turn sessions on your own subscription.

The team runs a live public proof-of-concept: Nova, an AI voice operating a real business (Swedexpress) with a public daily ledger. Current mission: turn $50 into $1,080. The ledger publishes every decision, expense, and dollar earned.

Kompany is open-source, self-hosted and developer-native. It's infrastructure you run yourself, not a SaaS product.

## What Pancake does

Pancake is built for the part of a business that brings in customers: go-to-market. Setup is adding your website. Pancake reads it and builds its GTM Brain, a shared memory of your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections, and it keeps adding to that memory as it learns what works.

The agents then watch six kinds of buying signals. Some point to people: those posting about a keyword you pick, or engaging with a competitor's posts, an influencer your buyers follow, or your own posts. Others point to companies: those hiring for roles that match your buyer, and those whose job posts name a tool you replace.

Each morning brings new leads, with the signal behind each one. You approve them in the app or from Slack. Outreach runs from your own account on the professional social network: a profile visit, a like on a recent post, an invite with no note, then up to three messages. The opening message asks one light question tied to the signal instead of pitching. The agents also write articles built to show up in Google and AI answers, which you approve.

If you already work in Claude, ChatGPT or Codex, Pancake meets you there. Its MCP server lets your assistant pull up the day's leads, check what the GTM Brain knows about your buyer and kick off outreach mid-chat. Pricing is [$99/month flat](/pricing), with every agent included and no usage billing.

## The Core Difference: Selling vs Building

Kompany and Pancake sit at different points on the company lifecycle.

Kompany is for the build phase. You have a directive — a business idea, a project, a venture — and you want AI to execute it from scratch. The AI CEO creates the project structure, agents execute research and build work, and the ledger tracks every decision. The model assumes you're starting from a blank slate and want AI to construct the operation.

Pancake belongs to the selling phase. You already have a product, a website that explains it and a buyer you can describe. Its agents find more of those buyers every morning and open the conversation.

The typical Kompany user is a solo developer or technical founder who wants to spin up an AI-native business as quickly as possible, with full transparency into what the AI is doing and why, on their own infrastructure.

Pancake fits a founder who has a product to sell and needs a steady flow of buyer conversations without hiring a salesperson.

So they don't compete. Kompany starts a venture. Pancake finds customers for one that already exists.

## When Kompany Makes Sense

Kompany is the right tool when:

- You're a developer and want full control over the AI stack (BYOK, self-hosted, open-source)
- You're building a new AI-native venture and want the AI to construct and operate it from a directive
- You want a transparent ledger of every decision and cost
- You want to plug in your own LLM subscriptions and control your infrastructure
- The "business" you're operating is itself an AI experiment or proof of concept

The Nova demo — an AI voice turning $50 into $1,080 via a publicly audited ledger — is the clearest expression of what Kompany is optimized for: demonstrating and operating AI-native business concepts with full transparency.

## When Pancake Makes Sense

Pancake is the right tool when:

- You have a product or service that already sells, even a little
- You sell to businesses, and your buyers are active on the professional social network
- You want leads each morning, each with the reason it was picked
- You don't want to manage infrastructure, API keys or LLM subscriptions
- You'd rather pay one flat monthly price than meter usage

If the goal is finding buyers for an existing product, Pancake is the managed option. If the goal is to build and run an AI-native venture from zero, Kompany is the more relevant architecture.

## A Note on Open-Source vs SaaS

Kompany's open-source model is a deliberate design choice. It means developers can inspect the routing logic, modify the approval gates, swap in different LLM backends, and self-host everything. That's valuable for teams that want full stack control or are building on top of the engine.

Pancake takes the opposite route. It is hosted and priced flat, so there is no stack to run and no LLM bill to watch. The trade-off is the one every hosted product makes: the agents run on Pancake's side, not on your servers. Paperclip AI is another self-hosted option founders weigh in this space; see [Pancake vs Paperclip AI](/blog/pancake-vs-paperclip) for that comparison.

Neither approach is wrong. They reflect different buyer priorities.

## What Kompany Gets Right

The transparent daily ledger is compelling. Running Nova as a live public experiment, with a real business, real money and published decisions, shows the "nothing hidden" design principle at work. Most autonomous company tools operate as black boxes. Kompany makes every decision legible.

The approval gate model is also well-designed. Agents run free on reversible work. Irreversible actions wait. That's the right boundary for any AI company tool, and Kompany's implementation of it is explicit.

## The Bottom Line

If you're a developer who wants to build an AI-native company from a command line and own your entire stack, Kompany is a serious option. It's well-designed, open-source, and the Nova proof-of-concept shows it can operate a real business.

For a company with a product and too few buyer conversations, Pancake is the more direct tool. It spots people with a reason to care, hands you new leads every morning and, once you approve them, starts the conversation from your own account.
