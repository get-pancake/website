## What lead enrichment does to a record

Lead enrichment turns a thin record into one you can qualify, route and contact. The input is usually a name and a company domain, from a form, an event list or a search. The output is everything a rep or a sequence needs.

Take a hypothetical demo request with three fields: a name, a work email, and the company, "Larkspur Freight." After enrichment the record reads: freight software, 120 employees, Denver; VP of Operations, executive level; email verified; two data analyst roles open; matches the ICP. A rep can now decide in ten seconds whether to call.

Enrichment is not list building, which decides who belongs on the list ([how to build a prospect list](/blog/how-to-build-a-prospect-list) covers that), and it is not intent data. It completes the record once someone is on the list.

## Lead enrichment fields worth paying for

Buy a field only if a rule, a score or a message uses it. Here is what each group covers and when it goes stale.

| Field group | Example fields | Usual source | Goes stale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company | Industry, headcount, revenue band, HQ, founding year | Company databases, the company's site | Slowly; headcount moves first |
| Funding | Last round, date, amount, investors | Funding databases, announcements | At the next round |
| Technology | Tools named in job posts or seen on the site | Job posts, site scanners | When the stack changes |
| Person | Title, seniority, department, profile URL | Contact databases, public profiles | At each job change |
| Contact | Work email, mobile or direct dial | Waterfall providers, verified per lookup | At each job change |
| Signals | Open roles, recent posts, engagement | Job boards, public posts | Within weeks |
| Derived | ICP fit score, persona, owner | Your own rules | When your ICP changes |

Skip revenue band, for example, unless a routing rule or a score reads it. Start with the fields your routing and your first message need, and add others when a decision calls for them.

## How waterfall enrichment works

A waterfall sends each lookup through several providers in a fixed order and stops at the first verified answer. No single provider covers every region, company size and seniority, so a chain of them finds more contacts than any one alone.

1. **Normalize the input.** Full name, company domain, and a profile URL if you have one.
2. **Ask the first provider.** If it returns an email, verify it.
3. **Fall through on a miss.** If the result fails verification or nothing comes back, ask the next provider.
4. **Stop at the first verified result.** Record which provider found it, and the date.
5. **Reorder over time.** Put the providers with the best find rate on your segment first, then sort by price.

Tools package this differently. [Clay](https://www.clay.com/pricing) lets you build your own waterfalls across 150+ providers and bring your own API keys. FullEnrich runs a fixed waterfall across 25+ sources, and lemlist sells a data add-on that draws on 25+ providers.

Before you buy, run 200 contacts whose emails you already trust through each candidate. Compare the found rate, the verified rate and the cost per verified email. Published find rates come from each vendor's own sample, and yours will differ.

| [Pancake](/) (our product) | From $99 a month per workspace, with 60,000 credits | 50 credits | 500 credits | Free |

Contact databases such as Apollo price enrichment inside a seat: the credits come with the plan, pool across the team, and also pay for other features, so the cost of one email is hard to separate out.

Three costs hide behind the per-result price:

- **Expiry.** Apollo's credits expire at the end of each billing cycle. FullEnrich's roll over for 3 months on monthly plans and 12 on yearly ones. lemlist's roll over while you stay subscribed.
- **Platform fees.** lemlist's data credits sit on top of a lemlist plan, and Clay's data credits sit beside the actions that measure platform use.
- **Refreshes.** Every re-check of a stale record is a new lookup, so enriching too early means paying twice.

## Verification: what "verified" means for emails and phones

An enriched email is only worth something if it delivers. Verification runs in layers:

- **Syntax and domain:** the address is well formed and the domain accepts mail.
- **Mailbox check:** the mail server is asked whether the mailbox exists, without sending a message.
- **Catch-all handling:** some domains accept mail for any address, so the mailbox check can't confirm the person. Tools label these "catch-all" or "risky."
- **Phone type:** a mobile or direct line is useful, a switchboard rarely. FullEnrich, for one, returns landlines without charging for them.

FullEnrich says its verification removes about 30% of what its sources return before a result reaches you, a rough measure of how much raw data fails. Send to "risky" addresses last and in small batches, and see [how to automate lead generation](/blog/how-to-automate-lead-generation) for the sending rules that bounces put at risk.

## How often to re-enrich: job changes set the clock

Contact data ages with every job change. In January 2026, [the Bureau of Labor Statistics found](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/tenure.nr0.htm) that 20.6% of US wage and salary workers had been with their current employer for a year or less, and median tenure was 4.1 years.

- **Enrich at the moment of use,** not months ahead in bulk.
- **Re-verify every email** before it enters a new sequence.
- **Treat an out-of-office reply that names a successor** as a job change: update the old record and enrich the new person.
- **Keep the source and date of each field,** so you know which facts are old.

## Lead enrichment and privacy law

Enrichment processes personal data you did not collect from the person, and privacy law reaches that. What follows is a practical summary, not legal advice.

- **Tell people where their data came from.** The [European Commission's GDPR guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en) says that when data comes from another source, you must inform the person, including the source, at the latest within one month of obtaining it or when you first communicate with them.
- **Keep it accurate.** GDPR requires personal data to be accurate and up to date, which makes re-verification a compliance task as well as a deliverability one.
- **Collect less.** Data minimisation means enriching only the fields you use.
- **Check your providers.** Ask where their data comes from, how they handle deletion and opt-out requests, and whether they sign a data processing agreement.
