**TL;DR:** NanoCorp launches new micro-businesses from a single prompt. You describe an idea, and an AI agent builds it and runs it to maximize revenue. [Pancake](/) works on a business you already have. It's an AI GTM team that finds people showing buying signals, runs outreach to the leads you approve and writes articles for Google and AI search. Pick NanoCorp to test ideas with near-zero effort. Pick Pancake to find customers for a product you already sell.

---

Both tools cut the headcount it takes to grow a business. Past that, the problem each one solves, the bet behind it and the founder who gets value from it all diverge.

This post explains where each one fits.

## What NanoCorp does

NanoCorp (nanocorp.so) is a Y Combinator company from the Winter 2024 batch, founded by Pierre-Louis Biojout, formerly CTO of phospho. It is built around one idea: one prompt, one company, AI does the rest. You describe the business you want to exist. An agent spins up the company, builds a landing page, creates a product or Stripe link, and runs cold email outreach to drive revenue. You monitor from a dashboard. You don't have to configure anything.

The pitch is deliberately hands-off. NanoCorp describes each business as a long-running software entity where agents do content, outreach, ops and ads on a schedule, with no human in the loop. The agents run in isolated sandboxes with per-task budget caps and persistent memory, and they report back to the owner. NanoCorp says it has passed $1M in ARR.

What NanoCorp ships: landing pages, simple Stripe-linked products, email outreach and ads. It's early-stage business-building by AI, not deep automation of a company you already run.

**Pricing (as of September 2026):** a free 3-day trial, no card needed, with 15 welcome credits for your first business. The Founder plan costs $30/month for 30 credits and scales to 2,000 credits a month. NanoCorp also takes a 20% fee on the revenue you withdraw.

**What NanoCorp does well:**

- Zero to live business in minutes, with no setup required
- Designed for hands-off autonomy: the AI picks the strategy, not you
- Run a portfolio of micro-businesses from one dashboard
- Purpose-built for testing business ideas at near-zero activation cost
- A clear revenue-share model: NanoCorp keeps 20% of the revenue you withdraw

**Where NanoCorp has limits:**

- Execution centers on new-business basics: landing pages, Stripe links, outreach and ads
- It's built around the businesses it creates, not around the tools and data of a company you already run
- One person runs the businesses, and the businesses are small
- The businesses NanoCorp builds are new AI-created micro-ventures, not your real company

## What Pancake does

Pancake starts from the opposite end: a company that exists and a buyer you can describe. It's an AI GTM team for founders and small B2B companies, with agents that find your buyers and start the conversations.

You add your website. Pancake learns who buys from you (positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof, objections) and stores it in a shared memory called the GTM Brain.

Then three jobs run. Pancake watches six kinds of buying signals and delivers new leads each morning, each tagged with the signal that picked it. It runs outreach to the leads you approve from your own account on the professional social network, opening with a light question instead of a pitch. It writes articles built to show up in Google and AI answers, and you approve them before they go live.

You approve leads in the app or from Slack, or run Pancake from Claude, ChatGPT or Codex through its MCP server. The price is [$99/month flat](/pricing), every agent included. There is no revenue share and no credit system. A 3-day free trial starts with a credit card.

## The founding bet behind each product

NanoCorp's bet is that most of the value in starting a business sits in the early validation loop: picking an idea, spinning up a live presence, driving the first revenue. If AI can compress that loop to near-zero effort, anyone can run ten businesses in parallel and find the one that sticks.

Pancake's bet starts after validation. Once there is a product to sell, finding customers should not stay a pile of disconnected founder tasks. A shared GTM Brain lets signal monitoring, outreach and articles improve one another.

Neither bet is wrong. They're optimizing for different stages.

## Who should use which

**Use NanoCorp if:**

- You want to test a business idea with minimal risk and zero setup: describe it, let the AI run it, see if revenue appears
- You're curious about running a portfolio of micro-businesses in parallel and want one dashboard for all of them
- You don't have an existing product and want the AI to build the whole thing
- You're comfortable with narrow execution today (landing page, Stripe, outreach, ads) while the platform keeps shipping

**Use Pancake if:**

- You sell to other businesses and can describe who buys from you
- You need more conversations with the right people, not a new business idea
- You want each lead to come with a reason: a post on your topic, a like on a competitor's post, a hiring signal
- You want a flat monthly price with no cut of your revenue

**Use both in sequence:** NanoCorp to find out whether an idea earns money; Pancake once you've committed to a direction, sell to businesses and need to reach buyers.

## The compounding difference

Here's what matters most when comparing tools in this category: what does continuous use look like a year from now?

NanoCorp's value compounds if it keeps shipping deeper execution: better ads, better outbound, deeper product automation. The vision is real, and the team is iterating fast. Today, though, it's early-stage execution on a narrow set of actions.

Pancake compounds on your own market. The GTM Brain keeps learning from what works, so the longer it runs, the more it knows about who buys from you and why.
