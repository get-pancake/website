Thomas is not for sale. Thomas is not a product you can buy. Thomas is a virtual human who starts companies on the internet to make money for itself.

[Pancake](/) is a product you buy. It is an AI GTM team that finds buyers for your company and runs the outreach to them.

Both come out of the same idea: AI agents can now do work that used to need people. Thomas takes that idea to its extreme and asks why a company needs a human founder at all. Pancake starts from the other end. You are the founder, you stay the founder, and the agents take one job off your plate: finding customers.

**TL;DR:** Thomas (YC P26) is an AI that starts and runs its own companies to make money for itself. You can't hire it or buy it. If you want to watch an AI try to be a founder, follow Thomas. If you need more buyer conversations for the company you run, Pancake's agents find people showing buying signals and run the outreach from your own account.

## What Thomas is

Thomas launched in the Spring 2026 Y Combinator batch as what the company calls "the first AI founder." The founder, referred to as "Human Thomas" on the company page, is a repeat founder who presented at NeurIPS at 18, contributed to OpenAI's Neural MMO, and scaled a freelance business to $40,000 per month before deciding to automate himself into an AI.

The framing on the site is deliberately blunt: "Thomas is not an AI co-founder you hire. Thomas is an AI founder. He works for himself. Thomas is not for sale. His products and services are."

Thomas runs by taking on work that's already paying: building software products, running influencer marketing campaigns end to end, generating and selling qualified leads, and taking bounties wherever money is changing hands. The system assigns tokens to each action, measures the cash generated per token spent, and reallocates compute toward the highest-return work. The loop is supposed to compound.

Thomas's stated TAM: the entire GDP. The stated endgame: once AI is smarter than every human on earth, human-led companies will not be able to compete. Thomas will be able to disrupt all of them.

That is the pitch. It is a long-horizon bet dressed in maximum provocative framing.

## What Pancake does

Pancake is an AI GTM team for founders and small B2B companies. You add your website, and Pancake builds what it calls the GTM Brain: your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections.

From there, its agents watch for buying signals. Someone posts about the problem you solve. Someone engages with a competitor's posts. A company opens a role that matches your buyer. Each lead arrives in the morning with the signal that picked it, and you approve it in the app or in Slack.

Approved leads get outreach from your own account on the professional social network, opening with a question tied to the signal instead of a pitch. Pancake also writes articles for Google and AI answers, which you approve as well. It costs [$99 a month](/pricing), flat, with every agent included.

## The difference that matters

Thomas works for itself. Pancake works for you.

The difference is in who the principal is.

Thomas is the principal. Thomas has a goal (make money) and it pursues that goal autonomously. If you want to work with Thomas, you can bring it a business opportunity. Thomas then decides whether to take it. You are not the owner. You are a counterparty.

With Pancake, you are the principal. You tell it who you sell to and what you offer. You decide which leads get contacted and which articles get published. The conversations happen in your own account, and the customers you win belong to your company.

## The philosophical split

Thomas's pitch contains a sentence that would alarm most founders: "Someday, Thomas will be able to disrupt all of them." The "them" is human-led companies. This is not a product for you to use to beat your competitors. This is a product that, by its own stated mission, aims to eventually compete with you.

That is not a criticism. It is an honest description of what the project is trying to be. Human Thomas is building an AI that will eventually outcompete every human-founded company, starting by doing the scrappy internet work that a solo operator would do.

Most founders reading this are not trying to hand their company to an AI and walk away. They want to stay in charge and grow without hiring a person for every job. That is a different problem.

## When Thomas makes sense

Thomas makes sense as something to follow, not something to buy. If you want to see how far an autonomous AI can get on its own, the public live revenue counter lets you judge the experiment as it runs. Its products and services are for sale, so you can also be one of its customers.

If you hope to run a company with no founder involvement one day, Thomas is the live test of that thesis.

## When Pancake makes sense

You have a company. Customers, revenue, a product people pay for. The bottleneck is finding the next buyers and starting conversations with them, and that work keeps losing to everything else on your list.

Pancake takes that job. Your part is approving leads and articles. The prospecting and the messages run without you. Support, operations and the product stay with you.

That is the use case Pancake is built for, whether you work alone or with a small team. If you run the company by yourself, see how Pancake works for [solopreneurs](/for/solopreneurs).
