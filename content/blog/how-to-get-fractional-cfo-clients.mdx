**TL;DR:** Fractional CFO work goes to whoever a CEO already trusts with money questions: their outside accountant, their lead investor, their banker, a former colleague. So the fastest clients come through those people, plus outreach timed to the moments that create the question, such as a round that closed this month, a controller job post, or a job post that mentions an ERP implementation. That is how to get fractional CFO clients this quarter. Directories and teaching pay off later.

This guide is for independent CFOs and small CFO firms selling to companies of about 10 to 200 people.

---

## Five moments that send a CEO looking for a CFO

A CEO doesn't shop for a fractional CFO. They hit a question their bookkeeper can't answer, and someone suggests one. Five moments produce most of those questions:

1. **A round closed.** The company now has a board, investor updates, an 18-month plan and a hiring budget to defend.
2. **A round is coming.** Investors will test the model, the metrics and the data room.
3. **The books outgrew the bookkeeper.** Annual contracts need revenue recognition, a second entity needs consolidation, or an investor asks for a first audit.
4. **A lender or buyer wants numbers.** Covenant reporting for a loan, or a diligence list for a sale.
5. **Cash gets tight.** Runway under a year, a missed forecast, a payroll that only clears because a customer paid early.

Each moment leaves a trace: a funding announcement, a job post, a founder's post about runway, a question put to the company's accountant. Every channel below is a way to be present when one appears.

## Pick three channels for the practice you run

Run three channels at a time, and make sure at least one reaches people who don't know you yet.

- **Newly fractional:** channels 1, 2 and 5. Your old network signs first, and an accounting firm or two fills the gaps.
- **Solo, with a few clients:** channels 2, 3 and 6. A solo CFO needs only a handful of new clients a year, and two partners plus one signal channel can supply them. [Getting clients as a solopreneur](/blog/how-to-get-clients-as-a-solopreneur) fits that work into two sessions a week.
- **A firm with several CFOs:** channels 3, 5 and 6, plus 8. A firm needs a flow that doesn't depend on the founder's calendar.

Referrals arrive in clumps: a partner who trusts you may send two clients in a month, then none for the next six. Keep one channel you control, such as outreach on signals, running underneath. The [wider consulting playbook](/blog/how-to-get-consulting-clients) covers the channels every independent shares; the rest of this page is specific to CFO work.

## How to get fractional CFO clients through the CEO's advisors (channels 2 to 4)

The people a CEO already pays, or answers to, hear the money question first. Your job is to be the name they give.

**Accounting and bookkeeping firms.** A bookkeeping or CPA firm closes the books and files the returns. Many don't build forecasts or sit in board meetings, and their clients ask them who does. Offer a clean split: you leave tax and the monthly close to them, and you send them every client who needs books. Then ask a question they can answer with a name: "Which of your clients asked about a forecast or a raise this year?"

**Investors and accelerators.** Seed and Series A investors watch portfolio companies struggle with the first board deck, the budget and the next raise. Offer something a cohort can use: open office hours, a board-deck review for two portfolio companies, a session on investor updates. Say which stage you serve, so the investor knows when to think of you.

**Bankers, venture lenders and deal lawyers.** A loan with covenants or a sale process creates a CFO need with a budget attached. These partners send fewer clients, but they send them at the moments when a CEO pays most for help.

**Before Friday:** list five accounting firms and three investors whose clients look like your best client. Write to each with one sentence on what you take on and what you leave to them.

## Companies that recently raised, or are about to (channel 5)

A priced round turns "we should get a CFO someday" into "we need a plan before the first board meeting." The money comes with a board, investor reporting and a hiring budget, and seed and Series A companies often have no one senior to own them.

Rounds appear in announcements, funding databases and SEC filings. A company that sells securities under Regulation D [must file a Form D within 15 days after the first sale](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/filing-form-d-notice), and the notices are public on EDGAR. Each one lists the company's executive officers and directors, and how much it has sold so far.

Write about what the money has to do: "Congrats on the Series A. Who is building the budget you'll take to your first board meeting?" Before a round, the trace is different: founders posting about fundraising or runway. Ask about the model instead. ## Job posts that show the books outgrew the bookkeeper (channel 6)

A controller or accounting manager opening means the company has decided its numbers need an owner. If there is no CFO, someone senior has to manage that person, and today it is the CEO.

Three kinds of post are worth a message:

- **Controller or accounting manager, at a company with no CFO.** Offer to scope the role, interview the finalists, or supervise the new hire as the part-time CFO above them.
- **Finance manager or FP&A analyst.** Someone asked for forecasts the team can't produce yet. The analyst will need a model to run and a senior person to review it.
- **Job posts naming an ERP.** A staff accountant post that asks for NetSuite, Sage Intacct or Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central tells you which system you would inherit. Words like "implementation" or "go-live" point to a project already underway.

A tool named in a job post proves the company uses it somewhere. It doesn't prove a move is planned, so ask before you pitch a migration. ## QuickBooks ProAdvisor and the Xero advisor directory (channel 7)

Software directories reach small businesses that already chose the tool, so they suit CFOs who also oversee the monthly books. As of November 2026:

- **QuickBooks.** Joining the [ProAdvisor program](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/accountants/proadvisor/) is free, with training, certifications and badges. Business owners use Find a ProAdvisor to search by location, filter by industry, services and software, and contact an advisor through a form.
- **Xero.** The [Xero partner program](https://www.xero.com/us/partner-programme/) is free, with no ongoing costs, and partners can be listed in Xero's advisor directory. To join, you need an accounting practice with at least one registered accountant, and someone on the team must commit to completing the Xero advisor certification within 30 days.

Both directories present themselves as the place to find an accountant or bookkeeper, so many searchers want help with the books or taxes. Put "fractional CFO" and the stage you serve in the first line of your profile, and list advisory services first. If you aren't an accountant, a listed bookkeeping partner can send you its advisory work instead.

## Fractional CFO marketing: do part of the job in public (channel 8)

Fractional CFO marketing works when it shows a founder how you think about their numbers. Generic lists of "KPIs every startup should track" don't. Pick one stage and one industry, then publish things a CEO can use the same afternoon:

- a 13-week cash flow template with a one-page guide;
- an investor update teardown, with three anonymized updates rewritten;
- a first-board-deck workshop for an accelerator cohort;
- short posts on the platforms founders read, each answering one question a client asked that week.

Fractional CFO business development is the follow-up, not the publishing. Send each person who uses the template one question about their numbers, and offer a defined first project rather than an open retainer: a board-ready budget, a raise-ready model and data room, or a cleanup of the close. A fixed fee and an end date make the first yes easy, and the readout is where you propose the monthly seat.
