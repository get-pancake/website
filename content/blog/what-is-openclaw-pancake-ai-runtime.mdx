OpenClaw is an AI assistant you host yourself. It runs on your laptop, a spare computer or a rented server. You message it from WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack or iMessage the way you'd message a person. It reads documents, browses the web, runs commands and sends messages for you, using whichever AI model you plug in.

The project passed 240,000 GitHub stars by March 2026. This guide covers where it came from, how it works, what it costs, where it goes wrong, and how it differs from a hosted product like Pancake.

## Where OpenClaw came from

Peter Steinberger, the Austrian developer who founded PSPDFKit, built it as a personal tool: an AI assistant on his own hardware that he could reach over WhatsApp. It first shipped in November 2025. It was best known as Clawdbot, then briefly Moltbot, before it became OpenClaw in late January 2026.

In February 2026 Steinberger joined OpenAI. The project stayed open source and moved to the OpenClaw Foundation, a nonprofit that steers development under the MIT license.

## How OpenClaw works

An OpenClaw setup has four moving parts.

**The Gateway.** A single process on your machine or server. It holds your sessions, routes messages and keeps the connections to your chat apps: WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, Signal, iMessage, Microsoft Teams, Matrix and more through plugins.

**The model.** OpenClaw ships no AI of its own. You connect a provider such as Claude, GPT, Gemini or a local model, and you can set a failover for when one is down.

**Skills.** A skill teaches the agent a repeatable procedure. You write your own or install one from ClawHub, the community marketplace.

**Memory and schedules.** Configuration and conversation history stay on your machine, so the agent can pick up past work. Cron jobs and webhooks let it act without a prompt, such as a morning summary or an hourly check.

One Gateway can also run several agents. Multi-agent routing gives each agent, workspace or sender its own session.

## What people use OpenClaw for

The uses are whatever its owner sets up. Common ones include sorting an inbox, managing a calendar, researching a topic on a schedule, filling in web forms and running scripts on a home server. Developers also use it to steer coding agents from their phone.

That range is the appeal. It also means OpenClaw does nothing useful until you decide what it should do and wire it up.

## What OpenClaw costs

The software costs nothing. Running it costs money in two places:

- **Model usage.** Every message and every scheduled job calls the model you connected, billed by your provider.
- **Hosting.** Free on a computer you already own. A small VPS or a managed OpenClaw host starts at a few dollars a month. Our [OpenClaw hosting comparison](/blog/best-openclaw-hosting-alternatives-2026) lists current plans.

The cost that doesn't show on a bill is your time: installing, updating, vetting skills and fixing things when a dependency changes.

## The risks to know

OpenClaw has deep access by design. It can run shell commands, read files and send messages as you. Each of those abilities is also a way in for an attacker.

- **Skills run code.** In early 2026, researchers found hundreds of malicious skills on ClawHub. Many posed as crypto wallets or YouTube tools and installed info-stealing malware on Macs.
- **Prompt injection.** A web page or message the agent reads can carry instructions it may follow. Limit what it can touch.
- **Exposed gateways.** A Gateway reachable from the public internet is an open door. Keep it on a private network or behind a VPN.

## Who OpenClaw is for

OpenClaw suits developers and technical operators. It fits if you want one assistant across your chat apps, full control over models and data, and you're willing to host and maintain it.

If that's you, the next choice is self-hosting or a managed host. The main open-source alternative is Hermes Agent from Nous Research, which we compare in [Pancake vs Hermes vs OpenClaw](/blog/pancake-vs-hermes-openclaw).

## OpenClaw vs Pancake

Pancake is not an OpenClaw host, and you don't install or run OpenClaw to use it. It is an AI GTM team for founders and small B2B companies, built for one job: finding people who show buying signals and running outreach to them.

You add your website. Pancake learns your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections into one shared memory, the GTM Brain. Its agents watch for signals such as people engaging with a competitor's posts or companies whose job posts name a tool you replace. Leads arrive each morning with the reason they were picked, and you approve them in the app or from Slack.

Outreach then runs from your own account on the professional network. It starts with a profile visit, a like on a recent post and an invite with no note, followed by up to three messages. The first one asks a light question about the signal and makes no pitch. Pancake also writes articles for Google and AI search, which you approve.

## When OpenClaw is the better choice

- Your use case goes well beyond sales and marketing.
- You want to inspect and change the agent system itself.
- You have engineering time for deployment and upkeep.
- Owning the stack matters more to you than reaching an outcome fast.

For a technical team building a custom agent product, that control is worth the work.

## When Pancake is the better choice

- The goal right now is finding customers.
- You want signal research, outreach and articles to draw on the same knowledge of your buyers.
- You want the work done each day, not suggested.
- You don't want to build or maintain an agent runtime.

The GTM Brain ties it together. Every agent reads from the same picture of who buys from you, and Pancake keeps learning from what works.

## The short version

OpenClaw is a free, self-hosted AI agent with a large community and real security trade-offs. It can do almost anything you configure it to do, and nothing until you do. Pancake does one thing, finding your buyers and starting the conversation, and costs $99/month flat after a 3-day free trial.
