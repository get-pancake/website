**TL;DR:** Five criteria pick a niche: deal size, how often the niche rebuilds, how easy the buyer is to find, how crowded it is, and how much its members refer each other. Scored that way, the best niche for web design agency owners selling to B2B companies is often B2B SaaS or professional services, while local studios do well with clinics, law firms and trades. Don't commit from a list: run the same outreach to two niches for 30 days and keep the one that books calls.

A niche decides who you talk to. The channels that fill your calendar once you pick one are ranked in [how to get web design clients](/blog/how-to-get-web-design-clients).

## What makes a web design niche pay

A niche pays when it scores well on five criteria. Check each one before you rewrite your homepage.

1. **Deal size.** What a site is worth to them. The site that books demos, appointments or orders gets a bigger budget than a brochure nobody visits.
2. **Rebuild cycle.** How often they redo the site. Funding rounds, launches, rebrands and new rules force rebuilds; "we redid it in 2019 and it's fine" does not.
3. **Buyer access.** Whether you can name the person who decides (a head of marketing, a practice manager, a managing partner) and see them in public.
4. **Competition.** How many studios already claim the niche. Search "[niche] web design agency" and count the specialists with case studies on page one.
5. **Referral density.** Whether members talk to each other through associations, conferences and peer groups. A niche that talks sends its next client to whoever built the last good site.

Add a sixth for yourself: an unfair advantage. Two past projects in a niche, a former career in it, or a partner who serves it beats any score.

## What to know about each niche before you pick it

Each niche has one fact that changes how you sell to it. Here is the one to check first.

- **B2B SaaS.** Sites change with every funding round, rebrand and product launch, and the head of marketing owns them. Buyers show up in job posts and funding news, which makes this niche the easiest to prospect by signal (see [how Pancake finds them](/for/web-design-agencies)). It is crowded, so narrow it: dev tools, fintech, or one platform.
- **Professional services firms.** Consulting, accounting and engineering firms with 20 to 200 staff buy credibility. Partners decide slowly, and they refer peers once you deliver.
- **Healthcare clinics.** Booking and forms carry the site, and so does privacy. HHS's [bulletin on online tracking technologies](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html) says regulated entities can't use tracking tools in a way that leads to impermissible disclosures of protected health information; a federal court vacated part of it in June 2024. Expect a clinic to ask how you handle analytics and forms.
- **Law firms.** Every claim on the site falls under advertising rules. ABA [Model Rule 7.1](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_7_1_communication_concerning_a_lawyer_s_services/) bars false or misleading communications about a lawyer's services, and the ABA says its Model Rules serve as models for the ethics rules of most jurisdictions. Plan for the firm to sign off on case results and "best lawyer" lines.
- **Construction and trades.** Buyers live on Google Maps and in reviews, and suppliers refer them. They rarely post jobs for web roles, so this is a phone-and-Maps niche.
- **E-commerce brands.** The site is the store, so the budget follows revenue. Replatforms and theme rebuilds come often, and Shopify specialists are many.
- **Nonprofits.** Google's [Ad Grants](https://www.google.com/nonprofits/offerings/google-ad-grants/) gives eligible nonprofits $10,000 a month of in-kind text ads, and its [website policy](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1657899?hl=en) requires the whole site to run over HTTPS, carry a significant amount of original content and have working links. "A site that qualifies for Ad Grants" is a concrete offer. Budgets are tight and boards decide.
- **Manufacturers.** Many run dated sites, budgets are real, and few studios specialize in them. The buyer is a marketing manager or a VP of sales, and trade shows set the calendar.

## Platform niches: Webflow, Framer, Shopify, WordPress

A platform can be a niche on its own, and it stacks with an industry: "Webflow sites for B2B SaaS" says more than either half.

- **Webflow** suits marketing teams that want to publish pages without engineers. Channels specific to it are in [how to get Webflow clients](/blog/how-to-get-webflow-clients).
- **Framer** draws startups and designers who want to own their site. See [how to get Framer clients](/blog/how-to-get-framer-clients).
- **Shopify** ties the site to sales, which keeps budgets tied to revenue.
- **WordPress** is the widest market, from a one-truck plumber to an enterprise newsroom, and the natural home of care plans.

The strongest platform signal is a job post that names the platform: a company asking for "Webflow experience" has already chosen. Job posts are where the [tools a company uses](/plays/tech-stack) show up.

## How to choose a web design niche in one afternoon

Score your own history first, then the market. Two hours and a spreadsheet are enough.

1. **List your last ten projects.** Industry, fee, how they found you, and whether you would take the same client again.
2. **Circle two or three industries** that repeat, paid best, or that you enjoyed.
3. **Count the market.** The Census Bureau's [County Business Patterns](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp.html) gives the number of establishments by industry for counties, metro areas and ZIP codes, and breaks them out by employee count; the latest data year is 2023. Look up dentist offices (NAICS 621210) in your metro, for example, and you know how big a local dental niche can get.
4. **Check the competition.** Search "[niche] web design agency" and "[niche] website design" and count specialists with real case studies.
5. **Check the talk.** Find the associations, conferences and peer groups. No community means no referrals.
6. **Pick two to test.** Not one: you need a comparison.

## How to test a niche in 30 days before you commit

Run the same outreach to two niches for four weeks and compare calls, not opinions.

- **Week 1: lists and offers.** Build a list of 40 businesses per niche, each with a reason to write now: a job post, a launch, a new location. Write one offer and one first message per niche, in that niche's words.
- **Week 2: first messages.** Send all 80, spread across the week. The [web design cold outreach templates](/blog/web-design-cold-outreach) give you a starting point for each reason.
- **Week 3: follow-ups and insiders.** Send follow-ups, and talk to two people who serve each niche, such as a dental supply rep or a SaaS marketing consultant. Ask what their clients complain about.
- **Week 4: count and decide.** Replies, calls and proposals per niche. More calls wins; on a tie, pick the higher deal size.

Say B2B SaaS gets 7 replies and 3 calls from 40 messages, and dental groups get 4 replies and 1 call (made-up numbers). Pick SaaS, rewrite your homepage headline for it, publish one case study, and keep the dental list for later. If neither niche books a call, the offer is the problem, not the niche.
