In July 2026, Victora called herself "the world's first AI co-founder." She learned your business from a brief and ran 46 capabilities across strategy, legal, marketing, operations, and investor relations.

By September 2026, Victora's homepage sells something narrower: a leads list. It learns your business, reads the places your buyers talk, and hands you real people asking for what you sell.

That puts Victora much closer to [Pancake](/), an AI GTM team whose agents find people showing buying signals and run your outreach. The two now chase the same outcome, new customers, from different sources.

**TL;DR:** Victora finds public conversations on Reddit and Hacker News where someone describes a problem you solve, and drafts a first reply for each. You copy the message and reach out yourself. Pancake watches six buying signals, delivers leads each morning, and runs outreach from your own account on the professional social network once you approve a lead. It also writes articles for Google and AI search. Victora costs $49 a month; Pancake costs $99.

---

## What Victora does now

Victora (victora.ai) works in four steps, in a fixed order:

- **Business context.** It learns what you sell, who you serve, how you speak and what makes you different.
- **Customer context.** From that, it works out who buys, what they struggle with, and the words they use to ask for help.
- **The read.** It searches the places your customers gather. Today that means Reddit and Hacker News, with more sources promised.
- **Your leads list.** It returns real people and live conversations, each with a link, a date and the reason it matches.

Every lead comes with a first message written in your voice. Victora also shows its work: the phrases it searched, why each person matched, and every lead it rejected, with the reason.

The list refills as new threads appear. Victora costs $49 a month, and you can start free with no credit card. It is aimed at founders, consultants and coaches, and small businesses.

## What Victora used to be

The earlier Victora, the one some "AI co-founder" lists still describe, was built around a Business Playbook. You briefed her on your company, and she generated a living document set: strategy, brand positioning, legal groundwork, financial baseline, competitive research. When your business changed, the documents updated.

Beyond the playbook, Victora ran 46 capabilities on request:

- Strategy and market research
- Legal groundwork and compliance prep
- Marketing collateral and campaigns
- Investor pitch decks
- Competitive intelligence

The interaction model was reactive: you asked, she delivered documents, reports and content. It cost $49 a month plus tokens.

As of September 2026, Victora's About page still describes that back-office product, built by Rob Gaudet in New Orleans. The homepage has moved on to the leads list. If you landed here comparing Pancake with the document-first Victora, the short version is this: that Victora produced documents when you asked, and Pancake produces leads and outreach.

## What Pancake does

Pancake is an AI GTM team for founders and small B2B companies. Its agents do the prospecting and the first outreach, so you approve leads instead of working a list by hand.

Setup starts with your website. Pancake learns your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections into one shared memory, the GTM Brain, and updates it with every result.

The agents track six kinds of buying signals:

- **Keyword:** people posting about a topic you choose, like "ticket backlog".
- **Competitor:** people engaging with a rival's posts.
- **Influencer:** people engaging with posts from a voice your buyers follow.
- **Own brand:** people engaging with your own posts.
- **Hiring:** companies with open roles that match your buyer.
- **Stack:** companies whose job posts name a tool, such as one you replace.

New leads arrive each morning, each with the signal that picked it. You approve them in the app or from Slack.

Then Pancake runs the outreach from your own account on the professional social network: a profile visit, a like on a recent post, an invite with no note, and up to three messages. The first message pitches nothing. It asks one light question tied to the signal, the kind a person answers without feeling sold to.

Pancake also writes articles built to show up in Google and AI answers, and you approve those too. It costs [$99 a month flat](/pricing), every agent included.

## Where the leads come from, and who reaches out

Victora reads public threads. A lead is a person who wrote, in their own words, that they have the problem you solve. That context is valuable, and you can quote it back in your reply.

Pancake reads activity on the professional social network and in job posts. A lead is someone who posted about your topic or engaged with a competitor, an influencer or your own posts. It can also be a company whose open roles match your buyer or name a tool you replace.

The bigger split is what happens next. Victora hands you the list and a drafted message, and you do the outreach. Pancake runs the outreach itself, from your account, once you approve the lead.

Which one fits depends on where your buyers talk and how much of the outreach you want to do by hand.

## Who each tool is for

| Situation | Better fit |
|-----------|-----------|
| Your buyers ask for help on Reddit or Hacker News | Victora |
| Your buyers are professionals you'd reach on the professional social network | Pancake |
| You want to write and send every message yourself | Victora |
| You want outreach to run from your account after you approve a lead | Pancake |
| You want to start free, with no card | Victora |
| You also want articles for Google and AI search | Pancake |
| You want leads from hiring or tech-stack signals | Pancake |

Both tools start from the same premise: learn the business first, then look for buyers. They part ways on the source of each lead and on who sends the first message.

## Can you use both?

Yes. They read different sources, so each can surface people the other misses.

Use Victora to catch people asking for help on Reddit and Hacker News, where a thoughtful reply can turn into a customer.

Use Pancake for the professional side: people engaging with competitors and influencers, companies hiring for your buyer's role, and outreach that runs from your own account. Its articles work a third channel: buyers who search before they talk to anyone.
