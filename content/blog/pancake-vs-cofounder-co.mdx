**Short answer:** Cofounder.co and Pancake both put AI agents to work, but on different jobs. Cofounder.co is an agent orchestration platform built to run the business around your product. It covers strategy, brand, website, sales, marketing, support, operations and finance, with a roadmap whose first steps include picking a company name.

[Pancake](/) has a narrower brief. It's an AI GTM team for founders and small B2B companies. Its agents find people who show buying signals, run outreach for you, and write articles built to show up in Google and AI answers.

So the choice comes down to your gap. Pick Cofounder.co if you want one workspace to build out a new company. Pick Pancake if your product works and the missing piece is customers.

Everything below about Cofounder.co comes from its homepage, pricing page and docs as of September 2026. Both products change fast, so check the live pages before you buy.

One naming note. Cofounder.co is a different company from CoFounder.AI (cofounder.ai) and from the other "cofounder" tools on the market. This page covers cofounder.co.

## What Cofounder.co is

Cofounder.co is made by The General Intelligence Company of New York. The homepage headline reads "Cofounder lets you run an entire company with AI." The pitch is to start with an AI roadmap, then hand engineering, sales, marketing, design, finance and ops to agents.

The current version is Cofounder 2. In its launch post, CEO Andrew Pignanelli calls it a "superoptimizer," meaning a manager agent that delegates work to other agents.

### Departments and agents

The app is organized like a company. After onboarding, your workspace comes seeded with agents for the main operating areas: Operations, Engineer, Marketing, Design, Sales, Support, Ops and Finance. A top-level Cofounder agent sits above them and routes tasks to the right one. You can add custom agents, each with its own instructions, integrations and skills.

Work happens in a workspace view called Canvas. You describe an outcome, pick an agent, and track the task from there. Several tasks can run in the background at once.

### The roadmap

The roadmap gives a new company a structured path instead of a blank page. It breaks company building into stages: idea, initial setup, identity, build, GTM, launch and scale. Each stage has steps, such as picking a company name, buying a domain, setting up a social presence and opening a bank account. The "Incorporate LLC" step runs as a guided agent flow with Ramp.

Cofounder.co also publishes a four-part founder guide: Start, Build, Sell and Scale.

### Managed services and integrations

Cofounder.co can set up GitHub, Vercel and Supabase projects for you and manage them. The pricing FAQ says Pro and Team users can "graduate" at any point and claim ownership of those projects. The trade-off: Cofounder.co can't manage their configuration after that.

The native integration list is long. It includes Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack, Notion, Linear, Stripe, Attio, PostHog and Google Search Console, plus more apps through MCP. You can also import company context from ChatGPT or Claude into its Company Memory.

The homepage says over 10,650 companies run on Cofounder. That's the company's own figure.

### What Cofounder.co doesn't try to do

The docs are direct about one boundary. Cofounder.co isn't meant to build or rebuild your core product. Your main app, database, authentication and hosting stay in your own codebase and coding tools. The Engineer agent handles work around the product: landing pages, marketing sites, internal workflows, and handoff specs for whoever builds the product.

That framing tells you who it's for. The pricing page names early-stage software companies, solo founders and small teams that want to validate, build, launch and operate before hiring a full team.

## How Cofounder.co handles sales and outreach

Sales is where the two products meet, so it's worth a close look.

Cofounder.co's Sales Agent owns ICP work, target accounts, customer development, outbound, pipeline and follow-up. You ask for an outcome, for example "gather qualified prospects for our ICP," and it works from whatever context you've connected: product, CRM, email and calendar. It keeps records of accounts, contacts and opportunities as it goes. Research integrations include People Data Labs and PhantomBuster.

Outbound is built around email. Agents get their own inboxes on your domain through Agentmail. If you bought the domain through Cofounder.co, you can choose to warm a new inbox before it sends. Warmup lasts 21 days at minimum, 28 days are recommended, and outreach stays locked until the domain clears the minimum.

External actions are review-gated. You approve before the Sales Agent sends cold outreach, changes a calendar event, or marks a deal closed. The homepage puts it as "nothing ships without your approval."

## What Pancake is, and what it isn't

Pancake is an AI GTM team. It isn't an AI co-founder tool, and it doesn't try to run the whole company.

Setup starts with your website. Pancake reads it and learns who buys from you: your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections. All of it goes into one shared memory called the GTM Brain, which keeps learning from what works.

From there, the agents do three jobs.

**They find buyers.** Pancake watches six kinds of buying signals:

1. People posting about a keyword you choose, like "ticket backlog."
2. People engaging with a competitor's posts.
3. People engaging with posts from a voice your buyers follow.
4. People engaging with your own posts.
5. Companies hiring for roles that match your buyer.
6. Companies whose job posts name a tool, for example one you replace.

**They run outreach.** New leads arrive each morning, and each one says which signal picked it. You approve leads in the app, or from Slack, where Pancake posts every new lead. Outreach then runs from your own account on the professional social network. The sequence is a profile visit, a like on a recent post, an invite with no note, then up to three messages. The first message makes no pitch. It asks one light question tied to the signal.

**They write articles.** Pancake writes articles built for Google and AI search. You approve them.

Its limits are clear too. Pancake sends no email and makes no calls. It doesn't build websites, form companies, or touch engineering, finance or support. It doesn't sync to a CRM or export CSVs either. If you need those things, Cofounder.co or another tool will cover them better.

## Where they overlap: finding customers

Both products promise to find prospects and contact them. They go about it in opposite ways.

**Where prospects come from.** Cofounder.co's Sales Agent starts from a profile. You describe the ICP, and it researches and enriches matching accounts and contacts. Pancake starts from behavior. A person enters your pipeline because of something they or their company did: posted about the problem you solve, engaged with a rival's post, or opened a role that matches your buyer. The reason travels with the lead, so your opener has something real to point at.

**Which channel.** Cofounder.co sends from new agent inboxes. That means buying or connecting a domain, then provisioning inboxes. If you use Cofounder.co's warmup, it also means at least three weeks before outreach unlocks. Pancake sends from the account you already use on the professional social network, so there's no inbox to warm.

**What you approve.** Cofounder.co asks for review before each cold send. Pancake puts approval on the lead. You choose who enters outreach, and the sequence runs from there. Pancake doesn't offer message-by-message approval. If reading every message before it goes out matters to you, Cofounder.co's model fits better.

**What the first message sounds like.** Cofounder.co's agent drafts messages from your request and your company context, so you set the angle each time. With Pancake the angle comes from the signal. Someone who engaged with a rival's post gets a different opener than someone whose company is hiring.

Neither approach wins everywhere. Email reaches people who aren't active on social networks, and a researched list works well when your market is small and easy to name. Signals work well when your buyers talk about their problems in public and timing matters.

## Content, SEO and marketing

Cofounder.co goes wide here. Its Marketing Agent handles campaigns, positioning, launches and creative assets such as decks, images and short videos. The Pro plan includes SEO analysis and social post publishing. The Engineer can build landing pages and marketing sites, and the Design Agent covers brand systems and UI kits.

Pancake's content work stops at articles. They draw on the positioning, proof and objections in the GTM Brain, so they speak to the same buyers your outreach reaches. Brand design and websites are outside its scope.

If you need a launch kit, Cofounder.co does far more. If you need search articles aimed at the buyers you're already contacting, Pancake covers that part.

| Plan | Price | What you get |

A few details matter when you compare the two.

- Cofounder.co lists Pro as "starting at" $120, and its pricing FAQ describes the model as usage-based. Statutory filing fees and external spend are separate.
- Cofounder.co doesn't let you bring your own API key, Codex subscription or Claude Code subscription.
- Cofounder.co compares its price with an "$11,000+/month" stack of agencies, contractors and software. The page labels that a directional estimate.
- Pancake's price doesn't change with how much you use it. You can cancel anytime, and access runs to the end of the billing period. Details are on the [pricing page](/pricing).

The headline numbers sit close together, but they buy different work. Compare each one against the job you need done.

## Control and ownership

Both are hosted apps. You work inside Cofounder.co's workspace, and you work inside Pancake's app. The useful differences are in what stays yours.

With Cofounder.co on a paid plan, the managed GitHub, Vercel and Supabase projects can be claimed at any time. Company knowledge sits in its Company Memory, and the app connects to the tools your team already uses. Approval gates cover risky actions such as cold sends, calendar changes and closing deals.

With Pancake, outreach goes out under your own name from your own account. The connections and conversations it starts live on your profile. The GTM Brain lives in Pancake, and since there's no CRM sync, plan where you'll log deals once a conversation turns into one. Approvals sit on leads and articles. You stay in control of who gets contacted and what gets published.

## Choose Cofounder.co if…

- You're before launch and still need a name, a brand, a website, a domain and an LLC.
- You want one workspace with agents for sales, marketing, support, operations and finance.
- Your outbound plan is cold email, and you're willing to set up sending domains and warm new inboxes.
- You want to approve each outreach message before it sends.
- You want managed GitHub, Vercel and Supabase for the sites and tools around your product.

## Choose Pancake if…

- You already have a product and a website, and setup work is behind you.
- You sell B2B, and your buyers are active on the professional social network.
- You'd rather reach people who showed a buying signal than work from a list built on a profile.
- You want outreach to come from your own account, with no domains or inboxes to warm.
- You want one flat monthly price with every agent included.

## The bottom line

Cofounder.co is wide. It wants to be the operating system for the whole business, from naming the company to incorporating it to answering support tickets. For a founder starting from zero, that breadth is the point.

Pancake is narrow. It covers GTM only: the leads, the outreach from your account, and the articles.

Decide which problem you have this quarter. Then pick the tool built for that problem.
