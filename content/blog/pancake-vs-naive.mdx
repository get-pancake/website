Naïve launched as "Autonomous Company Infrastructure." Its pitch: give your agents what they need to run a business, from formation to payout. One platform covers company formation, identity, virtual cards, inboxes and phone numbers.

It's a strong product for builders. It also solves a different problem from [Pancake](/). Naïve hands developers the parts to build an agent-run company. Pancake is a finished AI GTM team for a company that already sells something: it finds buyers and runs outreach to them.

## What "Autonomous Company Infrastructure" means

Naïve defines infrastructure at the primitive level: the individual capabilities an agent needs to interact with the real world. Form a company. Issue a card. Receive emails. Make calls. These are the atoms of autonomous operation, building blocks for developers constructing businesses where agents are the primary workers.

Pancake sits at the top of the stack, where the work turns into customers. There are no primitives to compose. Its agents watch for buying signals, bring you leads each morning, start conversations with the ones you approve and publish articles for search.

Both are real. They sit at different layers of the stack.

## What Naïve does

Naïve is an API and CLI platform. It gives agents 100+ primitives for real-world interaction:

- **Company formation:** incorporate an LLC with hosted KYC, automated EIN filing and registered agent handling
- **Financial primitives:** virtual cards with spend controls, one credit balance across all tools
- **Identity and communication:** each agent gets its own email, phone number, domains and third-party accounts to act under
- **Research primitives:** web search, URL extraction, multi-source deep research
- **Orchestration and governance:** agents, delegation, budgets and tool policy declared in one committed file
- **Templates:** eight prebuilt companies, such as an AI media channel, an SEO/GEO agency, a newsletter and a recruiting agency

You can drive it from a terminal, from coding agents such as Claude Code, Cursor or Codex, or from Naïve Studio. Studio is the browser layer: pick a template, deploy a company and watch it run without touching a terminal. It lowers the floor for building agent-run companies.

The target user is a developer or technical founder building an agent-native business, one designed from the start to run on AI workers rather than human employees.

**Pricing (as of September 2026):** the Pro plan costs $20/month per organization with $20 of usage credit included. Model inference, compute and web tools are metered past that. A custom plan covers committed volume and procurement.

## What Pancake does

Pancake finds your buyers and starts the conversations, with no agents for you to build. It is an AI GTM team for founders and small B2B companies. Setup starts with your website. Pancake learns your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections, and keeps them in a shared memory called the GTM Brain, which gets sharper with every result.

Its agents watch six kinds of buying signals. People posting about a topic you pick. People engaging with a competitor's posts, with a voice your buyers follow, or with your own posts. Companies hiring for roles that match your buyer. Companies whose job posts name a tool you replace. New leads arrive each morning with the signal that picked them, and you approve them in the app or from Slack.

Approved leads get outreach from your own account on the professional social network: a profile visit, a like on a recent post, an invite with no note, then up to three messages. The first message makes no pitch. It asks about what got the lead picked, such as a post they wrote or a role their company is hiring for. Pancake also writes articles built to show up in Google and AI answers, which you approve before they go out.

Developers who live in Claude, ChatGPT or Codex can drive Pancake from there too. Its MCP server lets your assistant read the GTM Brain, review leads, adjust signals and start outreach in the same conversation, with a browser sign-in and no API key.

Pancake is built for go-to-market, so company formation, cards and phone numbers stay on Naïve's side. The price is [$99/month flat](/pricing), every agent included, with nothing metered.

## The real difference: parts vs a finished job

The cleanest way to separate Pancake and Naïve is the question each one answers.

**Naïve asks:** what does an agent need to operate in the real world?

**Pancake asks:** who should this company talk to this week, and why?

If you're building a new kind of business, agent-native from day one with AI workers doing the work and humans optional, Naïve gives you the foundation. You still build the agents. You still design the workflows. Naïve handles the real-world interface: the legal entity, the card, the email address, the phone number.

If you already have a product and customers, the hard part is finding the next ones. Pancake handles that loop without any agent building. You add your website, approve the leads worth reaching, and the outreach runs.

## When to use Naïve

Naïve makes sense if:

- You're building a new type of business designed from the start to run on AI agents
- You're a developer who wants primitive-level control over how your agents interact with the world
- You need to give agents real-world capabilities (bank cards, phone numbers, company formation) as part of your product
- You want to build custom agent workflows and need infrastructure rather than a finished product

Naïve's strength is flexibility and depth at the infrastructure layer. If you're building an agent-native product or an agent-run business from scratch, those primitives give you a real head start.

## When to use Pancake

Pancake makes sense if:

- You sell to other businesses and already have customers, or at least a clear picture of your buyer
- Your bottleneck is pipeline: too few conversations with the right people
- You want to see why each lead was picked before anyone gets contacted
- You want it working this week, without writing agent code
- You want to run prospecting from Claude, ChatGPT or Codex instead of another dashboard

## The deeper point

"Autonomous company infrastructure" is a category, not a single product. Naïve is building the picks-and-shovels layer: the raw materials agents need to act in the world. It's flexible, technical and built for people who write code.

Pancake plays in a different category. It is a finished application that gets a B2B company in front of its buyers and keeps the plumbing out of sight.

What you need depends on the question in front of you. If it's "how do I give my agents a card and a phone number," start with Naïve. If it's "who should I be talking to this week," start with Pancake.
