**TL;DR:** Founders hire a fractional CMO at three moments: right after a round, while a Head of Marketing job post sits open, and when they start saying in public that marketing isn't working. Reach them through people who have seen you lead marketing, investors and other fractional executives who refer, and direct messages to founders showing one of those moments. A solo practice can run every channel below, because you need a handful of clients at a time, not a funnel.

This is how to get clients as a fractional CMO when you sell to founders of B2B companies with roughly 10 to 150 people: the capacity math that sets how much selling you need, nine channels in one table, and the offers that win against a full-time hire.

---

## Fractional CMO business development starts with capacity math

Your best engagements end on purpose: the company hires a full-time CMO, or the plan you built runs without you. So a fractional practice always needs the next client, even when it's full.

Say each client buys one day a week, you hold four of them, and engagements last about nine months (example numbers; use your own). Then one client leaves roughly every ten weeks. If one first call in four becomes a client, you need about two first calls a month, every month, to keep the week full.

That changes how you sell. You don't need volume. You need a few conversations a month with founders who fit, started before a client leaves rather than after.

## What a founder is buying when they hire you

A founder who hires a fractional CMO is buying judgment and ownership: someone who decides what marketing does, hires the people or agencies who do it, and reports on it. The execution often comes from a team, agencies or freelancers.

Pick the founders you serve best and say so. A few lanes that each run on their own referrals:

- **B2B software startups after a seed or Series A round,** where the founder has sold the first customers alone and needs repeatable demand.
- **Founder-led service firms of 20 to 150 people,** where referrals dried up and nobody owns marketing.
- **Companies preparing a raise or a sale,** where the story and the numbers have to hold up in a data room.

A narrow lane makes every other channel work better. Investors refer the person who "does positioning for vertical SaaS," not the person who does marketing. [Pancake for fractional CMOs](/for/fractional-cmos) shows the founder signals each lane tends to give off.

## How to get clients as a fractional CMO: 9 channels compared

The order reflects how soon each channel tends to produce a first conversation with a founder who fits, then the effort it takes. It's our judgment of how each channel behaves, not survey data.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Founders and executives who've seen your work | Days | Free | Anyone with a track record | Waiting for them to remember you |
| 2 | Investors and accelerators | 1 to 3 months | Mentoring time | B2B startup specialists | Advice that never becomes a contract |
| 3 | Other fractional executives | Weeks | Referrals both ways | Every practice | Relationships that only flow one way |
| 4 | Founders showing a signal | 1 to 4 weeks | Your time, or a tool | Practices selling to B2B founders | Pitching before asking |
| 5 | Agencies that need a strategist | Weeks | Referral swaps | CMOs who don't execute | Agencies that want you to sell their retainer |

## Referral channels: people who've seen you lead (1 to 3)

**1. Founders and executives who've worked with you.** Former CEOs, peers from past leadership teams and board members have watched you make marketing decisions. Once a quarter, send the five who know your work best something useful for their stage: a teardown of a competitor's launch, a hire you'd make, a channel you'd cut. The ask comes later, and it's specific: "Who do you know who's running marketing themselves and hating it?"

**2. Investors and accelerators.** Investors hear about marketing problems in board meetings, and they want a name to give. Mentor at an accelerator, offer portfolio office hours on positioning, and tell the partners you know which lane you serve. Track which investors' founders become clients, and spend your time there.

**3. Other fractional executives.** A fractional CFO building a board deck often hears "our CAC is a mess" first. Fractional COOs, heads of people and CTOs sit in the same founders' calendars. Build a small circle of three or four, send work both ways, and meet each quarter to compare who needs what. [How fractional CFOs find their clients](/blog/how-to-get-fractional-cfo-clients) shows the same founders from the other chair.

## Founders showing a signal (channel 4)

Outreach works when it names a moment the founder chose to make public. Three signals matter most for a fractional CMO:

- **A Head of Marketing or first marketer job post.** The founder has decided marketing needs a leader and has budget for one. Startup job boards make this easy to check by hand: [Y Combinator's job board for marketing roles](https://www.ycombinator.com/jobs/role/marketing) listed openings such as "Founding Marketing Lead" and "Head of Marketing & Growth" in November 2026. [Reading job posts as buying signals](/blog/job-postings-as-buying-signals) has the timing and the roles to watch.
- **A recent funding round.** New money arrives with growth targets and, sooner or later, a marketing plan someone has to own. [How to find startups that raised money](/blog/how-to-find-recently-funded-startups) covers the sources and the timing.
- **Founders posting about marketing that isn't working.** "We tried two agencies." "What should our first marketing hire be?" "Positioning is our biggest problem." Each post names the job.

Write to the founder, not the applicant pipeline, and ask before you pitch: "Saw the Head of Marketing opening. What should that person own in the first 90 days?" Their answer tells you whether to offer the bridge, the diagnostic or nothing at all.

## Agencies and job boards (5 and 6)

**5. Agencies that need a strategist above the work.** Agencies keep meeting clients with budget and no marketing leader to brief them. A fractional CMO fixes that, and agencies with good clients will refer you if you send execution work back. Pick two or three agencies whose work you'd stake your name on. The [marketing agency client playbook](/blog/how-to-get-clients-for-marketing-agency) shows how they find their own clients. Skip any agency that expects you to sell its retainer to every client you bring.

**6. Fractional job boards.** Some companies post part-time leadership roles in the open. [Fractional Jobs](https://www.fractionaljobs.io/), a job board for fractional work, listed marketing and growth roles with weekly hours, such as a 20-hour growth lead, in November 2026. Answer with a short plan for the first 30 days, not a resume. Expect competition and rate comparisons, so treat the board as a way to fill a gap in the calendar, not a whole pipeline.

---

## Slow channels that compound (7 to 9)

**7. Posts under your own name.** Write for founders, not for other marketers: a positioning teardown of a company in your lane, what you'd stop doing in a founder's first marketing year, the hire you'd make before an agency. Founders who engage with those posts have told you what they're thinking about.

**8. Workshops for founder groups.** A 90-minute positioning session for an accelerator cohort or a founder community puts you in a room of companies at the stage you serve. Book follow-up calls before you leave the room.

**9. Clients who graduate.** When a client hires a full-time CMO, help write the role, interview the finalists and hand over the plan. That founder now refers you with conviction, and when they start their next company, you're the first call.

## Offers that beat a full-time hire

You compete with the job post more often than with other fractional CMOs. Three offers handle that:

- **A 30-day diagnostic.** A fixed fee for customer interviews, a channel review and a 90-day plan, ending in a decision about what comes next.
- **The bridge.** Part-time leadership until the full-time hire starts. You write the role, help pick the candidate and hand over a working plan, so the founder avoids both the empty seat and the rushed hire.
- **The standing seat.** A set number of days a week for a company that isn't ready for a full-time leader, reviewed each quarter.

Working alone is not a handicap in any of these. Founders hire the person, and a solo practice backed by a few agencies and freelancers you trust covers the execution. If you work alone, [two 90-minute selling sessions a week](/blog/how-to-get-clients-as-a-solopreneur) are enough to keep channels 1 to 4 running.
