People compare Hermes, OpenClaw and Pancake as if they were three versions of the same tool. Two of them are close cousins. The third does a different job.

Hermes Agent and OpenClaw are open-source AI agents. You host them, connect a model and teach them whatever you want done. Pancake is a hosted AI GTM team aimed at one outcome: customers. Its agents spot B2B buyers through buying signals and open the conversation for you.

Here is what each one does, where they overlap, and how to pick.

---

## What Hermes Agent does

Hermes Agent is an open-source agent from Nous Research, released under the MIT license in February 2026. You install it on your laptop, in Docker, on a small VPS or on a serverless host. Then you talk to it from the terminal or from 20+ messaging platforms, including Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp, Signal and email.

Its selling point is a learning loop. After a complex task, Hermes writes a reusable skill file and stores what happened in memory. Next time, it searches its past conversations before it starts. It works with the model you choose: Nous Portal, OpenRouter, OpenAI or your own endpoint. A built-in cron runs tasks on a schedule, and subagents take on work in parallel.

That makes Hermes a strong pick for a technical person who wants one agent that gets better at their own routine. It is still a system you operate. Hosting, API keys, model bills and permissions are yours to manage.

---

## What OpenClaw does

OpenClaw is the open-source personal assistant created by Peter Steinberger. The nonprofit OpenClaw Foundation now develops it under the MIT license. Its core is the Gateway: one process on your own machine or server that connects an AI agent to the chat apps you already use. The list runs from WhatsApp, Telegram and Slack to iMessage, Signal and Microsoft Teams.

You choose the model provider and set a failover. You teach the agent procedures through skills and install more from the ClawHub marketplace. Cron jobs and webhooks schedule the work. Multi-agent routing keeps sessions apart per agent, workspace or sender.

OpenClaw is general purpose by design. It can read your documents, open web pages and send messages on your behalf. What it does on a given day depends on how you set it up.

---

## Hermes vs OpenClaw: alternatives, not layers

The two projects overlap far more than they stack. Both are self-hosted and model-agnostic. Both keep memory, learn skills, run scheduled jobs and reach you through a messaging gateway. Hermes even ships `hermes claw migrate`, a command that imports an existing OpenClaw setup into Hermes.

So you pick one of the two. OpenClaw fits if you want the larger community and the ClawHub skills marketplace. Hermes fits if you want the agent to write and refine its own skills as it works.

## Where Pancake fits

[Pancake](/) is an AI GTM team built for founders and small B2B companies. Its agents find your buyers and start the conversations, and it arrives ready to work: no server to host, no model to pick, no skill to write.

Setup is your website. Pancake studies it for your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections, and files all of it in one shared memory, the GTM Brain, which keeps learning from what works.

Its agents watch six kinds of buying signals:

- people posting about a keyword you choose, such as "ticket backlog";
- people engaging with a competitor's posts;
- people engaging with posts from a voice your buyers follow;
- people engaging with your own posts;
- companies hiring for roles that match your buyer;
- companies whose job posts name a tool you replace.

New leads arrive each morning, each with the signal that picked it. You approve them in the app or from Slack. Outreach then runs from your own account on the professional network: a profile visit, a like on a recent post, an invite with no note, and up to three messages. The first message makes no pitch. It asks one light question tied to the signal.

The same agents write articles aimed at Google and AI answers, and none goes live without your approval.

If you already live in an AI assistant, keep it. Pancake runs a hosted MCP server, so the Claude, ChatGPT or Codex window you already have open can query the GTM Brain, go through the morning's leads, retune a signal or launch outreach without a context switch. You sign in through the browser, with no API key to manage. The price is [$99/month flat](/pricing) with every agent included, no seats and no model bill, after a 3-day free trial.

## Which one you need

Choose by the job, not by the layer.

- **Hermes Agent** if you're technical and want one general agent that improves at your own routine.
- **OpenClaw** if you want a personal assistant you can extend with community-built skills, and you're happy to host it.
- **Pancake** if the job is finding B2B customers and you'd rather not build that pipeline yourself.

Pancake and the other two rarely compete for the same slot. A self-hosted agent can sort your inbox and calendar while Pancake works on pipeline. Neither depends on the other.

---

## When to build on Hermes or OpenClaw

If you're building something custom, start from an open-source agent. A vertical AI product, a domain-specific agent or an internal tool for a workflow no product covers all need that foundation.

You get full control, and you build exactly what you need. You also build what makes agents useful rather than merely capable: memory design, workflow definitions, approval rules, testing and upkeep.

That trade-off makes sense when the agent system is your competitive advantage, or when your workflow is so specific that no product fits.

Could you rebuild Pancake's job this way? Partly. Both frameworks can run scheduled research and draft messages. You would still own the signal sources, the lead rules, the outreach steps and the fixes when something upstream changes. For a founder whose product isn't AI, those hours come out of selling. Our [OpenClaw hosting comparison](/blog/best-openclaw-hosting-alternatives-2026) shows what the self-hosted route costs before you write a single skill.

## The real question: which job are you filling?

Some founders enjoy building agents. Some have workflows so unusual that custom is the only option. For them, Hermes Agent or OpenClaw is the right starting point, and the choice between the two comes down to channels, skills and taste.

If the job on your list is "find people who might buy and start the conversation," an agent framework is a long way round. Pancake is built for exactly that job and does it every morning.
