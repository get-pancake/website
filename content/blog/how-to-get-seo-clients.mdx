**TL;DR:** SEO retainers start when search becomes someone's problem this quarter: a site migration, a drop after a Google update, a first SEO hire, a launch in a new market. Win clients by being there at that moment. Past clients and web studios see it first, outreach works when it names the moment, and your own rankings, case studies and directory reviews supply the proof that closes the deal.

That is how to get SEO clients without cold calling or mass audit emails. Below are ten channels ranked for agencies and consultants, a table to compare them, a plan for signing someone this month, and what to do once a prospect replies. The signals themselves have their own guide: [how to find businesses that need SEO](/blog/how-to-find-businesses-that-need-seo).

## Who buys SEO, and what makes them start looking

SEO agencies sell to two buyers who look for help in opposite ways.

**Local businesses.** A dental group, a law firm or a roofing company wants calls from Google Maps and the local results. The owner hires after a referral or a search, and often picks someone nearby. They rarely post about rankings or hire for SEO, so there is little public trace to watch. Referrals, local partners and your own Business Profile win here.

**Companies with a marketing team.** A B2B software company, an online store or a marketplace hires an agency when a project forces the question: a redesign, a platform move, a new country, a drop after a core update, or a first SEO hire who needs backup. Those moments leave traces in job posts and public posts, so you can reach the head of marketing before they ask anyone for proposals.

Both start from distrust. Google's guide for [site owners thinking about hiring an SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo) tells them to be wary of SEO firms that email out of the blue and says no one can guarantee a #1 ranking. It also hands them interview questions: examples of past work, whether you follow Google Search Essentials, what results to expect and when, and how you measure success. Read it before your next sales call. Your buyer may have it open.

## How to get SEO clients without cold calling: channels 1 to 4

None of these four needs a phone. Each starts from a person who already trusts you, or from a moment when search turned urgent.

### 1. Past clients and lapsed retainers

Your cheapest new client is an old one. SEO retainers end for ordinary reasons: a budget cut, an in-house hire, a new head of marketing. The site keeps changing after you leave, and the problems you used to catch come back.

Keep a list of every past client with the month they left and why. When something happens to their site, write: a redesign goes live, the domain changes, traffic falls after an update. With current clients, ask for a referral at the first result they can see in their own numbers, such as a recovered page or the first month organic leads beat paid ones.

**Monday task:** pick the three former clients who left over budget. Crawl each site the way you did when they paid you. If a template change has dropped title tags or canonicals since then, tell them in two sentences and ask whether anyone owns it now.

### 2. Web studios and other agencies that touch the site

Web design studios meet your buyer first. Every redesign and platform move needs a redirect map, and Google's guide to [moving a site](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes) tells owners to expect temporary ranking fluctuations and, for a medium-sized site, a few weeks or more before the new URLs replace the old ones. A studio would rather hand that risk to a specialist than own it.

Other partners see the same moments. PPC agencies have clients asking why they pay for every click. Branding studios run renames that mean a new domain. PR firms earn coverage that brings links, and developers run the hosting. Studios have their own playbook in [how to get web design clients](/blog/how-to-get-web-design-clients), and partner agencies sit near the top of it, so referrals can run both ways.

Make the offer easy to resell. A fixed-fee migration check before launch, plus 60 days of monitoring after it, fits inside a studio's proposal. If you'd rather add sites to your own offer, [white-label web design partners](/blog/white-label-web-design-partners) build them under your brand.

### 3. Outreach timed to a search problem

Outreach works when it names the reason you're writing. The reasons worth watching:

- a job post for an SEO manager, a content marketer or a head of growth ([companies hiring for a role](/plays/companies-hiring));
- a post or press release about a redesign, a new platform or a new domain;
- a marketer posting about lost traffic after a Google update;
- a launch in a new country or language;
- a funding round followed by marketing hires.

[How to find businesses that need SEO](/blog/how-to-find-businesses-that-need-seo) covers where to see each one. The first message asks one question about the signal and leaves the audit out: "Saw you're moving the store to a new platform in March. Who's mapping the old URLs?" The unsolicited audit PDF used to be the standard opener. Today it lands in the pile Google warns owners about. [SEO cold email templates](/blog/seo-cold-outreach) has nine openers tied to a reason, plus follow-ups.

This is the channel [Pancake for SEO agencies](/for/seo-agencies) is built around.

### 4. White-label SEO for agencies that sell it

A marketing agency that lists SEO on its services page but has no SEO specialist on its team page has a delivery gap. White label fills it: you do the work under the agency's brand and invoice the agency, not the end client.

The trade is lower rates, since the agency keeps a margin, in exchange for volume you don't have to sell. Put three things in writing before the first shared client: a non-solicit clause, reporting in the agency's template, and who answers the client's questions. Agencies with job ads open for SEO specialists are the warmest prospects, because they've admitted the gap. To see the sale from their side, read [how marketing agencies get clients](/blog/how-to-get-clients-for-marketing-agency).

## Channels 5 to 10: proof that closes and inbound that compounds

### 5. Your own rankings and case studies

An SEO agency that doesn't rank for its own niche has a credibility problem at every pitch. Pick the searches your buyers type, such as "Shopify SEO agency" or "SaaS site migration," and earn those positions before you chase broad terms.

Google's hiring guide tells buyers to ask for examples of previous work, so build three case studies around one problem each: a migration that held its traffic, a recovery after a core update, a new market launched from zero. Show the Search Console chart with the dates marked, get the client's permission, and say what you did in plain words. Expect six months or more before this channel brings inquiries. It lifts every other channel sooner, because prospects look you up before they reply.

### 6. Semrush Agency Partners and review directories

Directories put you in front of buyers who are already comparing agencies. The SEO-specific one is [Semrush Agency Partners](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1078-agency-partners-platform). As of November 2026, a listing costs $90 a month, buyers filter the list by service, industry, budget and location, and each agency carries an Agency Score from 1 to 5 that weighs its own website's performance and its Semrush Academy certifications. Leads from its matching form stay open for two business days, so answer the same day.

On review directories such as Clutch, [the profile itself is free](https://help.clutch.co/en/knowledge/free-features-clutch-offers) and Clutch's team verifies each client review before publishing it. A profile is worth what its reviews say, so ask for one at the end of every engagement.

### 7. Marketplaces: Upwork, Fiverr and Contra

Marketplaces can land a new consultant a first paid job within weeks, mostly fixed-scope work: a technical audit, migration QA, a content brief. Buyers compare proposals side by side, so rates stay low. Use them to earn reviews and one case study, and read each platform's terms before you move a client relationship off it.

### 8. Communities where site owners ask for help

Site owners post their problems in public: indexing errors, a traffic drop, a migration gone wrong. Google's Search Central Help Community, r/SEO and r/bigseo carry those threads, as do Slack groups for marketers and founders. Read each community's rules on promotion before you post, answer the question in the thread, and let people find your agency through your profile.

Pick the ones where buyers ask, not where SEOs talk shop: peer groups are good for learning and referrals, and thin for clients.

### 9. Local search and local groups

For an agency that sells to local businesses, your own Google Business Profile is the shop window. Fill every field, collect reviews from local clients, and give each city you serve a page with a real result from that city. Then join the groups where owners meet, such as the chamber of commerce, and team up with the local web designers owners already use. Signal-based outreach does little here, because a plumber rarely posts about rankings.

### 10. Newsletters, talks and free tools

A short note after each core update, sent to marketers who signed up for it, keeps you in mind for the month a drop hits. Talks at e-commerce and SaaS marketing meetups put you in front of buyers, while talks at SEO conferences put you in front of competitors. A free tool, such as a redirect checker or an hreflang validator, brings requests from people who chose to ask. An audit someone asked for opens a sales conversation. One you push is a cold email with an attachment.

## If you need an SEO client this month

Speed comes from warm contacts and urgent problems, not from new channels. A four-week plan:

1. **Week 1: one offer, warm list.** Package one fixed-fee project with a deadline built in, such as a pre-launch migration check or a core update review. Send it to every past client and to five partner studios.
2. **Week 2: 25 signal-based messages.** Companies hiring an SEO manager, teams announcing a new platform, marketers posting about a drop. One question each, no audit attached.
3. **Week 3: follow-ups and calls.** One follow-up per message, each adding something useful. Book a call with anyone who answers.
4. **Week 4: proposals within 48 hours of each call.** Lead with the fixed-fee project and show the retainer as the next step.

What not to do in a hurry: discount the retainer, promise rankings, or send free audits to strangers. Each one makes the next sale harder.

## From first reply to a signed retainer

The first call decides whether you sell a project or a long relationship. Four habits help:

- **Ask for read-only Search Console access.** Google's hiring guide tells owners to grant only read access at the audit stage, so asking for exactly that shows you know the rules.
- **Answer the interview questions before they come up.** Bring two examples of past work from their industry, say how you measure success, and give a timeframe you can defend.
- **Sell a paid first step.** A two-to-four-week audit or migration plan, priced on its own, tests the relationship before a 12-month retainer does.
- **Answer the AI search question with Google's own words.** Expect the question. Google says there are [no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), and that traffic from those features counts in Search Console's "Web" search type. Sell the fundamentals and a way to measure them, not a shortcut.
