## What is a team agent platform?

A team agent platform deploys shared AI agents accessible across a team, usually inside Slack or Discord. The pattern: a founder or ops lead connects the tool, and every team member can @-mention the agent or trigger it from a shared workspace. The agent answers questions, executes tasks, and produces outputs — documents, data analyses, code — that the team uses.

The defining property is that the human team still runs the company. AI agents assist execution and reduce toil. The team is still the unit of work.

That is the category analyst Ry Walker covered in their June 2026 team agent platforms report. It is a well-defined and well-funded category: Viktor raised $75M at a $15M ARR run rate; Dust raised $40M; Glean hit a $7.2B valuation. The market is validating the "AI as team member" thesis.

Pancake belongs to a different category: the AI GTM team. Its agents go after new customers, and Slack is where each new lead lands for your approval.

## The structural difference

Every team agent platform in the table above operates on the same assumption: a human team is the unit of work, and AI agents assist.

Viktor takes tasks in Slack or Teams, and it can run the ones you approve on a schedule. Dust grounds agents in company knowledge and lets teams query them. Glean indexes enterprise data and surfaces AI answers across it. Claude Tag adds one shared Claude to the channels a team already works in.

All four are general. Point them at research, reporting, code, or support, and they will take a swing at it.

Pancake starts from a different question: where will your next customers come from? You add your website, and Pancake learns your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof, and objections. It keeps them in one shared memory, the GTM Brain, which every agent reads and which gets sharper with every result.

From there, the work runs on its own schedule. Pancake watches six kinds of buying signals, from posts about a topic you pick to job posts that name a tool you replace. Once you approve a lead, Pancake starts the conversation from your own account on the professional social network: a profile visit, a like on a recent post, an invite with no note, then up to three messages. The first message asks one question tied to the signal, so the exchange opens warm instead of as a pitch.

Nobody on your team has to prompt it. The prospecting happens every day, whether or not anyone has time for it that week.

That focus on customers is also why the buyer is different. A team agent platform is bought by a team that wants help with many kinds of work. Pancake is bought by a founder or a small B2B team that wants more conversations with buyers. [See how Pancake finds your buyers](/).

## Who each platform is actually for

**Pancake** is for founders and small B2B companies whose bottleneck is finding customers. It picks out the people behind buying signals, reaches out to them, and writes articles aimed at Google and at AI answers. You approve every lead and every article, so you stay in control.

**Viktor** is for teams that already exist and want a capable AI coworker inside Slack or Teams. The zero-friction install and 3,200+ integrations are designed for someone who wants to add AI to their workflow without reconfiguring how work gets done. If you have a team of five and you want AI to handle research, data synthesis, and cross-tool execution — Viktor is the most mature option in that pattern. $75M raised, $15M ARR. It is a real product with real traction.

**Dust** is for organizations where knowledge is the bottleneck. Its core differentiation is grounding agents in your company's actual data — Notion, Google Docs, Confluence, GitHub. If your team spends significant time finding information or answering internal questions, Dust is designed for that. 3,000+ organizations, 300,000+ shared agents deployed.

**Glean** is an enterprise play. $7.2B valuation, 250 million+ agent actions. If you are a 500-person organization with sprawling internal knowledge and a need for AI answers that respect permissions and data governance, Glean is the category leader.

**Claude Tag** launched in beta in June 2026 and replaced the Claude in Slack app. It puts a shared Claude in the Slack channels you choose, where anyone can tag it with a task and see its work. It keeps memory per channel and can schedule tasks for itself. It comes with the Claude Team and Enterprise plans, so it suits companies that already work in Claude.

## Where team agent platforms stop

The team agent model has a ceiling: it scales with headcount rather than replacing it. Adding Viktor to a team of five does not reduce that team to three. It makes five people more productive.

That is a good value proposition. For prospecting, though, a general agent needs you to design the job first: which lists, which tools, which messages, which follow-ups. The platform supplies the agent. The GTM thinking still comes from you.

Pancake arrives with that job already defined. You choose the signals, approve the leads, and answer the replies. Pancake finds the people, picks the leads and opens every conversation.
