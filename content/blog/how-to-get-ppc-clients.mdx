**TL;DR:** PPC clients come from three places: accounts you already run, agencies that sell the services around paid media, and companies showing a fresh need in public, such as a paid media job post or a founder writing about rising acquisition costs. Partner badges from Google and Microsoft help a buyer trust you once they find you; they rarely start the conversation. Qualify on ad spend before anything else, because an account too small to carry your fee won't survive a bad month.

Below is how to get PPC clients for a paid media agency of 2 to 30 people: the spend floor, nine channels in one table, partner terms checked on Google's and Microsoft's own pages, and the signals that a company is about to spend. If paid media is one line in a full-service offer, the [marketing agency client playbook](/blog/how-to-get-clients-for-marketing-agency) covers the rest of the mix.

---

## Set your floor before you pick channels

Write down the smallest account you can take. A client who pays more in fees than the account can earn back will leave, whatever channel brought them in.

Say your minimum fee is $2,500 a month (an example; use your own). A brand spending $4,000 a month on ads pays you more than half its media budget again, and one flat month makes you the first cut. A brand spending $30,000 pays you a twelfth of it and can sit through a slow quarter. Ask about spend on the first call, in the first ten minutes.

Then sort the buyers you want:

- **E-commerce brands.** A head of growth or an e-commerce lead buys on return on ad spend. Expect Shopping, Performance Max and paid social.
- **B2B software companies.** A demand generation lead buys on pipeline and cost per qualified lead. Expect search, paid social and a long argument about attribution.
- **Local service businesses.** An owner buys on calls and booked jobs. They find agencies through referrals and local search, and they rarely post jobs or write in public, so the signals below won't find them.

Signal-based outreach fits the first two. [Pancake for PPC agencies](/for/ppc-agencies) shows what those buyers post and hire for.

## 1. Current clients and the platform they don't run yet

The cheapest new revenue sits in accounts you already manage. A client you run on Google Search may need Shopping, YouTube or Microsoft Ads. A client you run on Meta may be ignoring search demand. Bring it to the quarterly review with the numbers that justify a test.

Client contacts move, too. A growth lead who liked working with you reaches a new company with a budget and an agency decision ahead. Keep a list of client-side marketers and where each one works now.

Ask for referrals right after a result the client can repeat over dinner: the month return on ad spend cleared target, the quarter cost per lead fell by a third. "Who do you know who spends on ads with nobody watching the account?" gets you a name.

## 2 and 3. Agencies that sell next to you, by referral or white label

Agencies that don't run ads keep meeting clients who need them. A Shopify agency launches a store and the founder asks where the traffic will come from. An SEO agency's client wants results before organic rankings move. Web studios, CRO consultants and fractional CMOs hear the same question.

Two models work:

- **Referral partnerships.** They send you clients who need paid media; you send them clients who need SEO, a new store or a new site. The [SEO client playbook](/blog/how-to-get-seo-clients) and the [Shopify client playbook](/blog/how-to-get-shopify-clients) show how those partners find their own clients.
- **White-label PPC.** You run accounts under a full-service agency's brand. They keep the client and a margin; you get volume with no sales cost. Settle three things in writing first: who talks to the client, who owns the ad accounts, and what happens to your work if the agency loses the client.

## 4. Companies about to spend more on ads

Outbound works for a PPC agency when the message names something the company did in public. Four signals point to paid media budgets:

- **A paid media job post.** PPC specialist, paid search manager, paid social lead, performance marketing manager. The company may still make the hire, so pitch coverage while the seat is open, or support for the new hire on the platforms they don't know.
- **Job posts that name the ad platforms.** A growth or marketing role asking for Google Ads, Microsoft Ads or Meta Ads Manager experience tells you where the money goes today. [Searching job posts by the tools they name](/plays/tech-stack) shows how that works as a Play.
- **A funding round.** Growth targets arrive with the money, and paid acquisition is one of the fastest levers to pull. [Reaching recently funded startups](/plays/recently-funded-startups) shows that search as a Play.
- **Posts about rising CAC, ROAS or Performance Max.** A head of growth who writes that costs keep climbing on one platform has named the problem for you.

Check one thing by hand before you write. Google's Ads Transparency Center lets anyone search an advertiser and see its ads on Google platforms such as Search and YouTube, as [Google's advertiser verification page](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9703665) explains. A company with no search ads and a paid social job post is a different pitch from one that runs both.

Then ask one question and pitch nothing: "Saw the paid search opening. Is the plan to bring the account in-house, or to add someone next to your agency?"

## 5. Google Partners: what the badge takes

Google Partners is the badge buyers recognize. As of November 2026, [Google's Partner requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9702452), checked daily, are:

- **Performance:** an optimization score of at least 70% on your registered Google Ads manager account. Google notes you can apply or dismiss recommendations based on your clients' goals.
- **Spend:** $10,000 in ad spend over 90 days across the accounts you manage, about $3,300 a month in total.
- **Certifications:** at least half of your account strategists certified in Google Ads (capped at 100 users), plus at least one certification in each product area where you spend $500 or more in 90 days.

Signing up makes you a Member. The badge and the directory listing come with Partner and Premier Partner status only, per [Google's benefits table](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9029198). Premier Partner goes to the top 3% of participating companies in each country, decided once a year on client growth, new-client growth, retention, spend outside Search, and total spend.

Run every client account under your manager account from day one, since spend only counts across the accounts you manage. Then show the badge where buyers compare agencies: proposals and directory profiles.

## 6. Microsoft Advertising's partner program

Microsoft's program has three tiers, partner, select and elite, set by engagement and annual spend, according to its [program FAQ](https://msadvertisingpartnerprogram.powerappsportals.com/FAQ.pdf), which lists benefits current as of January 2026. Agencies need three months of clean fraud checks and a spend threshold that the FAQ doesn't state.

Every tier gets the program badge and a listing in the online partner directory; elite partners get a more prominent listing. Select and elite enrollment runs January to March, after a review each December. The partner tier is open all year: once you meet its entry requirements, your account's Super Admin signs in and accepts the terms.

## 7 to 9: slower channels that make the others convert

**7. Teardowns and posts.** Write about what in-house marketers fight with: why Performance Max swallowed the branded search budget, what you'd change in an account that spends half its money on its own brand name. Growth leads who engage with those posts are worth a message.

**8. Recommendation threads.** Founders ask for paid media agency picks in e-commerce and startup communities. Reply within hours with one line on fit and one result, or name an agency that fits better.

**9. Upwork and Clutch.** Upwork can land a young agency its first small accounts, and Clutch gathers the client reviews buyers read before a call. Both attract buyers who compare on price, so use them to earn proof, then move your hours to channels 1 to 4.

## Propose a test with a finish line

Every PPC buyer has heard "we'll audit your account" from five agencies. Propose a dated test instead: one platform or one product line, six to eight weeks, a target written down before you start, and a decision at the end.

Ask for access early. You can't scope a test without seeing the account structure, the conversion tracking and the search terms. If tracking is broken, fixing it is your first paid project.
