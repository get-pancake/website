**TL;DR:** Sell to the firm's economics, not to lawyer productivity. A firm that bills by the hour earns less when work takes fewer hours, so lead with work it doesn't bill for, like intake, billing and flat-fee matters, or with growth it can't staff. Pick one segment, since a solo practitioner, a 60-lawyer firm and a general counsel buy in different ways, and prepare for a security review built on the lawyer's duty of confidentiality.

Most advice on how to sell to law firms skips the first question: which firm? ## How to sell to law firms of each size, and to in-house teams

"Law firm" covers a solo immigration lawyer and a firm of a thousand lawyers with its own CIO. Each buys differently, so choose the segment before you write a word of outreach. As a rough cut:

| Buyer | Who decides | Who else weighs in | Where they look | What wins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solo and small firm (1 to 10 lawyers) | The owner or managing partner, who also practices | An office manager or senior paralegal | Their practice-management tool's app directory, peers, local bar events | Works inside the tool they already run, monthly pricing, set up in an afternoon |
| Mid-size firm (10 to 100 lawyers) | Managing partner, with a firm administrator or COO | IT lead, practice group leaders, the partners who bill the most | Peer firms, conferences, their IT provider | A pilot with one practice group and a result in dollars |
| Large firm (100+ lawyers) | CIO or innovation team, with partner sponsors | Information security, procurement, the firm's risk counsel | Peer firms, ILTA's community | A security packet, references from peer firms, a scoped pilot |
| In-house legal team | General counsel | Legal operations, IT security, procurement, finance | CLOC and peer GCs | Faster contract turnaround, intake that routes work, lower outside counsel spend |

Pick one row and one practice area. An intake tool for immigration firms and a contract tool for general counsels share little beyond the word "legal."

## The billable hour decides what a firm will pay for

A firm that bills by the hour sells time, so a tool that saves time can cut its revenue on the same matter. Sell where saved time turns into profit:

- **Flat-fee and contingency work.** In flat-fee practices such as immigration or estate planning, and in contingency work such as personal injury, the firm keeps what it saves. Efficiency is margin.
- **Work nobody bills.** Intake, conflict checks, time entry, billing and collections are overhead at every firm. Shrinking them costs no revenue.
- **Capacity.** A firm turning away matters is short of lawyer hours, so every hour saved becomes new billable work. Sell more matters per lawyer.
- **In-house teams.** Legal is a cost center inside a company, so saved time is the pitch, along with lower outside counsel bills.

Say your product drafts first versions of demand letters. A personal injury firm working on contingency keeps every hour it saves. A litigation firm billing by the hour may bill fewer hours for the same letter. Same product, opposite pitch.

## The confidentiality rules behind every law firm security review

Law firm security reviews grow out of the rules of professional conduct, so read the rules before your first firm quotes them at you.

- **Confidentiality.** [ABA Model Rule 1.6(c)](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_6_confidentiality_of_information/) requires lawyers to make reasonable efforts to prevent unauthorized access to client information. That is why a firm asks how you protect the data it sends you.
- **Technology competence.** [Comment 8 to Model Rule 1.1](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_1_competence/comment_on_rule_1_1/) says lawyers should keep up with "the benefits and risks associated with relevant technology." It gives your champion a reason to look at new tools, and a duty to question them.
- **Generative AI.** [ABA Formal Opinion 512](https://www.americanbar.org/news/abanews/aba-news-archives/2024/07/aba-issues-guidance-lawyers-use-ai-tools/), issued July 29, 2024, tells lawyers using these tools to weigh competence, confidentiality, client communication and fees. A lawyer must keep client information confidential unless the client gives informed consent, and may bill for time spent using and checking the tool, not for learning it.

The Model Rules are a template, and each state adopts its own version, so check the states your buyers practice in. Then build the packet a firm will ask for:

1. Where client data is stored, and who at your company can access it.
2. Whether client data trains any model, yours or a provider's.
3. Retention and deletion, including what happens to matter data when a firm leaves.
4. Encryption, single sign-on and audit logs.
5. Your sub-processors, including any AI model provider.
6. Your SOC 2 status, or the date you expect the report.

Large firms send their own questionnaire on top. Answer it fully the first time, then reuse the answers.

## Signals that a firm or legal team is ready to buy

Law firms announce their growing pains in job posts. The posts tell you a firm is busy, which tools it runs, and who will own a new system.

- **Paralegal, legal assistant and intake openings.** More matters than staff. At a small firm, the managing partner is the buyer.
- **A firm administrator, COO or legal operations hire.** Someone is about to own the firm's systems. Write to the partner who hired them, or to the new hire once they start.
- **Practice tools named in job posts.** Firm posts often ask for experience with Clio, MyCase, iManage or NetDocuments, and in-house posts may name the contract system. If you plug into or replace one, those posts are your list ([finding companies by tech stack](/plays/tech-stack)).
- **A funded company building a legal team.** A startup fresh off a round that posts its first in-house counsel role is building a legal function from zero.
- **A new general counsel.** A new GC inherits tools and outside counsel they didn't choose. Spot the announcement by hand.
- **Firm growth.** A new office, a lateral group joining, a merger. The legal press covers these, and you read it by hand.
- **Posts about the problem.** Partners and GCs writing about intake, billable hours, contract backlogs or outside counsel bills.
- **Lookalikes.** Firms with the practice area, size and tools of your best customer.

[The B2B buying signals guide](/blog/buying-signals-b2b) explains how to rank signals like these, and [the founder-led sales playbook](/blog/founder-led-sales) covers running these calls yourself until the pattern repeats.

## Where lawyers and legal ops teams look for tools

Lawyers check with other lawyers, and they look for tools inside the systems and events they already use.

- **Practice-management app directories.** [Clio's App Directory](https://www.clio.com/app-directory/) sorts integrations by category, from intake and e-filing to AI and automation. For a small-firm product, a working integration and a listing put you where solo and small firms shop.
- **ABA TECHSHOW.** The ABA's legal technology conference runs [March 3 to 6, 2027](https://www.techshow.com/conference/), at McCormick Place in Chicago, with an expo of more than 100 vendors that lawyers can test side by side.
- **ILTACON.** The International Legal Technology Association's yearly conference draws the technology and knowledge teams of larger firms and legal departments.
- **CLOC.** The Corporate Legal Operations Consortium is where in-house legal ops people trade notes, through regional groups and its Global Institute.
- **Bar associations.** A talk at a local bar association event, on the problem your product solves, reaches lawyers who chose to hear about it.
- **Peer introductions.** Ask every happy firm for one introduction to a firm like it.

## Writing to managing partners and general counsels

Lawyers read for precision. Say what you saw, ask one question, and never ask about a client matter.

- **Paralegal opening at an immigration firm:** "Saw the paralegal opening. Is that for keeping up with new cases, or with filings on existing ones?"
- **Clio in the job post:** "Your legal assistant posting mentions Clio. Do your lawyers enter time as they go, or at the end of the week?"
- **A GC posting about contract backlog:** "Your post on the contract queue stuck with me. Which contract type takes the most back-and-forth?"
- **A funded startup hiring its first counsel:** "Congrats on the round. Will your first lawyer start with a contract tool, or with a shared inbox?"

Write to the person who decides: the managing partner or administrator at a small or mid-size firm, the general counsel or legal ops lead in-house. Associates and paralegals use the product, but the decision sits above them.
