**TL;DR:** A US startup raising under Regulation D files a Form D with the SEC within 15 days of its first sale, and anyone can search those filings for free on EDGAR. The announcement comes on the company's own schedule, through its press page, a wire release or Crunchbase, and it's where you learn what the money is for. Use the filing to spot the round, the announcement to read the plan, and wait for a second signal, like job posts for the team you sell to, before you write.

This is for anyone who sells to startups: SaaS founders, agencies, recruiters, consultants. Below: six sources and what each misses, how to read a Form D in five minutes, who to contact at each stage, and how long to wait after the news.

## How to find startups that raised money: six public sources

No single source catches every round. The SEC filing can appear before the announcement, the announcement carries the detail, and a database like Crunchbase collects announced rounds after the fact.

| Source | What it tells you | When it appears | What it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEC Form D on EDGAR | Company, executives and directors, amount sold, date of first sale, number of investors | Due within 15 days of the first sale | Non-US raises, rounds under other exemptions, investor names |
| Company press page or wire release | Amount, stage, lead investor, what the money is for | When the company chooses | Rounds kept quiet |
| Crunchbase and Crunchbase News | Announced rounds in one searchable place, plus roundups of the largest | After the announcement, sometimes with a lag | Rounds never announced |
| Investor portfolio pages and posts | New companies in a firm's portfolio | When the firm publishes | Angel checks and small funds |
| Accelerator batches | Companies with a known first check | When the batch is listed, before demo day | Startups outside accelerators |
| Companies House (UK) | New shares issued by a UK company | Within one month of the allotment | Everything outside the UK |

A few notes on the less obvious rows. Crunchbase News publishes a weekly list of the biggest US rounds, and its [methodology note](https://news.crunchbase.com/ai/biggest-funding-rounds-ai-anthropic-65b-dominates/) says the list draws on announced rounds in the Crunchbase database, with a small lag for rounds reported late in the week. Accelerators list their companies, Y Combinator in its Startup Directory, and [YC commits $500,000](https://www.ycombinator.com/deal) to every company on the day it is accepted, as of November 2026. Each company in a new batch has a known first check. In the UK, the [Companies Act 2006 requires a return of allotment](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/555) within one month of issuing new shares, which makes the Companies House record a funding trail of its own.

## How to read a Form D in five minutes

Form D is a short notice, not a prospectus. The SEC says a company [must file it within 15 days after the first sale](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/filing-form-d-notice) of securities in an offering under Rule 504 or 506 of Regulation D, with no filing fee, on EDGAR. The date of first sale is the day the first investor is "irrevocably contractually committed" to invest.

To find them, open [EDGAR full-text search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/), set the form type to D, pick a filing date range, and filter by the state of the company's principal office. Then read six items on each notice:

1. **Item 3, Related persons.** The names of executive officers and directors. A partner from a venture firm listed as a new director is a clue to who led the round.
2. **Item 4, Industry group.** "Pooled Investment Fund" means a fund raising money, not a startup. Skip those first.
3. **Item 5, Issuer size.** A revenue range, or "Decline to Disclose."
4. **Item 7, Date of first sale.** Compare it with the filing date and with any announcement to see how old the money is.
5. **Item 13, Offering and sales amounts.** The total offered against the total sold. Money left to sell, or an offering marked "Indefinite," can mean the round is still open.
6. **Item 14, Investors.** How many have invested so far.

Item 9 helps too: under the form's instructions, a convertible note is filed as debt, so a note round reads differently from a priced equity round.

### Funds outnumber startups on EDGAR

Filter before you read. When we ran EDGAR full-text search on November 3, 2026 for new Form D notices filed in October 2026, it returned 5,780 of them. About two-thirds, 3,841, named "Pooled Investment Fund": venture funds, private equity vehicles and other pools. Roughly 400 named "Other Technology."

Small rounds look small on the form. One October 2026 notice from a California technology startup reported a first sale on October 2, a filing on October 14, $100,000 sold to a single investor, the offering size marked "Indefinite," and revenue "Decline to Disclose." That's a first check into a round that may still be open, not a finished seed round. Write to that founder about the problem you solve, not about their raise.

## Reading the round: stage, plan and who signs

The most useful sentence in a funding announcement is the one about what the money is for. "To grow our go-to-market team" points at sales and marketing hires. "To expand into Europe" points at localization, recruiting and local partners. "To accelerate product development" points at engineering hires and the tools they'll want.

Stage tells you who to write to:

- **Pre-seed and seed.** The founders sign for almost everything. Write to the CEO or the founder closest to your problem.
- **Series A.** The first heads of sales, marketing or customer success tend to arrive, each with a budget and a mandate to build. They become your buyers for their team's tools.
- **Series B and later.** Functional leaders own their stacks, and a procurement or security review can sit between the yes and the signature.

Two cautions. A round labeled an extension or a bridge can be about runway more than growth, so ask before you assume a budget. And a large round draws every vendor in the category, so the best-funded name on a list isn't always the best prospect on it.

## When to reach out after the announcement

Don't pitch on announcement day. A public announcement reaches every seller at once, so a message that only says congratulations competes with all of them.

Write when the round shows up in a second signal:

- **Job posts for the team you sell to.** Hiring is where the plan turns into spending. A startup posting three support roles after its Series A is building a support team, and that team will need tools. [Job postings as buying signals](/blog/job-postings-as-buying-signals) matches roles to purchases and covers the timing.
- **A new leader for that team.** A first head of marketing tends to arrive with a plan and a short list of things to fix.
- **A post about the plan.** Some founders explain in public what the round is for. That post gives your message something to answer.

Check the careers page a few weeks after the announcement. New openings for the team you sell to are your opening. If nothing changes, ask what the plan is before you assume it includes you. Funding is one signal among several; the [guide to buying signals](/blog/buying-signals-b2b) shows how it combines with the others.

## What to say to a company that recently raised

Ask about the plan the money pays for. Leave the amount out, and never write "now that you have budget." Three openers, by what you sell:

- **Support software:** "Saw the three support roles that went up after the Series A. Are you hiring the team before you choose its tools, or after?"
- **Recruiting:** "Your careers page lists six engineering roles since the round. Are you running all of those searches in-house?"
- **A design studio:** "With a sales team coming, will the pitch deck and product pages be rebuilt in-house, or with outside help?"

Each one names something the company did after the round, and each can be answered in one line. A reply gives you a reason for a call.
