**TL;DR:** Data consulting clients come from three places. People who have seen your work, such as past clients and former colleagues, sign fastest. The platforms your clients run, such as Snowflake and dbt Labs, send work to partners who already bring them deals. And job posts often describe the project before anyone buys it: a first data engineer, a migration with a deadline, a stack named line by line. Read those posts, offer a small fixed-scope first project, and write to the head of data or the CFO rather than the recruiter.

Below: who buys and how each buyer describes the problem, eight channels ranked, how to read a data job post, five first offers, and what partner programs ask in return. The fundamentals that apply to any specialty are in [how to get consulting clients](/blog/how-to-get-consulting-clients).

---

## Who buys data consulting, and the words each buyer uses

Data buyers describe the same problem in different words, and the words tell you which offer to lead with.

**The head of data with a backlog.** A team of three to ten people can't ship the migration, the new models and the dashboards in the same quarter. They say "Snowflake," "dbt," "orchestration" and "capacity," and they buy specific expertise and extra hands.

**The CFO or COO without a data team.** At a 150-person company, finance owns reporting in spreadsheets and a BI tool nobody maintains. They say "the dashboards disagree," "month-end takes too long" and "I don't trust the revenue number." They buy outcomes: one set of numbers, a faster close.

**The startup hiring its first data engineer.** The company has decided data matters, sometimes right after a [funding round](/plays/recently-funded-startups). Until the hire starts, and for months after, it needs someone who has built a stack before.

**The platform's new customer.** A company that signed with Snowflake, Databricks or a BI vendor needs implementation help. These projects arrive through the vendor, which is why partner programs appear in the ranking.

## 1: the people who have already seen your work

Your first five clients are usually people you have worked with: a former manager who now runs data somewhere else, an analyst who became a head of finance, a client from your last firm whose stack outgrew it. Data people move between companies, and each move puts someone who knows your work inside a new budget.

Keep a plain list of everyone you have delivered for or beside, with where they work now. When one of them changes jobs, write that week: "Congrats on the new role. What did you inherit on the data side?"

Ask current clients for introductions during the project, the week a report finally reconciles, not at the handover.

## 2 and 3: read the job post like a project brief

If you're working out how to find data consulting clients you have never met, start here: a data job post often describes the project before it exists. Read the whole post, and match each line to an offer:

- **"Our first data engineer."** There is no foundation yet. Offer a six-week foundation build the hire inherits, or a part-time lead until they start.
- **"Migrate from Redshift to Snowflake" or "move off SQL Server."** A migration with a date. Offer a migration plan and a parallel run, so nothing breaks at cut-over.
- **"Own and clean up our dbt project."** The models grew without tests or documentation. Offer a dbt health check with a fix list ranked by risk.
- **"Power BI developer" next to "single source of truth."** Metrics disagree across teams. Offer a metric definitions sprint with finance in the room.
- **"Optimize warehouse costs."** The bill is growing faster than the team. In dbt Labs' [2026 State of Analytics Engineering survey](https://www.getdbt.com/blog/new-dbt-labs-report-finds-ai-driven-acceleration-is-outpacing-trust-and-governance) of 363 data practitioners and leaders, 57% reported higher warehouse and compute spend, and 36% reported bigger team budgets. Offer a cost review.

The stack line matters most. A post that names Snowflake, dbt, Databricks or BigQuery tells you the tools before the first call, and [finding companies by the tools in their job posts](/plays/tech-stack) turns those lines into a list. [Searching for companies hiring for a role](/plays/companies-hiring) does the same for the first-data-engineer post, and the guide to [job postings as buying signals](/blog/job-postings-as-buying-signals) explains which roles predict which purchases.

Write to the person above the role: the head of data, the CTO or the CFO. A recruiter can't buy a migration plan.

Public posts work the same way. A CFO writing that the dashboards stopped matching after a CRM change, or a data lead describing a migration that stalled, has put the project in writing. Quote it back as a question: "You mentioned the dashboards stopped matching after the CRM change. Is that a definitions problem or a pipeline problem?" ## Five first offers that turn a signal into a project

A small fixed-scope project is easier to approve than a retainer, and it shows the buyer how you work. Five that map to the signals above:

1. **A two-week data audit.** Interviews, a review of the warehouse and the models, and a written list of what to fix in which order.
2. **A migration plan.** Inventory, sequencing, a parallel-run design and a cut-over checklist, priced apart from the migration itself.
3. **A metric definitions sprint.** Finance, sales and data agree on ten core metrics, each written down with its SQL.
4. **A warehouse cost review.** Query patterns, warehouse sizing and job schedules, with each saving estimated.
5. **A fractional data lead.** A few days a month of senior judgment for a company not ready for a full-time head of data.

End each one with a proposal for the next phase. Price it as a fixed fee with a clear deliverable, so it fits under the buyer's own signing limit.

## 4: platform partner programs, and what they ask in return

Snowflake, dbt Labs, Databricks, Microsoft and Google Cloud all run partner programs for consultancies. Read what each one rewards before you join; Snowflake spells it out.

The [Snowflake Partner Network](https://www.snowflake.com/en/why-snowflake/partners/service-partners/) sorts services partners into Select, Premier and Elite tiers, and says the tier reflects a partner's certifications, closed pipeline, deal registrations and customer success stories, as of November 2026. Joining as a Registered partner is free, while each of the three tiers carries an annual fee. Tiered partners get exposure in the Snowflake Partner Directory, and Snowflake counts more than 14,000 partners in its network. Registering deals in the partner portal is how a partner reaches co-sell status.

[dbt Labs' Consulting Partners](https://www.getdbt.com/partners) provide implementation, ongoing development and training to dbt customers. The program adds partner-only enablement, a public Find a partner directory and dbt Developer certification for your team.

Two lessons follow. A directory with thousands of firms won't fill a bench by itself, so use the listing as proof for buyers you found elsewhere. And register every opportunity you bring, because that is what turns a vendor's sellers into a source.

## 5 to 8: peers, communities, public work and marketplaces

### 5. Other consultancies

Large systems integrators subcontract specialist work, and boutique firms pass on projects outside their stack. A Databricks shop gets asked about Power BI; a Power BI shop gets asked about pipelines. Write down what you take and what you refuse, share it with five peer firms, and send work back when it fits them better. Agree up front on who owns the client relationship.

### 6. Communities

Data practitioners ask for help in public: in the dbt Community, in vendor forums, at local meetups. Answer the hard questions well and people look up who you are. Many of these spaces ban vendor pitches, so solve the problem in the thread and let people find your firm on their own.

### 7. Public work

An open-source dbt package, a teardown of a public dataset, a meetup talk, a post on how you cut a warehouse bill: public work lets a buyer judge your thinking before a call. It is slow, and it makes every other channel easier.

### 8. Freelance marketplaces

Marketplaces such as Upwork can land a new freelancer small paid fixes within weeks, at prices set by side-by-side comparison. Use them for proof and reviews, then move to the channels above for larger work.
