Viktor and [Pancake](/) both sell AI that does work instead of answering questions. Past that, they aim at different jobs.

Viktor is a general AI employee. You hand it a task in Slack or Microsoft Teams, and it works across the tools you connect. Pancake is an AI GTM team. Its agents find people who show buying signals, run outreach from your own account, and write articles built to show up in Google and AI answers.

## The short answer

- **Pick Viktor** if you want one AI coworker for many kinds of work: reports, ad audits, internal tools, code, back-office paperwork.
- **Pick Pancake** if your bottleneck is customers and you want the prospecting loop run for you at a flat price.
- **Run both** if you need a generalist for internal work and a dedicated team for pipeline.

## What Viktor is in September 2026

Viktor is an AI employee that lives in Slack and Microsoft Teams. You add it to your workspace, connect your tools, most with a one-click sign-in, and message it like a colleague. Viktor says setup takes about two minutes. Web, desktop and phone apps sit next to the Slack and Teams versions.

Viktor has its own computer in the cloud. It writes and runs code to finish a task, so what you get back is a finished thing: a PDF report, a spreadsheet, a deployed web app, a pull request. It connects to 3,200+ tools, from HubSpot and Stripe to GitHub and Google Ads. When a tool is missing, it can plug into a custom MCP server or API.

Viktor also works without a prompt. You can ask it to run any task on a schedule, such as "every weekday at 9 AM, post yesterday's revenue in #finance". It proposes automations based on what it sees your team doing. It writes down how your team works in notes it calls skills. Before a sensitive action, it asks for approval in the chat, and you can mark a tool as always approved.

The company has money behind it. In May 2026, Viktor announced a $75M Series A led by Accel, with Slack co-founders Stewart Butterfield and Cal Henderson among its angel investors. Press coverage of the round reported about $15M in annualized revenue roughly ten weeks after launch. Viktor's site now says 65,000+ teams use it.

## What Pancake is

Pancake is built for founders and small B2B companies. Everything it does is about finding buyers and starting conversations with them.

Setup starts with your website. Pancake reads it and builds the GTM Brain, a shared memory of your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof and objections. Every agent works from that memory, and the memory keeps learning from what works.

From there, Pancake watches six kinds of buying signals:

1. **Keyword:** people posting about a topic you choose, like "ticket backlog".
2. **Competitor:** people engaging with a rival's posts.
3. **Influencer:** people engaging with posts from a voice your buyers follow.
4. **Own brand:** people engaging with your own posts.
5. **Hiring:** companies with open roles that match your buyer.
6. **Stack:** companies whose job posts name a tool, for example one you replace.

New leads arrive each morning, and each one says which signal picked it. You approve leads in the app or in Slack, where Pancake posts every new lead. Outreach runs from your own account on the professional network: a profile visit, a like on a recent post, an invite with no note, then up to three messages. The first message makes no pitch. It asks one light question tied to the signal.

Pancake also writes articles for Google and AI search, and you approve each one.

## A normal week: which one does the job

The quickest way to see the difference is to list real jobs and ask who does them.

| Job | Viktor | Pancake |
|---|---|---|
| Post a Stripe revenue summary every Monday | Yes, as a scheduled task | Not offered |
| Audit Meta and Google Ads spend | Yes | Not offered |
| Build an internal dashboard or approval app | Yes, deployed with a database and login | Not offered |
| Triage bugs and open pull requests | Yes | Not offered |
| Update deals in HubSpot or Salesforce | Yes | Not offered (no CRM sync) |
| Find people posting about the problem you solve | Not built in; depends on the tools you connect | Yes, keyword signal |
| Find people engaging with a competitor's posts | Not built in; depends on the tools you connect | Yes, competitor signal |
| Find companies hiring for roles that match your buyer | Not built in; depends on the tools you connect | Yes, hiring signal |
| Cold email sequences | Yes, through tools like Instantly | Not offered |
| Outreach from your own professional-network account | Through connected tools like HeyReach | Yes: visit, like, invite, up to three messages |
| SEO and AI-search articles | Yes, written and published on a schedule | Yes, written for Google and AI answers, approved by you |

Count the rows and Viktor covers more of them. That is the point of a generalist. Every row Pancake covers is about getting in front of buyers.

## Where Viktor wins

**Breadth.** Hand Viktor almost any task you can describe. One week it assembles your investor update from Stripe and your CRM. The next it audits ad spend, turns customer feedback into Linear tickets, and ships a small internal app. Pancake does none of that.

**Where it lives.** If your team already works in Slack or Teams, Viktor joins the workspace as a member. Anyone on the team can ask it for help without learning a new tool.

**Integrations.** Viktor lists 3,200+ tools, plus custom MCP servers and APIs. If a process spans ten systems, Viktor can reach all ten.

**A free start.** Viktor fronts every new workspace $100 of credits, and you don't enter a card to use them. You can test real workflows before you pay.

## Where Pancake wins

**The signals come built in.** With Viktor, outbound is a stack you assemble: a data tool for lists, a sending tool, a CRM, and prompts or schedules that tie them together. Pancake ships the signal layer. You choose the keywords, the competitors and the voices your buyers follow, and each lead arrives with the reason attached.

**Outreach that opens with context.** Every lead comes from a signal, so the first message has something specific to ask about: a post the person wrote, a rival's post they engaged with, a role their company is hiring for. A sequence sent to a filtered contact list has to find its hook some other way.

**A memory built for selling.** Viktor learns how your team works in general. Pancake's GTM Brain holds one subject: who buys from you, and why. Lead picks, messages and articles all draw on it.

**A fixed bill.** Your Pancake bill is the same $99 every month. Viktor's bill follows usage, and a scheduled workflow draws credits on every run.

## Outbound: where they compete head to head

Outbound is where the two products meet, so it deserves more detail.

Viktor's own site lays out an outbound routine. It builds ICP lead lists from Apollo, enriches the contacts, pushes them to HubSpot or Attio, launches email sequences through Instantly, and reports what converts. It also connects to HeyReach to run sequences on the professional network. Viktor operates that stack for you. You still pick the tools, pay each vendor, and decide who goes on the list.

Pancake replaces the stack for one channel. There is no list tool or sending tool to wire up. It finds people through the six signals, tells you why it picked each one, and runs the outreach from your own account. It sends no email. If cold email is your main channel, Viktor with Instantly, or a dedicated email tool, fits better.

Control works differently too. Viktor's approvals sit at the action: it checks with you before a sensitive step. Pancake's sit at the lead: you approve who gets contacted. You don't approve each message one by one.

## Content and AI search

Both products write articles. The difference is who drives them.

Viktor's content engine writes SEO blog posts, launch copy, email campaigns and social drafts. It can publish to your CMS or GitHub on any schedule you set. You supply the brief, the voice and the topics, or you teach them to Viktor over time.

Pancake writes articles built to rank in Google and to be picked up in AI answers. It works from the GTM Brain, so topics and angles come from what your buyers care about.

## Pricing as of September 2026

| | Viktor | Pancake |
|---|---|---|
| Free option | $100 in credits, no credit card | 3-day free trial, credit card required |
| Entry price | $50 a month for 20,000 credits | $99 a month, flat |
| How the bill grows | More credits: $100 for 40,000, up to $50,000 a month for 20 million | One plan, no tiers |
| Seats | None; credits are shared by the workspace | None |
| Usage charges | Every task and scheduled run uses credits | None |
| Enterprise | Custom plan with invoicing, DPA and SLA | No separate plan |

Viktor's credits track the model costs of each task. Its pricing page puts a quick task at 100 to 300 credits, a complex workflow at 500 to 1,500, and a full project at 2,000 to 5,000. At the entry rate of 400 credits per dollar, that works out to roughly $0.25 to $0.75 for a quick task and $5 to $12.50 for a full project. Unused monthly credits roll over for one month.

Here is what that means in practice. Say you schedule a 1,000-credit prospecting workflow every day. That is about 30,000 credits a month, which lands on Viktor's $75 tier. Apollo, Instantly and any other tool the workflow drives bill you on top.

Pancake costs [$99 a month](/pricing) with every agent included. You can cancel anytime, and access runs to the end of the billing period.

Viktor's entry plan is cheaper. Whether it stays cheaper depends on how much you run through it and which paid tools it drives. For the full cost walk-through, read [Viktor vs Pancake pricing](/blog/viktor-vs-pancake-pricing).

## Setup and daily use

**Viktor:** install it in Slack or Teams, connect your tools, and start asking. Schedules are set in conversation ("move the daily brief to 8 AM"). Daily use is you, or anyone on the team, handing it work.

**Pancake:** add your website, then pick the signals to watch. From then on, your part is review: approve the day's leads in the app or from Slack, and approve articles when drafts are ready.

## Who should pick which

### Choose Viktor if

- Your team runs on Slack or Teams and wants one AI it can hand any task to.
- Most of the work you want off your plate is internal, from finance reports to bug tickets.
- You already pay for an outbound stack and want someone to operate it.
- You want to start free, with no card.

### Choose Pancake if

- Your problem is pipeline: not enough of the right people to talk to.
- You would rather not assemble and pay for separate list, data and sending tools.
- You want every first message tied to a signal, such as a post or a job opening.
- You want one flat monthly price.

### Use both if

Split the work by job. Viktor takes the internal work and any email outreach. Pancake takes signal-based prospecting on the professional network, plus your search articles.
