**TL;DR:** A web design referral program has three parts: a short list of people who see website projects before you do, a reward that fits your margin, and the habit of asking at the right moments. Past clients refer best on launch day and when the new site brings its first result. Partner agencies refer steadily once the terms fit on one page. Pay a flat fee, a share of the first project, or referrals back, and log every intro.

Referrals sit at the top of the ranking in [how to get web design clients](/blog/how-to-get-web-design-clients) because the trust is already there when the conversation starts. The work is making them happen on purpose instead of by luck.

## Who sends web design referrals

Four groups can send you clients, and each wants something different in return.

- **Past clients.** When a peer admires their new site, they get asked who built it. They want to look smart for recommending you, and a thank-you that fits the relationship.
- **Partner agencies.** SEO, branding, PPC and content agencies run into the website in their own work. They want a builder who ships on time, never goes after their clients, and sends work back.
- **Freelancers.** Developers, designers and copywriters turn down projects that are too big, too small or outside their skills. They want referrals in return, or a finder's fee.
- **Local advisers.** Accountants, bookkeepers, business coaches and IT providers get asked for recommendations by the owners they serve. They want their clients to stay happy, so your work has to make them look good.

Local studios get the most from clients and advisers. B2B studios get more from agencies and freelancers.

## How much to pay: web design referral fee options

There is no standard web design referral fee. Choose a model from what a new client is worth to you and what each referrer values. The examples below use a hypothetical $12,000 build with a $300 monthly care plan, not market rates.

| Model | How it works | Example on the $12,000 build | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat fee | A fixed amount per signed client | $500 once the deposit clears | Past clients and one-off referrers |
| Share of the first project | A percentage of the first project's invoices | 10% of $12,000 = $1,200 | Agencies and freelancers who send real projects |
| Share of the first year | A smaller percentage of all billing for 12 months | 5% of $12,000 + $3,600 in care = $780 | Partners who send care-plan clients |
| Referrals back | No cash: you send them work in return | Your SEO requests go to them | Agencies selling services you don't |
| Client credit | Credit against the referrer's own invoices | One free month of care plan | Clients on a care plan or retainer |

To set your number, work out what a signed client costs you through your other channels: ad spend, directory fees, or the hours of outreach behind one project. A referral fee below that figure is a good trade, since the referred client arrives warm. Pay when the client's first payment clears, not on the intro, so a lead that goes nowhere costs you nothing.

## Web design affiliate programs: getting paid by your tools

"Web design affiliate program" means two different things, and a studio can use both.

**Programs that pay you.** The builders you already use pay for the clients you bring them. [Webflow's Partner Program](https://webflow.com/certified-partners/apply) pays 10% of the Site plans and add-ons you bring in at its Foundations tier and 20% at the Certified and Premium tiers, for the first 12 months of each subscription. [Framer's creator program](https://www.framer.com/creators) pays 50% of a referred subscription for 12 months when the person upgrades. Both terms are as of November 2026. Tell clients when a tool you recommend pays you a commission; it costs nothing and keeps your advice credible. The [guide to getting Webflow clients](/blog/how-to-get-webflow-clients) covers what the higher Webflow tiers add besides commission.

**Programs you pay for.** A classic affiliate program pays strangers a commission for tracked leads, which suits low-priced packages sold online. For custom builds, a few named partners with a written agreement work better.

## When to ask clients for referrals

Ask when the client feels best about the work, and make the ask about one kind of person.

1. **Launch day.** The client is proud of the site and showing it around. That's when peers ask who built it.
2. **The first result.** The first inquiry through the new form, the first online booking, the first sale. Send the result with the ask.
3. **Unprompted praise.** When a client says something kind, thank them and ask for the intro in the same reply.
4. **The 90-day review.** If they're on a care plan, review what the site did in its first quarter and ask then.

Don't ask in the middle of a delay or a scope disagreement. Fix the problem first.

## What to say: referral scripts

Keep each ask short, specific and easy to act on. Edit these to sound like you.

**Launch day:**

> "The site's live. Thank you for trusting us with it. One ask while it's fresh: is there one business owner you know whose site is overdue for this? If you're open to it, I'll draft a two-line intro email you can forward."

**After the first result:**

> "The new booking form brought in its first five appointments this week, which is the part we're proudest of. If a peer ever asks who built it, we'd love the intro. Any project that signs from it gets you a free month of care plan."

**The intro email you draft for them:**

> "Subject: Intro, [your name] and [their contact]. [Contact], meet [your name], whose studio rebuilt our site this spring. Since launch, [one result]. [Your name], [contact] runs [business] and mentioned the site needs work. I'll let you two take it from here."

When the intro lands, reply the same day and book the call that week. [How to sell web design services](/blog/how-to-sell-web-design-services) covers the steps from that call to the deposit.

## How to set up referral partnerships with agencies

Agency partners can send work for years, so they deserve a real pitch and written terms. Start with agencies whose clients look like yours and whose services you don't sell: SEO, branding, paid ads, copywriting, hosting and IT support. Pitch a promise about their clients, not a request for leads:

> "We build Webflow sites for B2B software companies and don't do SEO. Your audits probably turn up sites that need a rebuild. Would it help to have a builder you can send those to? Every SEO question our clients ask would go to you."

Then put the deal on one page:

- **What counts as a referral:** a named introduction to a company that isn't already in your pipeline.
- **The reward:** the fee, what it's calculated on, and when it's paid, such as within 14 days of each client payment.
- **The term:** for example, projects signed within 12 months of the intro.
- **Client ownership:** who keeps the relationship, and a promise that neither side pitches the other's clients.
- **Refunds and cancellations:** what happens to the fee if the project stops.
- **Updates:** how and when you report status back.

If the agency wants you to build under its brand instead, that's a different deal: see [white-label web design partnerships](/blog/white-label-web-design-partners).

## Paperwork: taxes and disclosure

Two US rules touch referral payments. This is general information, not tax or legal advice.

**Form 1099-NEC.** If you pay a referral partner who isn't your employee $2,000 or more in 2026, you generally file Form 1099-NEC by January 31 of the next year; payments to corporations are usually exempt. The [IRS instructions for Form 1099-NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec) list referral fees paid by one professional to another as reportable, and the [reporting threshold rose from $600 to $2,000](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/am-i-required-to-file-a-form-1099-or-other-information-return) for payments made in 2026.

**Rewards and reviews.** Keep referral rewards apart from review requests. The [FTC's endorsement guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) says a business can't make an incentive depend on a review being positive, and that people who get something in return for a review should disclose it.

## Track your web design referral program in one sheet

Give each intro a row with these columns:

- date of the intro, referrer, company and contact;
- status: call booked, proposal sent, won, lost, not now;
- project value and the fee owed;
- date the fee was paid, and date you sent the thank-you.

Review the sheet monthly to see which partners send work and which only promised to. Thank every referrer within a day of the intro, whether or not the project happens, and tell them how it ended.
