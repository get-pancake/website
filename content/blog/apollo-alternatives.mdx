**TL;DR:** Most Apollo alternatives replace one piece of Apollo, so name the piece you want gone before you compare. ZoomInfo sells a bigger database on contract, Lusha sells direct dials with a free plan, and Snov.io is the cheapest all-in-one at $29.25 a month billed yearly with unlimited seats. FullEnrich finds mobile numbers, Instantly sends from many mailboxes, and Clay lets you design your own enrichment. If you would rather not run a prospecting tool at all, Pancake, our product, finds people showing a buying signal and starts the conversation under your name, from $99 a month per workspace.

Every price below was read on the vendor's own pricing page in November 2026. Apollo marks parts of [its pricing page](https://www.apollo.io/pricing) as introductory pricing that may change, so check again before you sign anything.

## What Apollo still does better than most

Apollo covers most of an outbound team's work in one login. Its database lists more than 240 million contacts and 30 million accounts. Prospecting, sequences, a dialer, deliverability tools and deal management sit in the same workspace, and Apollo says more than 600,000 companies use it.

The free plan includes 900 credits per seat per year, two sequences, one intent topic and basic filters, with no card needed to sign up. Paid seats add waterfall enrichment, six intent topics, unlimited sequences and CRM integrations. Apollo's agents research accounts, build lists and draft outreach, and Apollo Anywhere brings its data into your CRM, Gmail, Claude or ChatGPT.

For a sales team that wants search, sequences and calls under one bill, Apollo remains a sound default. Most teams that leave aren't rejecting the whole product. They hit one of the limits below.

## Where Apollo pinches: credits, seats and send caps

- **Credits go faster than they look.** An annual Basic seat gets 30,000 credits a year, and a monthly one gets 2,500 a month. An email costs 1 credit and a phone number 8, so a month of Basic credits buys about 310 phone numbers. Unused credits expire at the end of each billing cycle.
- **Every seat pays, and every seat shares one plan.** Apollo's own FAQ says you can't put some users on Professional and others on Basic. Organization needs at least three seats, on annual billing only.
- **Low send caps on the lower plans.** Free and Basic list one mailbox per user and 250 email sends a day. Professional lifts both.
- **Annual terms are hard to unwind.** Mid-cycle downgrades on annual plans aren't permitted, and downgrades bring no refund for unused time.
- **The extras are add-ons.** The Inbound package and the Advanced Dialer start at $119 per team a month, billed annually.
- **It is still a tool you operate.** Someone picks the filters, spends the credits, reviews the sequences and watches mailbox health. A founder who sells alone rarely has those hours.

## The bar each tool had to clear

Each pick had to do at least one of Apollo's jobs better or for less: data, enrichment, sending, or deciding who to contact. Each had to publish its prices or say plainly that it quotes. Each had to offer a free plan, a free trial or a demo.

Pancake is our product and comes first because it answers the question a database leaves open: who is worth contacting this week, based on [buying signals](/blog/buying-signals-b2b) rather than filters. ZoomInfo, Lusha and Snov.io follow as the closest like-for-like swaps. FullEnrich, Instantly and Clay are specialists that each replace one job. [Pancake's comparison pages](/compare) set it beside more GTM tools.

## 1. Pancake (our product): buyers and first conversations

Apollo hands you a database to work. Pancake does the prospecting and opens the conversation. It reads your website and builds a GTM Brain: what you sell, your ideal customers, personas, competitors and voice. Describe an audience in one sentence, such as "Find heads of sales at US software companies with 20 to 200 staff hiring SDRs," and it becomes a Play that runs on the schedule you pick.

Three signals suit most B2B teams: [companies hiring for a role](/plays/companies-hiring), people engaging with a competitor's posts, and startups that recently raised. Each lead arrives with one line on why it fits. The first message asks about the signal and pitches nothing: "Saw you're hiring two SDRs. Starting outbound, or scaling what works?" Messages go out under your name, from your own account, and you can edit any of them first. A reply stops the sequence.

**Pricing (November 2026):** from [$99 a month per workspace](/pricing), with 60,000 credits and free teammates. A work email costs 50 credits and a phone number 500, charged only when found. The 7-day free trial comes with 30,000 credits and asks for a card.

**How it differs from Apollo:** Apollo bills seats for data you search. Pancake bills a workspace for leads it finds and checks against your ideal customer. It sends no email, makes no calls and doesn't identify website visitors.

**Pick it if** nobody has hours to run a sales platform and you want conversations with people showing a reason to buy.

## 2. ZoomInfo: more data, sold by quote

ZoomInfo is the enterprise database Apollo gets measured against. Its homepage cites verified data on more than 500 million contacts and 100 million companies, with intent signals, org charts and AI agents for account research and meeting prep. An MCP server connects Claude, ChatGPT and custom agents to the same data.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [ZoomInfo's pricing page](https://www.zoominfo.com/pricing) lists three sales packages, Professional, Copilot Advanced and Copilot Enterprise, with no prices. You fill in a form. The page says the price depends on features, the number of licenses and credit usage, and that each export costs one credit. ZoomInfo also offers ZoomInfo Lite, a free plan with no card, and a full-platform trial that typically runs 7 days.

**How it differs from Apollo:** ZoomInfo goes deeper on company hierarchies, intent and data upkeep, and sells through a sales conversation. Apollo starts free, with its prices on the page.

**Pick it if** you run a funded team with a CRM to keep clean and a budget for a yearly contract. Teams heading the other way can read our [guide to ZoomInfo alternatives](/blog/zoominfo-alternatives).

## 3. Lusha: direct dials and a free plan

Lusha sells verified emails, phone numbers and company data through a web workspace, a Chrome extension, an API and an MCP server for Claude and ChatGPT. Every plan lists automated email sequences, the free one included. Higher plans add lookalikes, buying intent topics, signals and webhooks.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Lusha's pricing page](https://www.lusha.com/pricing/) lists a Free plan with 40 credits a month and no card. Starter costs $37.45 a month billed yearly for 4,800 credits a year, or $49.90 billed monthly. Pro costs $52.45 a month billed yearly with two seats, and Premium $299.95 with five. An email costs 1 credit and a phone number 5.

**How it differs from Apollo:** a phone number costs 5 credits instead of 8, and plans include seats: one on Starter, two on Pro, five on Premium. Apollo's sending side goes further, with A/Z testing, a dialer and a deliverability suite.

**Pick it if** you mostly need direct dials and emails for accounts you have already chosen, and your team fits a seat bundle.

## 4. Snov.io: Apollo's shape on a smaller bill

Snov.io covers most of Apollo's loop for less: B2B search, an email finder, 7-tier email verification, drip sequences with unlimited senders, warm-up, a shared inbox and a sales CRM. Every paid plan includes unlimited team seats and MCP access.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Snov.io's pricing page](https://snov.io/pricing) lists a free Trial plan that renews monthly, with 50 credits and 100 recipients. Starter costs $39 a month, or $29.25 billed yearly, for 1,000 credits and 5,000 recipients. Pro starts at $99 a month, or $74.25 billed yearly, for 5,000 credits. Unused credits roll over while you stay subscribed.

**How it differs from Apollo:** you pay per plan instead of per seat. A recipient is counted once per lead in a billing period, and follow-ups to that lead are free. Apollo's database is larger, and its dialer and intent topics don't appear in Snov.io's sales plans.

**Pick it if** you want the same find-verify-send loop for less, and you plan to add teammates.

## 5. FullEnrich: mobile numbers from 25+ sources

FullEnrich does one job. You give it names and companies, and it runs a waterfall across more than 25 sources for a verified work email, mobile number or personal email. You pay only for data it finds and verifies. People and company search, CRM integrations, an API and an MCP server are included, with unlimited users.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [FullEnrich's pricing page](https://fullenrich.com/pricing) lists a free trial with 50 credits and no card. Pro starts at $29 a month for 500 credits, or $55 for 1,000. A work email costs 1 credit and a mobile number 10. Unused credits roll over for three months on monthly plans and twelve on yearly ones.

**How it differs from Apollo:** FullEnrich has no sequences. You bring the list, from Apollo, your CRM or a CSV, and it fills in the contact details.

**Pick it if** phone numbers drive your motion and Apollo's mobiles come back thin in your market.

## 6. Instantly: sending from unlimited mailboxes

Instantly is a cold email platform first. Each outreach plan comes with as many email accounts and as much warm-up as you need. Its Lead Finder searches more than 450 million B2B leads, with work emails found through a waterfall of five or more providers. Sending and lead credits are sold as separate plans.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Instantly's pricing page](https://instantly.ai/pricing) lists Growth at $47 a month, or $37.60 billed yearly, for 1,000 uploaded contacts and 5,000 emails a month. Hypergrowth costs $107, or $85.58 billed yearly, for 25,000 contacts. Lead credits start at $47 a month on their own plan. The page says you can start without a credit card.

**How it differs from Apollo:** Apollo's Free and Basic plans allow one mailbox per user. Instantly connects as many mailboxes as you want on every plan and prices by contacts and volume instead of seats. Our [lemlist alternatives comparison](/blog/lemlist-alternatives) covers more senders.

**Pick it if** email is your main channel and you send from several domains.

## 7. Clay: enrichment you design yourself

Clay is a GTM data platform where you build the enrichment: tables and workflows that pull from more than 150 data providers, waterfalls that try them in order, and Claygent, an AI agent that researches the web. Signal tracking, ad audiences and an email sequencer sit on top.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Clay's pricing page](https://www.clay.com/pricing) lists a Free plan with 100 data credits and 500 actions a month. Launch costs $185 a month, or $167 billed annually, for 2,500 data credits and 15,000 actions. Growth, which adds CRM sync, costs $495 a month, or $446 billed annually. Launch and Growth each start with a 14-day trial.

**How it differs from Apollo:** Apollo searches its own database. Clay chains many providers and lets you write the logic, which finds more contacts and takes more skill. Our [Clay alternatives guide](/blog/clay-alternatives) covers simpler options.

**Pick it if** someone on your team likes building data workflows, and your lists need data no single database holds.

## Test two tools on 50 contacts you already know

A vendor's database size says little about your market. Coverage shifts by country, industry and seniority, and forum threads, Reddit's included, describe someone else's buyers. An hour on your own contacts tells you more.

1. Pull 50 contacts whose email and mobile you already know: recent customers, closed deals, people who replied to you.
2. Strip the contact details, then run the names and companies through two tools.
3. Count matches, misses and wrong answers, for emails and mobiles separately.
4. Price the winner at the volume you will run each month, not at the entry plan.

Emails for 50 people fit inside most free allowances: Apollo's 75 credits a month, FullEnrich's 50 trial credits, Snov.io's 50. Mobile numbers cost 5 to 10 credits each on Lusha, Apollo and FullEnrich, so test phones on one paid month.
