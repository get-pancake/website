**TL;DR:** This guide covers Clay, the GTM data and enrichment platform, not the modeling material. If you want Clay's tables for less, Databar is the closest swap, starting at $99 a month, and Bitscale trades Clay's two meters for one. If you want less building, Apollo puts a database, waterfall enrichment and sequences in one login, Origami builds lists from a prompt, and FullEnrich runs only the email and phone waterfall. Pancake, our product, isn't a spreadsheet you build: you describe who to reach in one sentence, and it finds people showing a buying signal and starts the conversation, from $99 a month per workspace.

## Where Clay is still the best tool

Be clear about what you would give up. [Clay's pricing page](https://www.clay.com/pricing) counts more than 150 data partners, and its homepage more than 200 data and AI vendors, all bought through one contract. No tool on this list matches that breadth.

Every plan, the free one included, runs multi-provider waterfalls and Claygent, Clay's AI agent for web research. You can bring your own API keys and skip Clay's data credits. An enrichment that returns nothing costs no data credits and no actions, and unused data credits roll over up to twice the monthly amount on Launch and Growth.

Growth adds CRM auto-sync, data warehouse connections, HTTP APIs, webhooks, web intent signals and unlimited ad audiences. You can build in tables or on a visual canvas, with clicks, code or chat. Clay says more than 500,000 GTM teams use it. If someone on your team designs data workflows for a living, Clay remains the strongest place to do it.

## Why teams shop around: two meters and a builder's job

- **Two meters to watch.** Data credits pay for data, and actions pay for each enrichment run, AI call or export. Actions reset each billing cycle and don't roll over.
- **The jump from free to paid.** Phone number enrichment and job change signals start on Launch, at $185 a month. CRM auto-sync starts on Growth, at $495 a month.
- **Variable AI costs.** Token-heavy models such as GPT-5.1 and Claude 4.6 Sonnet bill on what each run consumes, at cost, so a research column shows an estimate until it finishes.
- **Somebody has to build it.** Clay's homepage says GTM engineers build on Clay, and that is the honest staffing plan: tables, waterfalls, prompts and scoring need an owner. Our guide to the [GTM engineer role](/blog/gtm-engineer) covers what that person does.
- **The output is a list.** Clay can trigger emails through its sequencer or integrations, but spotting the [buying signals](/blog/buying-signals-b2b) worth acting on and starting each conversation remain your design work.

## Who made this list

Each tool had to replace at least one job people buy Clay for: building lists, enriching contacts, researching accounts, or turning data into outreach. Each had to publish its prices; a recommendation in a Reddit thread didn't count without them. Each had to offer a free plan or a trial.

Pancake is our product and comes first, for teams that bought Clay to reach buyers rather than to build data systems. Databar and Bitscale are the closest like-for-like tables. Apollo, Origami and FullEnrich each replace part of Clay with less to build. [Pancake's comparison pages](/compare) cover more tools.

## 1. Pancake (our product): Plays instead of tables

Clay sells the parts of a prospecting system. Pancake is one finished system: it finds people showing a buying signal and starts the conversation under your name. It isn't a spreadsheet you build columns in. Describe an audience in one sentence, such as "Find RevOps leaders at US software companies with 50 to 500 staff whose job posts name Salesforce," and it becomes a Play that runs on the schedule you pick.

Behind each Play, 30+ data providers come included, with no API keys or data contracts. Plays find [companies by the tools their job posts name](/plays/tech-stack), companies hiring, recently funded startups and people engaging with competitors' posts. Each lead comes with one line on why it fits your GTM Brain. Pancake finds a work email for 50 credits or a phone number for 500, charged only when found, and writes the first message from the signal: "Saw your RevOps opening lists Salesforce. Cleaning up the current setup, or rebuilding it?" The same work runs from [Claude](/claude), ChatGPT or Codex over MCP.

**Pricing (November 2026):** from [$99 a month per workspace](/pricing) with 60,000 credits. The 7-day trial includes 30,000 credits, and starting it takes a card.

**How it differs from Clay:** Clay lets you design every step and bills two meters; Pancake runs one job on one kind of credit. It sends no email, and leads leave for your CRM as a one-way export.

**Pick it if** you came to Clay for leads and conversations, not to maintain workflows.

## 2. Databar: the closest table-for-table swap

Databar works the way Clay does: tables where each column calls a data provider, an AI model or your own API. It connects 100+ data providers, runs waterfall enrichments, and adds an AI web researcher agent, formulas, a Chrome extension, an MCP server and a CLI. It bills a single meter, credits, and charges only for requests that return data.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Databar's pricing page](https://databar.ai/pricing) lists a Free plan with 150 credits and one editor. Build starts at $99 a month, or $87 billed annually, for 5,000 credits a month and three editors. Scale costs $495 a month, or $436 billed annually, for 50,000 credits and ten editors. The paid features can be tried free for 14 days without a card, and unused credits roll over for one billing period.

**How it differs from Clay:** one meter instead of two, and your own API keys cost nothing on any plan, where Clay still counts actions for them. Clay includes unlimited seats, while Databar's Free, Build and Scale plans cap editors at one, three and ten, and Clay's signal tracking and ad audiences don't appear on Databar's plan list.

**Pick it if** you like working in tables and want Clay's core loop for less.

## 3. Bitscale: a grid with unlimited actions

Bitscale is another enrichment grid, built around CRM data: 100+ data sources on paid plans, intent enrichments, a two-way HubSpot integration, webhooks, HTTP APIs and inbound integrations such as RB2B. Credits are spent only when a provider returns a valid result. Failed lookups, formula columns and CRM syncs cost nothing, and paid plans include unlimited actions.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Bitscale's pricing page](https://bitscale.ai/pricing) lists a lifetime Free plan with 200 credits and no card. Growth costs $349 a month, or $314 billed annually, for 15,000 credits a month. Booster costs $799, or $719 billed annually, for 50,000. Enterprise starts at $15,000 a year. Paid plans roll unused credits over, up to three times the plan amount.

**How it differs from Clay:** paid plans have no actions meter, so one number tells you what you can run. The entry price is higher than Clay's Launch, and the Salesforce integration starts on Booster.

**Pick it if** your team runs on HubSpot and wants enrichment costs it can forecast.

## 4. Apollo: database and waterfall in one login

Apollo suits teams that used Clay mostly to find contacts. It searches its own database of more than 240 million contacts and 30 million accounts, fills gaps with waterfall enrichment for CSVs, CRM records and API calls, and runs sequences and calls from the same login.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Apollo's pricing page](https://www.apollo.io/pricing) lists a Free plan with 900 credits per seat per year. Basic costs $49 per seat a month billed annually, or $65 billed monthly, with 30,000 credits per seat a year on annual billing. An email costs 1 credit and a phone number 8, and credits don't carry over to the next cycle.

**How it differs from Clay:** Apollo starts from one database and fills the gaps; Clay starts from whichever providers you chain. Apollo bills per seat and has fewer moving parts. Our [Apollo alternatives guide](/blog/apollo-alternatives) covers where it runs short.

**Pick it if** you want contacts and sending in one tool, and Clay's flexibility went unused.

## 5. Origami: an agent that builds the list

Origami swaps the table for an agent. You enter your website, and it works out your best customers, finds people who need what you sell now, writes to each of them and sends the replies to your inbox. Contact data runs through waterfalls of five email providers and nine phone providers at cost, and every paid plan includes a sequencer for email and social cadences.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Origami's pricing page](https://origami.chat/pricing) shows plans starting at $99 a month for Starter, with $165 of usage included, then Pro at $199 with $330 and Max at $399 with $660. A verified email costs $0.066 and a verified phone $0.33, charged only when found, and talking to the agent is free. New organizations can try Starter for 7 days with $66 of usage, and a card is required.

**How it differs from Clay:** you describe the list in plain words instead of building it column by column, and Origami also runs the outreach. Clay gives you more control over each data point. Our [Origami alternatives guide](/blog/origami-alternatives) compares it with similar agents.

**Pick it if** you want lists built from a description, local businesses included, with outreach in the same place.

## 6. FullEnrich: the contact waterfall on its own

If most of your Clay credits go to email and phone lookups, FullEnrich sells that one job. It tries more than 25 sources in order, verifies what it finds and charges only for verified results. It covers work emails, personal emails, mobile numbers and reverse email lookups, with unlimited users, an API, an MCP server and a HubSpot integration.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [FullEnrich's pricing page](https://fullenrich.com/pricing) offers 50 free trial credits without a card. Pro starts at $29 a month for 500 credits and costs $104 for 2,000. A work email costs 1 credit, a mobile number 10 and a personal email 3.

**How it differs from Clay:** no tables, AI research or signals. A list goes in, verified contacts come out.

**Pick it if** contact data is the only Clay job you would miss.

A worked example: 1,000 work emails and 100 mobile numbers a month. Apollo covers it inside one Basic seat, at about 1,800 of its 2,500 monthly credits. FullEnrich needs 2,000 credits, which costs $104 a month. Origami spends about $66 on the emails and $33 on the phones, inside the $165 of usage that Starter includes. The same lookups would take 100,000 Pancake credits, more than the entry plan holds, so for enrichment alone a contact-data tool costs less.

## Before you cancel Clay

1. Export every table you still use as a CSV, enriched columns included.
2. Note which provider each enrichment column calls, and whether it runs on Clay's credits or your own API key. Keys you own move with you, and Databar connects them free on every plan.
3. Copy the Claygent prompts and scoring rules you rely on into a doc.
4. Plan around your balance. Data credits roll over up to twice the monthly amount on Launch and Growth, but actions reset each cycle.
5. Rebuild the workflow closest to revenue first, and run old and new side by side for two weeks before you move the rest.
