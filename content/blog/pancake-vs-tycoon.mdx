Tycoon and Pancake make the same bet: a small team with AI agents can get more done than its headcount suggests. Tycoon applies that bet to the whole company. Pancake applies it to one job, bringing in customers.

The machines underneath are different.

**TL;DR:** Tycoon gives you an AI manager, Tycoon Agent, that splits your goals into tasks and hands them to AI agents for coding, content, ads, research and more. You pay for usage from a wallet. [Pancake](/) is an AI GTM team for B2B companies: it finds people showing buying signals, runs outreach from your own account and writes articles for search, for a flat monthly price. Pick Tycoon for breadth. Pick Pancake if customers are the bottleneck.

---

## What Tycoon is

Tycoon (tycoon.us) grew out of an experiment by founder Xiaoyin Qu. In April 2025 an AI CEO named Astra replaced her as CEO of HeyBoss, and Tycoon packages that setup for other founders. As of September 2026, its homepage calls the manager Tycoon Agent.

You share an idea or a goal, and Tycoon Agent breaks it into tasks. It assigns them to agents from a roster Tycoon has trained: software delivery, a head of content for SEO posts and landing pages, a campaign manager for ads and cold email, plus research, metrics, contract review and SEO. You can also bring your own agents, such as Claude Code, or build a custom one.

You can start a new company or point Tycoon at an existing one by importing GitHub and social media accounts. You talk to Tycoon Agent through iMessage, Slack, Discord or the web app.

Tycoon Agent lets low-risk, reversible work move forward on its own. It escalates decisions on strategy, public publishing, customer communication, spend, production changes and legal risk.

Pricing is usage-based. Billing runs on two meters: model tokens at OpenRouter prices, and machine-hours while agents run. The homepage lists $50 a month, a $5 welcome credit and no per-seat fee.

Tycoon isn't built for one kind of company. Its site has pages for SaaS founders, agencies, consultants, e-commerce owners and creators. You set the goals. Tycoon Agent manages the agents and brings the important calls back to you.

## What Pancake is

Pancake starts from your website. It reads the site and builds its GTM Brain, a shared memory of who buys from you, what you offer, your proof and the objections you hear. The agents keep learning from what works.

Every morning you get new leads. Each one comes from a signal Pancake watches: a post on a topic you chose, engagement with a competitor or an influencer, engagement with your own posts, a matching job opening, or a job post that names a tool you replace. Each lead says which signal picked it.

You approve leads in the app or in Slack. Outreach runs from your own account on the professional social network: a profile visit, a like, an invite with no note, then up to three messages. Pancake also writes articles for Google and AI answers, and those wait for your approval too.

The price is [$99/month flat](/pricing), every agent included, with no seats and no usage meter.

| Best for | Founders who want to manage many agents like staff | B2B founders who need more buyer conversations |

## When Tycoon is the better choice

Tycoon fits if you want one place to run many kinds of work. A founder who needs code shipped, a launch page written, ads tested and a competitor teardown done in the same week can route all of it through one manager.

It also fits if you already work with agents. You can import your Claude Code or Codex setup, including CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md and skills, and keep paying for those subscriptions directly.

Usage billing suits uneven workloads. Idle agents cost nothing, and a quiet month costs less than a busy one.

---

## When Pancake is the better choice

Pancake fits if your product works and the problem is pipeline. You don't need a manager for dozens of agents. You need people who might buy, found every morning, and a first message that makes sense to them.

The price is fixed at $99 a month, so you know the bill before the month starts.

It is not limited to software, either. The signals work the same way for a SaaS startup, an agency or a [consultant](/for/consultants) selling to other businesses.

The other difference is breadth. Tycoon can take on your product, marketing and research at once. Pancake covers finding buyers and writing articles for search, and leaves the rest of the company to you and the tools you already use.
