**TL;DR:** Sell to one function, on the retail calendar. E-commerce, merchandising, store operations, supply chain and insights each hold a budget and buy different software, so name the function before you name the company. Retailers on the NRF's 4-5-4 calendar start their fiscal year around February and make much of their year at the holidays, so plan pilots for spring and keep go-lives clear of the holiday weeks.

Most guides on how to sell software to retailers treat "the retailer" as one buyer. It is at least five, and a brand selling through retailers is a different customer again. If you're earlier than this, start with [the first-customers playbook for B2B SaaS](/blog/how-to-get-first-customers-b2b-saas); [our retail tech startups page](/for/retail-tech-startups) shows the signals below as example Plays.

## Retailers and brands are different customers

A retailer sells many brands' products through stores and online. A brand makes products and sells them through retailers, its own site, or both. They share the holiday calendar, not the buying process.

- **Retailers** buy for e-commerce, merchandising, store operations, supply chain, loss prevention and IT.
- **Brands** buy for direct-to-consumer e-commerce, brand marketing, consumer insights, retail account teams and supply chain.

Stores still take most of the money. E-commerce made up 17.1% of US retail sales in the second quarter of 2026, by the [Census Bureau's quarterly estimate](https://www.census.gov/retail/ecommerce.html), and grew 12.2% from a year earlier while total retail grew 6.7%. For a retail tech startup, that puts most of the money in stores and most of the growth online.

## How to sell software to retailers, function by function

Each function owns a metric, and the product that moves that metric gets the meeting. Here is who buys what:

| Function | Typical buyer | What they buy | Measured on | Signal to watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E-commerce and digital | VP E-commerce, head of digital | Site search, personalization, product content, reviews | Conversion, revenue per visit | Platform named in job posts, e-commerce hiring |
| Merchandising and planning | VP Merchandising, planning director | Assortment, pricing, allocation, forecasting | Sell-through, margin, inventory turns | Planner and analyst openings |
| Store operations | VP Stores, retail operations director | Scheduling, task management, store communications | Labor cost, store sales, compliance | Store manager hiring in new markets |
| Supply chain and fulfillment | VP Supply Chain | Order management, warehouse, returns | Cost per order, on-time delivery | Fulfillment and returns roles |
| Insights and CX | Head of Insights, CX director | Research, surveys, feedback analysis | Satisfaction, speed of research | Insights manager openings |

IT approves every integration with the POS, the ERP and the e-commerce platform, so bring IT in before the pilot, not after it.

### A "retail buyer" usually buys products, not software

In retail, a buyer is a merchant who picks the products the stores will carry. If you searched for how to sell to retail buyers, that person decides on product, not software, though they work in planning and assortment tools every week. Software budgets sit with the functions in the table. A merchant can still champion a planning tool, because they feel its gaps first.

## The retail calendar sets the sales cycle

Retailers run on a calendar built around comparable weeks and the holidays. [The NRF's 4-5-4 calendar](https://nrf.com/resources/4-5-4-calendar) splits the year into months of four, five and four weeks so like days compare with like days, and lines up holidays, which the NRF notes account for a significant share of retailers' sales.

Three things follow:

- **A fiscal year that starts around February.** New budgets arrive early in the calendar year, which makes spring the natural time to start pilots.
- **A quiet November and December.** Store and e-commerce teams are running the peak. Don't plan a go-live or a demo push for those weeks.
- **Summer decisions.** A pilot that reports by midsummer can win a rollout decision before the holiday weeks.

Say a retailer's fiscal year starts in early February. A pilot in 20 stores from April to June gives results by July, in time for a rollout plan before the holidays and a line in next year's budget.

## Store pilots: test stores, control stores, then the chain

A store product proves itself in a handful of stores against stores that didn't get it. Run the pilot so the result survives a skeptical CFO:

1. **Pick test and control stores.** Match them on size, region and sales so the comparison holds.
2. **Fix the metric and window up front.** Labor hours per store, task completion, shrink or conversion, measured over set weeks.
3. **Train store managers before go-live.** If store managers don't use it, the pilot measures nothing.
4. **Report in the sponsor's metric.** A weekly line on the number they answer for.
5. **Write the rollout path into the agreement.** Region next, then the chain, with the per-store price agreed during the pilot.

For an e-commerce product, the pilot is an A/B test on a share of site traffic, with the same rule: agree on the metric before the test starts.

## Security and payment reviews

Retail IT reviews every integration and every script that touches shoppers. If your product runs a script on a retailer's payment page, PCI DSS requirements 6.4.3 and 11.6.1 apply. The PCI Security Standards Council added them to curb e-skimming: they require payment page scripts to be authorized, checked for integrity and monitored for tampering, and they [took effect on March 31, 2025](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/coffee-with-the-council-podcast-guidance-for-pci-dss-e-commerce-requirements-effective-after-31-march-2025).

Expect the retailer to ask for your script inventory and change process, alongside the usual questions: what shopper data you collect, where it is stored, who can access it, and how your product connects to their POS, ERP and e-commerce platform.

## Signals that a retailer or brand is about to buy

Retail teams hire for their gaps in public, and their job posts often name the platforms they run.

- **Function hiring.** E-commerce managers, product data or PIM specialists, merchandise planners, insights managers. Count the brand's own posts and skip agency listings.
- **The platform named in job posts.** Shopify Plus, Salesforce Commerce Cloud or Adobe Commerce; PIMs such as Salsify, Akeneo or Syndigo. If you plug into one, those companies are your list, and [tech stack signals](/blog/tech-stack-signals) explains what a job post can prove about a stack.
- **New stores.** A batch of store manager and associate openings in a city where the chain had no store. Watch for these by hand, along with store opening announcements.
- **Funding.** Consumer and direct-to-consumer brands that raised are adding channels, and tools with them.
- **Posts about the problem.** E-commerce leads writing about return costs, listing errors or inventory accuracy.
- **Engagement with your own posts.** Insights and e-commerce people who react to or comment on your company's or founder's posts.
- **Lookalikes.** Brands or chains with the category, size and platform of your best customer.

[The guide to B2B buying signals](/blog/buying-signals-b2b) explains how to weigh these, including the ones you can only track by hand.

## Where retail and brand teams meet vendors

Retail leaders still meet vendors in person, and small merchants find them in app stores.

- **NRF's Big Show.** [NRF 2027: Retail's Big Show](https://nrfbigshow.nrf.com/) runs January 10 to 12, 2027, at the Javits Center in New York. Book meetings before you arrive.
- **Category shows.** Shoptalk for e-commerce leaders and Groceryshop for grocery are where those teams compare tools.
- **The Shopify App Store.** If your buyers are Shopify merchants, the store is a channel in itself. As of November 2026, [Shopify's revenue share](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share) lets developers below its size limits keep all of their first $1,000,000 in lifetime gross app revenue and pay 15% above that, plus a 2.9% processing fee, after a one-time $19 registration.
- **Agencies and integrators.** The firms that build and run retailers' e-commerce sites recommend tools in every build.
