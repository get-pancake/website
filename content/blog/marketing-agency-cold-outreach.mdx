**TL;DR:** Marketing leaders read your cold message as a sample of your marketing. Write each one from a reason they'd recognize: a demand-gen job post, a funding round, a launch, a founder asking for agency picks. Open with the reason, ask a single question, and end the message there. Follow up twice, adding something new each time. For email, follow CAN-SPAM: truthful headers and subject lines, a postal address, and an opt-out you honor within 10 business days.

Marketing agency cold outreach has a handicap other sellers don't: your buyer does this for a living. Where to find the companies worth writing to is covered in [lead generation for marketing agencies](/blog/lead-generation-for-marketing-agencies), and the other client channels are ranked in [how to get clients for a marketing agency](/blog/how-to-get-clients-for-marketing-agency).

## Why marketing leaders are the hardest inbox for an agency

Your buyer writes subject lines and ad copy for a living and reads yours the same way:

- **The subject line is your headline.** Vague loses, and clever loses faster.
- **The first line is your hook.** "Hope you're well" spends it on nothing.
- **The question is your call to action.** "Open to a quick call?" asks for 30 minutes and offers nothing back.

If your opener wouldn't pass your own client review, don't send it.

## Match the opener to the reason

Each reason below earns one kind of question, and each has a line that kills it.

| Reason you saw | Ask about | Never say |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring a demand-gen manager or head of growth | Which work the hire will own | "We can do that job for less" |
| A funding round | Which channel has to grow first | "Ready to scale?" |
| A product launch | How they'll judge the launch | "Your launch could have gone better" |
| A post about a missed pipeline target | What they've ruled out | "We fix pipeline" |
| A rebrand | Where buyers will see the change first | "Love the new look" and nothing else |
| A request for agency picks | What the agency would own first | A pasted credentials deck |
| Comments on a rival agency's post | The topic itself | The rival's name |
| A job post that mentions managing agencies | What a good agency month looks like | "Fire your agency" |
| A former client contact in a new job | What they inherited | A generic catch-up |
| A partner's introduction | A meeting, directly | A pitch that ignores the intro |

## Marketing agency cold outreach: ten openers, written out

Each opener is an email: a subject line, two lines, then your name and agency. For a direct message, drop the subject and the sign-off and send it where they posted or commented. Openers 1 to 7 start from signals you can search for, by hand or with [Pancake for marketing agencies](/for/marketing-agencies). Opener 8 comes from reading a job post in full, and 9 and 10 from your own network.

### 1. They're hiring a demand-gen manager

**Subject:** the demand-gen role

> Hi [Name], saw the demand-gen manager opening at [Company]. Which channels will that person own, and which stay with outside help?

The answer tells you whether to pitch the work before the hire starts or support after. [Companies hiring](/plays/companies-hiring) shows the search side.

### 2. They closed a round

**Subject:** after the Series A

> Hi [Name], congrats on the Series A. Which channel has to carry the new pipeline target first: paid search, paid social or content?

Write while the plan is still on the whiteboard. [Selling to recently funded startups](/plays/recently-funded-startups) covers timing.

### 3. They launched a product

**Subject:** the [product] launch

> Hi [Name], saw [product] went live on Tuesday. Ninety days from now, what number tells you the launch worked?

### 4. They posted about a missed target

**Subject:** your post on Q3 pipeline

> Hi [Name], read your post about Q3 pipeline coming in short. What have you already ruled out as the cause?

A public miss draws unsolicited advice. A question about what they've tried stands apart.

### 5. They announced a rebrand

**Subject:** the new [Company] brand

> Hi [Name], saw the new brand go live. Where will buyers notice the change first: the site, the ads or the sales deck?

### 6. They asked for agency recommendations

**Subject:** your agency question

> Hi [Name], saw your post asking for a [B2B paid social] agency. We run [paid social] for [two companies like theirs]. What would the agency own in its first 90 days?

They asked, so one line of fit belongs here. Save the case study link for your second message.

### 7. They commented on a rival agency's post

**Subject:** [the topic, such as paid social costs]

> Hi [Name], you weighed in on a thread about rising paid social costs last week. Is acquisition cost the number you're watching at [Company] right now?

Don't name the rival. The topic is what they care about; the thread was only where they said it.

### 8. Their job post mentions agency partners

**Subject:** your agency setup

> Hi [Name], your [role] posting mentions managing agency partners. What does a good month with an agency look like for your team?

### 9. A former client contact started a new job

**Subject:** congrats on [Company]

> Hi [Name], congrats on the move to [Company]. What did you inherit on the marketing side that you'd change first?

### 10. A partner introduced you

**Subject:** [Partner] suggested we talk

> Hi [Name], [Partner] told me you're weighing outside help on [channel]. Would 20 minutes on Thursday or Friday be useful?

The introduction is the reason, which makes this the one opener that can ask for time.

## Two follow-ups, then stop

Reply in the same thread, add something new each time, and stop after the second.

**Follow-up 1, about four business days later:**

> Hi [Name], one data point in case it helps: [a result for a similar company, in one line, with a number that client cleared you to share]. Does that match what you're seeing?

**Follow-up 2, about a week after that:**

> Hi [Name], last note from me. Want me to send [the one-page plan] when [the hire starts / the quarter closes], or should I stay out of your inbox?

A "no" ends it: thank them and move on. A "not now" gets a date in your CRM.

## What marketing leaders delete on sight

- **Result promises.** "We'll 3x your ROAS" says you either don't know their numbers or don't mind guessing.
- **The unrequested audit.** A 20-slide teardown from a stranger asks for an hour and implies their team got it wrong.
- **"Are you the right person?"** Asking them to route you is asking for a favor. Do the research.
- **The service menu.** Twelve services in one email says you have no specialty.
- **Praise with no detail.** "Love what you're building" fits every company, so it means nothing to this one.
- **A calendar link in message one.** It asks for a meeting before they've said a word.

## Cold email for a marketing agency: the legal and delivery rules

CAN-SPAM covers your cold email even when the recipient is a business: "The law makes no exception for business-to-business email," in the words of the [FTC's CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business). Before an email goes out, check five things, as of November 2026:

1. The From, To and Reply-To lines name you and your agency.
2. The subject line matches what the message says.
3. The message is clearly and conspicuously identified as an ad; the FTC leaves the wording to you.
4. It carries a valid physical postal address.
5. It explains how to opt out, the opt-out works for at least 30 days after you send, and you act on requests within 10 business days.

Each email that breaks the rules can cost up to $53,088, the FTC says. If you run outreach for clients, the company whose product the email promotes and the company that sends it can both be held legally responsible, so write who handles opt-outs into the client contract.

Delivery is its own gate. Google's [sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) require everyone who sends to Gmail accounts to set up SPF or DKIM and to keep the spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%. Above 5,000 messages a day, they add DMARC, both SPF and DKIM, and one-click unsubscribe for marketing messages. The spam-rate limit applies at any volume.

Prospects outside the US fall under other rules, such as the GDPR and national e-privacy laws in the EU; read the regulator's guidance for each country first. None of this is legal advice.

## Email or DM: one channel per person

- **They post or comment.** A direct message that refers to the post reads as a reply, not a pitch. Two lines, no link.
- **They never post.** Email their work address, with a subject line from the openers above.
- **Never both on the same day.** The same opener in two inboxes at once reads like automation.

Send under your own name either way.
