**TL;DR:** Companies that have already chosen Webflow and have work waiting are the fastest clients to win: a job post for a Webflow developer, or a marketing role that names Webflow, means a budget and a deadline. The Webflow Partner Program comes next. Its Certified tier lists you in the directory and in client matching, but only after three client sites, so it rewards clients you win elsewhere. Templates, the community and other Webflow studios build your name more slowly.

This is the Webflow chapter of our [guide to getting web design clients](/blog/how-to-get-web-design-clients): how to get Webflow clients through the channels that exist because you build in Webflow. Every program term below comes from Webflow's own pages, read on November 3, 2026.

## How to get Webflow clients: channels ranked by speed

Job posts come first, the partner directory second, and the channels that compound last. The speed column is our judgment for a studio that already has a portfolio.

| Channel | What it takes | Who you meet | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Companies hiring a Webflow developer | Fresh job posts and a short first message | Heads of marketing with a budget and a deadline | Fast |
| Other roles that name Webflow | The same search, read past the job title | Teams already on Webflow who need help with the hard parts | Fast |
| Companies leaving WordPress | Job posts naming WordPress, posts about a rebuild | Marketing teams planning a migration | Medium |
| Certified Partner directory and matching | Three client sites, an assessment, a portfolio review | Buyers who chose Webflow and want vetted help | Slow to qualify |
| Referrals from other Webflow studios | A public list of what you take and refuse | Overflow and projects outside their skills | Slow |
| Templates and Made in Webflow | Finished templates or cloneables you maintain | DIY buyers, a few who want custom work | Slowest |

## Find companies hiring a Webflow developer

A company that posts a Webflow job has approved a budget and has web work it can't staff today. The person who owns that work is usually the head of marketing or the web lead. The recruiter who posted the job is the wrong person to write to. Hiring is also the first signal on [our page for web design agencies](/for/web-design-agencies).

Search job boards for "Webflow" and read each post in full. Three kinds of posts matter:

- **"Webflow Developer" or "Webflow Designer."** The site is a priority now.
- **Marketing roles that list Webflow as a skill**, such as a content or growth manager. The team edits the site itself and needs a partner for builds, custom code and migrations.
- **Contract or part-time Webflow roles.** The company wants project help, and a studio with a team can cover more than one freelancer.

### Win the work even if they hire in-house

Some companies will hire. The post still tells you the site matters now, and a developer hire takes weeks to close. Offer one of three roles:

1. **The bridge.** Ship the pages that can't wait, like a launch page or the pricing page, while the search runs.
2. **The foundation.** Build the component library, class naming and CMS structure the new hire inherits, so they start on a clean build.
3. **The bench.** Sell a small monthly retainer for overflow once the hire starts: launches, migrations, custom code.

Your first message should pitch none of these. Ask one question about the post: "Saw the Webflow developer role. Is it for a rebuild, or for running the current site?" The answer tells you which role to offer.

## The Webflow Partner Program, tier by tier

The [Webflow Partner Program](https://webflow.com/certified-partners/apply) has three tiers, and only the upper two put you in front of buyers. As of November 2026:

| Tier | How you get there | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | Join in minutes as a freelancer or agency | 10% commission on Site plans and add-ons, partner resources, the private partner community |
| Certified | Active Freelancer or Agency Workspace plan, a pre-qualification assessment, three new client sites built after joining Foundations, a portfolio review | 20% commission, a public profile, a directory listing, client matching |
| Premium | Keep delivering client projects in Webflow as a Certified Partner | Preferential placement in the directory and in matching |

Commission runs for the first 12 months of each subscription, and Enterprise plans pay 10% of contract value on every tier. Webflow says most declined applications come down to an inactive Workspace plan or client sites below its quality bar, judged with a site grading rubric.

Read the Certified requirements in order: the three client sites must be built after you join Foundations. So join Foundations before your next project starts. A site you finished last month won't count.

## How buyers use the Webflow experts directory

Searchers still call it the Webflow experts directory. Webflow now calls it the [Certified Partner directory](https://webflow.com/certified-partners/browse), and its matching form still runs on the experts.webflow.com domain. Buyers filter before they read a profile:

- **Budget**, from under $1,000 to $100,000 and up.
- **Location and language.**
- **Partner type** (Premium or Enterprise only) and **business type** (freelancer or agency).
- **Services offered**, from Web Development and Platform migrations to Animation and SEO Audit & Optimization.

Three habits follow. Set a starting budget that matches the clients you want, because each card shows "Starting at" next to your name. Tick the three or four services you want to be hired for, not every box. And keep your "Accepting new projects" status true. Some cards also carry a "Highly responsive" badge, so when a match arrives, reply the same day with one question about the brief.

## Companies moving from WordPress to Webflow

A migration is one of the clearest Webflow pitches. The buyer is a marketing team that wants to publish pages without waiting on a developer. Three signs point to one:

- a B2B company whose marketing or content job posts name WordPress;
- a marketer posting about a site rebuild or a slow publishing process;
- a new head of marketing, who will look at the site with fresh eyes.

A job post naming WordPress proves the team uses WordPress somewhere, not that it wants to leave. Check the site by hand before you write: WordPress sites load files from a /wp-content/ path you can see in the page source. Then ask about the pain, not the platform: "How long does it take your team to ship a new landing page today?"

[WordPress studios pitch the same companies](/blog/how-to-get-wordpress-clients) from the other side, so lead with the team's problem. If you build in Framer too, the [Framer client playbook](/blog/how-to-get-framer-clients) covers the startup buyers that platform attracts.

## Templates, Made in Webflow and the community

These channels build your name slowly. They work as proof more than as a pipeline.

- **Templates.** Creators in the Template Creator Program [keep 95% of paid template sales](https://webflow.com/updates/template-creator-enhancements) on the Webflow Marketplace, and fulfillment links let you sell a template from your own site. A buyer who needs a custom section can turn into a small paid project. Expect fixes more often than rebuilds.
- **Made in Webflow.** The showcase takes published sites and cloneable projects. A cloneable that solves a hard interaction shows other builders, and the odd marketer, what you can do.
- **The community.** Webflow counts [300,000+ members in its community](https://webflow.com/community), plus Network Groups that meet in person and online. Treat it as a referral network more than a client list: studios that only design, or that are booked solid, pass on what they can't take. Post a short note on what you take and what you refuse, and send work back when it fits someone else.
