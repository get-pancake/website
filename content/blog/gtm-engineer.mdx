## What a GTM engineer is, and where the title came from

A GTM engineer is an operator who writes software for the revenue team. A RevOps admin configures tools; a GTM engineer builds systems across them, such as a pipeline that turns a demo request into an enriched, scored CRM record, or an agent that briefs a rep before every first call.

Clay, the data-enrichment tool, [says it coined the title in 2023](https://www.clay.com/blog/gtm-engineering), and it runs a job board for the role. Posts with the title now come from AI companies, cloud infrastructure startups and restaurant software makers alike.

Definitions still vary by company. Vercel's post asks for a software engineer with four years of full-stack experience. Baseten's asks for someone who has pushed Clay to its limits and owns changes in Salesforce. Same title, two different hires.

## Six systems GTM engineers build

Six systems keep coming up in the job posts we read. Each replaces hours of manual work with something that runs on its own.

1. **Enrichment pipelines.** Every new signup, demo request or event list comes back as a complete, scored CRM record. Sierra's post describes owning this end to end across Clay, ZoomInfo, Cognism and LLM-powered sources. [Lead enrichment, explained](/blog/lead-enrichment) covers the waterfall logic underneath.
2. **Fit scoring.** A model grades every account against the ideal customer profile and writes the score back to the CRM and the warehouse, so reps sort by it.
3. **Routing.** Rules send each lead to the right owner in minutes, with territory and round-robin logic that survives the next reorganization.
4. **Signal-based outbound.** Lists start from triggers, such as a company hiring for a role or naming a tool in its job posts, and each lead carries a reason to write.
5. **Rep assistants.** Account research, call summaries, RFP answers and Slack agents put context in front of reps on demand. Baseten's post sets the pace: a sales leader asks on Monday, and the workflow is live by Wednesday.
6. **The GTM data layer.** Identity resolution, CRM schemas and warehouse models connect people, companies, product usage and revenue. Modal's post calls its version an org map and a people graph.

## GTM engineer vs RevOps, ops, growth engineers and SDRs

The GTM engineer overlaps with four other roles. Here is how the work divides when each seat exists.

| Role | Owns | Typical output | Judged on |
|---|---|---|---|
| GTM engineer | The systems that create and work pipeline | Enrichment pipelines, scoring, agents, signal lists | Pipeline created, hours saved, rep adoption |
| RevOps | The rules of the revenue engine | Forecasts, territories, comp plans, CRM governance | Forecast accuracy, data quality |
| Sales or marketing ops | Day-to-day tool administration | Fields, workflows, list pulls | Requests closed, uptime |
| Growth engineer | The product-led funnel inside the app | Signup and activation experiments | Activation and conversion |
| SDR | Conversations with prospects | Booked meetings | Meetings and pipeline sourced |

In a small company, one person often holds two of these rows. That works as long as the job post says which row comes first.

## GTM engineer skills: three profiles behind one title

Most posts we read ask for CRM depth, APIs and webhooks, SQL, and daily use of AI tools. Past that core, the posts split into three profiles. Pick one before you write the job description.

### The ops builder

The ops builder lives in Clay, Salesforce or HubSpot, and an automation platform such as n8n, Zapier, Make or Workato. They call APIs without help, write SQL, and know the point where low-code stops scaling. Baseten's post is a good template, down to the AI coding assistants it names: Claude Code, Cursor and Codex.

### The software engineer

This profile writes production Python or TypeScript with code review, logging and error handling. Sierra wants someone who can design a database schema and debug an incident from logs alone. Vercel wants a Next.js app already shipped to real users. Expect internal apps and agents from this hire, not tables.

### The data builder

The data builder owns the models under everything else: SQL, dbt, Snowflake, identity resolution, and reverse ETL back into the CRM and ad platforms. Modal's data platform post is the clearest example of the profile.

Two habits separate strong hires in all three profiles. They know how a lead moves through the funnel and why CRM data decays over time. And they document what they build, because a system nobody can read gets switched off.

## How to hire your first GTM engineer

Decide what the first hire should build before you post the role. Every other choice follows from that list.

1. **Write down the first three systems.** Be concrete: "every demo request enriched, scored and routed within five minutes," "a weekly list of companies hiring for the role we sell to, with the right contact," "an account brief in Slack before each first call." The list picks the profile and hands the new hire a backlog on day one.
2. **Pick one profile.** Tables and CRM workflows call for the ops builder. Internal apps and agents call for the software engineer. A messy warehouse calls for the data builder. Write the post for one of them.
3. **Look where builders already are.** Start inside: the SDR who built a Clay table to skip research, the RevOps admin who taught themselves Python. Then try Clay's job board, the communities of the tools you run, and engineers who want to work closer to revenue.
4. **Test with a paid build, not a quiz.** Hand over 100 real accounts, a small data budget and three days. Ask for enrichment, a fit score, a routing rule and the cost per finished row. Then ask what breaks at ten times the volume, when an API goes down, or when one account arrives five times at once. Sierra's post lists those exact failure cases.
5. **Ask references what reps still use.** The best signal is a system the sales team still runs months after the candidate built it. Baseten's post puts it bluntly: a build isn't done until the field uses it.
6. **Plan the first 90 days.** The first two weeks are an audit of every tool and workflow. After that, ship one system every two weeks and measure adoption and hours saved, not lines of code.

If a full-time hire is premature, a contractor or GTM agency can build the first systems. Budget for someone in-house to own them afterward, because every pipeline needs a caretaker once the builder leaves.
