Shogo and Pancake both put AI agents to work. The real question is what each of them automates.

[Pancake](/) is an AI GTM team that works for founders and small B2B companies. Its agents find people showing buying signals and run outreach to them. Shogo builds the internal software your company runs on, then has that software keep rewriting itself as your needs change.

Both are interesting bets. They're not the same product.

## TL;DR

Pancake works on your pipeline: it finds business buyers and reaches out to them from your own account. Shogo replaces rented SaaS with custom apps and workflows that its agents build and maintain. If you need more buyer conversations, pick Pancake. If you want to own and evolve your internal tools, pick Shogo.

## What Shogo Actually Does

Shogo is an open-source, self-evolving agent platform. The pitch: describe what your company needs in plain English, and Shogo's agents build the internal apps, workflows, dashboards, and automations your team uses — then keep rewriting them as your needs change.

The core claim is that the SaaS-rental era is ending. Instead of buying Jira, Salesforce, or Notion and spending months customizing them, you describe what you want and Shogo builds it for you. You own the software. The agents keep improving it.

Shogo is self-hostable and open-source. The agents live inside the software they build, which means the system adapts as you use it. Founder Guru Angisetty frames it as: "the software you depend on should be auditable, forkable, and owned by you."

## What Pancake Does

Pancake finds your business buyers and starts the conversations. You add your website, and it builds the GTM Brain, a shared memory of your positioning, ideal customers, offers, proof, and objections. What works feeds back into that memory.

Its agents track six kinds of buying signal: posts on a topic you pick; engagement with a competitor, with an influencer your buyers follow or with your own posts; open roles that match your buyer; and job posts that name a tool you replace.

New leads arrive each morning, each tagged with the signal behind it. You approve them in the app or in Slack. Pancake then runs outreach from your own account on the professional social network, and the first message is a light question about the signal instead of a pitch. It also writes articles for Google and AI search, which you approve before they go out.

You can also drive Pancake from the AI tools you already use. Connect Claude, ChatGPT or Codex to its MCP server with a browser sign-in and no API key, then ask it to pull up today's leads, change a signal or start outreach. The price is [$99/month flat](/pricing) with every agent included.

## Where They Overlap

Less than the shared "AI agents" label suggests. Both believe AI should do operational work rather than advise on it, and both split that work across specialized agents. Both keep a human checkpoint: Shogo stops at gates for irreversible actions, and Pancake asks you to approve leads and articles. Past that, they share no features.

## Where They Differ

**The problem they're solving is different.** Shogo is attacking SaaS bloat — the idea that companies spend too much on rented software they don't fully control. Pancake is attacking a thin pipeline: founders who sell to businesses and don't have the time or the team to find buyers.

**The substrate is different.** Shogo builds software that its agents then operate. Pancake leaves your software alone. It works from your website, the signals it watches, and your own account on the professional network.

**The time horizon is different.** Shogo is a compounding bet: it gets more useful as it builds and rewrites your software over months. Pancake has no build phase. Its agents look for people who show signals today, not for software you will need later.

**Open source vs. hosted.** Shogo's code is open source and self-hostable, with Shogo Cloud as the hosted option. Pancake is a hosted product with nothing to install. Shogo gives you full ownership and control; Pancake gives you a working GTM team without running any infrastructure.

## The Honest Trade-Off

Shogo's self-evolution model is compelling for a specific type of company: one that's frustrated by SaaS lock-in, has the technical appetite to self-host, and wants to own the software it runs on. If you believe SaaS is over, Shogo is the early infrastructure for that bet.

Pancake is for founders who don't want to build anything. They want more conversations with the right buyers, without hiring a salesperson or spending their mornings prospecting. That problem exists today, whatever your software stack looks like.

Neither is a chatbot. Both take action instead of giving advice, on different problems.

## Five Questions to Figure Out Which One You Need

**1. What do you need more of: customers or better internal tools?**
Customers → Pancake. Internal tools you own and can change → Shogo.

**2. How technical are you?**
Self-hosting Shogo means running its open-source server yourself; Shogo Cloud removes that step. Pancake needs no technical setup: you add your website and start approving leads.

**3. Is your primary pain a thin pipeline or SaaS costs?**
Too few buyer conversations → Pancake. SaaS cost/control (renting too many tools) → Shogo.

**4. How long can you wait for the payoff?**
Shogo's self-evolution model compounds over weeks and months. Pancake puts new leads in front of you every morning once it has read your website, and the conversations it opens move at the pace of your normal sales cycle.

**5. Who do you sell to?**
If your customers are other businesses, Pancake is built for you, from [SaaS startups](/for/saas-startups) to agencies and consultancies. Shogo's value does not depend on who your customers are.
