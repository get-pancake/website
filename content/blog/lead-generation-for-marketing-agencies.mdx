**TL;DR:** Digital marketing agency lead generation repeats when you watch for public signs that a company needs help soon, instead of buying lists of people who might. Nine signs cover most agency work, from a head of growth job post to a founder asking for agency picks. Seven can be searched; two you track by hand. Qualify each lead with five questions, tag it with the signal that found it, and keep the signals that turn into calls.

The channel ranking lives in [how to get clients for a marketing agency](/blog/how-to-get-clients-for-marketing-agency). This page is the machinery under the outbound channel: where agency leads come from, what makes one worth a proposal, and how to tell which signals pay.

## Why bought lists stall for agencies

A bought list gives you names, titles and email addresses. It can't tell you who needs an agency this quarter, so every message starts without a reason to write.

- **No timing.** A head of marketing who signed an agency last month sits on the list next to one whose agency just quit. Nothing tells them apart.
- **Shared contacts.** Unless the vendor sells the list to you alone, other agencies write to the same people with the same opener.
- **Stale entries.** People change jobs after a list is compiled, and a message to last year's title shows you never looked.
- **EU rules.** For contacts in the EU, the [European Commission's guidance on third-party data](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data/can-data-received-third-party-be-used-marketing_en) says the seller must be able to show the data was collected in line with the GDPR. You must keep the list current, leave out anyone who objected, and tell each person, at the latest in your first message, that you hold their data to send them adverts.

A list still has one use: as the universe you filter. "Every B2B software company in Texas with 20 to 200 staff" is a fine starting set, as long as a signal decides who hears from you.

## Nine signals that a company needs a marketing agency

A signal is a public event that gives a company a reason to talk to an agency soon. The first seven can be searched, by hand or with a tool such as [Pancake for marketing agencies](/for/marketing-agencies). The last two need your own records.

| Signal | What it usually means | Where to see it | Who to write to |
|---|---|---|---|
| A job post for a marketing manager, head of growth or demand-gen lead | A gap the team can't fill yet, with budget behind it | Job boards and the company's careers page | The founder or the head of marketing |
| A recent funding round | A growth target and money to reach it | Funding announcements and Form D filings | The CEO or head of marketing |
| A founder asking for agency recommendations | An open search, often with a brief ready | Social posts, founder Slack groups, subreddits | The person who asked |
| A launch announcement | A date that needs demand behind it | The company's posts, Product Hunt, its changelog | Head of marketing or product marketing |
| A post about a missed pipeline target or rising acquisition costs | A problem the current setup isn't solving | Posts by the head of growth or the CEO | The person who posted |
| A rebrand or a new name | New positioning that needs a launch plan and new creative | The announcement, a new site, renamed accounts | CMO or brand lead |
| Comments on a rival agency's posts | Interest in the problem that agency solves | Comment threads under the rival's posts | The marketer who commented |
| A new marketing leader | A fresh look at agencies and budgets | Team pages and announcements | The new leader, a few weeks in |
| A former client contact in a new job | Trust you already earned, with a new budget | Your own contact list | Your former contact |

Three rows need a closer look.

**Funding.** Press coverage misses the rounds companies don't announce. Filings catch some of them: a US company that raises under Rule 504 or 506 of Regulation D must file a Form D within 15 days of the first sale, according to the [SEC's Form D guidance](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/filing-form-d-notice), and the filing goes through EDGAR. [EDGAR's search tools](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings) are free to the public, with full-text search across more than 20 years of filings. [Selling to recently funded startups](/plays/recently-funded-startups) covers when to write after a round.

**The last two rows.** No job board announces that your old client contact moved, or that a company hired a new CMO. Keep a spreadsheet and check it each quarter. When a company loses its marketing lead, though, the replacement job post shows up under the first signal.

## What the job post says before you write

A job post is the richest signal on the list, because the company describes its own gap in writing. Read the whole post. Four kinds of lines matter:

- **"Manage our agency partners."** The company already buys from agencies and plans to keep doing so. Your pitch is to be one of them, or to replace the one that's underdelivering.
- **"Bring paid media in-house."** The company is moving work away from agencies. Offer a bridge while they hire, or training for the team they're building, and skip the retainer pitch.
- **"Our first marketing hire" or "wear many hats."** One person will carry everything and will need hands for the channels they can't run alone.
- **Tools named in the post.** HubSpot, Marketo, Google Ads or Klaviyo tell you which platforms you'd work in, and whether your certifications match.

Write to the person the new hire will report to. [Finding companies by the roles they're hiring for](/plays/companies-hiring) shows the search side.

## Digital marketing agency lead generation: three feeds, three jobs

Signals are one of three feeds into an agency's pipeline. Each does a different job, so plan for all three.

- **Inbound** (your site, case studies, directory profiles, client referrals) brings the highest intent, on the buyer's schedule. You can't make it arrive this month.
- **Partners** (web studios, PR firms, fractional CMOs) bring trust you didn't have to earn. Their volume depends on their pipeline, not yours.
- **Outbound timed to a signal** is the feed you can turn up. Without a reason in every message, it turns back into a bought list.

Give each feed a share of your target. If referrals and partners can bring 7 of the 12 new clients you need this year (example numbers), outbound has to bring the other 5. If your niche moves on other triggers, the guide to [buying signals](/blog/buying-signals-b2b) lists more of them.

## Qualify an agency lead in five questions

A lead earns a proposal when you can answer five questions, most of them from public information before the first call.

1. **Why now?** Name the dated reason: the job post, the round, the launch. Without one, there's no urgency to work with.
2. **Who owns the budget?** The founder at a 20-person company; the CMO at a 200-person one. A coordinator can't sign a retainer.
3. **Does your floor fit?** If your smallest retainer is $6,000 a month (an example), a four-person company with no funding rarely clears it.
4. **Who does the work today?** Nobody, a stretched generalist, or another agency. Each one needs a different pitch.
5. **How did the last agency end?** Ask this on the call. "They never understood our product" and "we couldn't see what we paid for" are briefs in disguise.

Write the five answers on one line per lead in your CRM. A lead with three blanks isn't ready for a proposal; set a date to look at it again.

## Track leads by signal, then cut the weak ones

At the end of each quarter, ask which signal produced clients. Tag every lead with the signal that found it, and read four numbers per signal: leads, replies, calls and wins.

Say a quarter shows this (made-up numbers): hiring posts gave 60 leads, 9 replies, 4 calls and 1 client; founder requests gave 12 leads, 6 replies, 4 calls and 2 clients; rival-agency comments gave 40 leads, 3 replies and no calls. Keep the first two. Give the third one more quarter with a narrower audience, then drop it if nothing changes.

Two traps. Don't judge a signal on a handful of contacts. And don't blame the signal when the message is the problem: if replies are low on every signal at once, rewrite the first message before you touch the lists.
