**TL;DR:** An AI SDR is software that does a sales development rep's prospecting job: it pulls leads from a contact database, researches them, writes personalized outreach, sends it (mostly by email), answers replies and books meetings. It is strong on volume and on follow-ups that never slip. It is weak on timing, on deliverability at scale, and on saying what only someone who knows your market would say. The other model keeps your own agent as the brain and adds GTM infrastructure that finds people with a reason to talk and starts the conversation under your name.

## What is an AI SDR?

An AI SDR is software sold to do a sales development rep's job, end to end or in part, with a language model writing and deciding at each step. The human job it copies fills the pipeline for account executives: build lists, research accounts, send first messages, follow up, qualify replies and book meetings.

Vendors brand the idea differently. Artisan sells Ava as "the AI BDR". 11x sells agents for voice, chat, email, social and SMS. HubSpot calls its version a prospecting agent. Under the names, the category splits in two:

- **Outbound AI SDRs** find new people and write to them first.
- **Inbound AI SDRs** answer people who came to you: a form fill, a chat on your site, a demo request. 11x, for one, says its agents respond to inbound leads in under 60 seconds.

The rest of this page is about outbound AI SDRs, the kind sold as a replacement for prospecting work.

## How an AI SDR works, step by step

Most outbound AI SDRs run the same seven steps. The vendors differ in how much of each one you can see and change.

1. **You describe the buyer.** Titles, industries, company sizes and regions, or a plain-English prompt.
2. **It pulls a list.** From the vendor's contact database or your CRM. HubSpot's agent sources contacts from providers such as ZoomInfo and Apollo.
3. **It enriches and researches.** Work emails and phone numbers through a chain of data providers, plus web research on each company.
4. **It writes.** A first message and follow-ups for each person. Artisan says Ava tests dozens of message variations per campaign and shifts volume toward the winners.
5. **It sends.** Mostly email, from mailboxes and domains the vendor buys and warms up. AiSDR sets up sending on lookalike domains and keeps them warm; Artisan sends in your reps' names from your own sending domains.
6. **It handles replies.** It sorts answers, responds to objections and books meetings, or drafts a reply for a person to check.
7. **It hands off.** The booked meeting lands on a rep's calendar and in the CRM.

## What AI SDRs do well

AI SDRs are strongest at the steady, repetitive part of the job, the part people let slip.

- **Coverage.** Software can work every account in a territory, including the long tail reps never reach.
- **Follow-up that never slips.** The third message goes out on schedule, whatever else happens that week.
- **Speed on inbound.** An agent answers a form fill at 2 a.m. as fast as at noon.
- **Testing at volume.** With enough sends, the agent can compare message variants and move volume to the one that gets answers.
- **Records.** Every touch is logged where the team can see it.

For a sales team with reps to feed, a large market and email as its main channel, those strengths are worth paying for.

## Where AI SDRs fail

The weak spots are as consistent as the strengths. Six come up again and again.

1. **Fit without timing.** A database list says who could buy. Without an event behind each name, a well-written message still lands at a random moment.
2. **Deliverability at volume.** Cold email at scale means new domains, warm-up and spam reports. [Gmail's sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en) ask senders to keep their spam rate below 0.10% and never reach 0.30%, which is three spam reports per 1,000 messages. Lookalike domains protect your main domain, at the cost of writing from an address the buyer has never seen.
3. **Research that isn't a reason.** "Congrats on the growth" plus a line from the About page is personalization in name only. Buyers recognize an assembled message.
4. **Your name, the vendor's judgment.** The agent writes and answers under your reps' names, so a wrong fact or a tone-deaf reply is yours. Review modes help, but reading every message at volume becomes a job of its own.
5. **Memory you don't keep.** What the agent learned about your market lives inside the vendor's product. Switch vendors and you start over.
6. **Cost per conversation.** Per-person prices look small until you divide by replies. At $1.50 per person, 1,000 people cost $1,500 before anyone answers.

## The other model: your agent plus GTM infrastructure

If you already work in Claude, ChatGPT or Codex, there is a second way to get the SDR job done. Your own agent stays the brain: it reads a stored memory of your business, picks the audience and drafts in your voice. GTM infrastructure supplies what a chat can't: people data with timing, a channel under your name, and a place where searches keep running after the chat closes. [AI GTM](/blog/ai-gtm) explains each of those pieces.

In practice the two models differ on five points:

- **Who sets the audience.** You and your agent, one sentence at a time, instead of a vendor's playbook.
- **Where the memory lives.** In a brain your agent reads and you correct, not inside a vendor's model of you.
- **Where a lead starts.** From a reason, such as a hire, a round or a post, not only from a database filter.
- **Whose voice.** Messages from your own account, under your name, with no persona in between.
- **How it connects.** Over MCP, the open protocol agents use to call tools, so you keep the agent you already pay for.

[Buying signals in B2B](/blog/buying-signals-b2b) lists the reasons worth acting on, and [AI agents for sales](/blog/ai-agents-for-sales) covers how to set your own agent up safely.

## AI SDR, human SDR or your own agent

Match the choice to how your company sells.

- **Buy an AI SDR** if you have reps who need meetings, a large addressable market and email as your main channel. [AiSDR alternatives](/blog/aisdr-alternatives) and [Artisan alternatives](/blog/artisan-alternatives) compare the vendors; their prices date from September 2026, so check the current ones.
- **Hire a human SDR** if your deals are large and multi-threaded, or you want someone to write the playbook the next hires follow. [Pancake vs hiring a BDR](/blog/pancake-vs-hiring-a-bdr) runs the cost of a first hire.
- **Run your own agent with GTM infrastructure** if you are a founder or a small B2B team, your buyers hire, raise money and post in public, and you want to see why each lead was picked.
