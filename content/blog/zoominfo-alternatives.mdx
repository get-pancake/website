## What ZoomInfo does that small tools don't

ZoomInfo's depth is real. Its homepage cites verified data on more than 500 million contacts and 100 million companies, built from public web sources, company websites, regulatory filings, job postings and a contributory network, with more than 300 human researchers checking records. On top sit intent signals, website visitor identification, org charts, buying groups, champion tracking and AI agents that work from its GTM Context Graph. Claude and ChatGPT connect through its MCP server.

There is a free way in, too. ZoomInfo Lite is a permanent free plan with no card, and the full-platform trial typically runs 7 days without one.

If you sell to large enterprises and map buying groups across named accounts, none of the tools below matches that depth. The case for leaving is narrower: a small team that needs contacts and conversations, and can't justify a contract to get them.

## Why small teams walk away: quotes, licenses and credit minimums

- **No price before a sales conversation.** [ZoomInfo's pricing page](https://www.zoominfo.com/pricing) lists the Professional, Copilot Advanced and Copilot Enterprise packages, then a form.
- **Licenses plus credits.** The page says the price depends on features, the number of licenses and credit usage. Each export of a contact or company profile costs one credit, whether it comes from the platform, the Chrome extension or an integration.
- **Integrations carry minimums.** The same FAQ says out-of-the-box integrations typically have a base cost and require a minimum purchase of bulk credits.
- **Intent costs extra.** Streaming Intent is an add-on, priced by the keywords and the accounts you track.
- **Billing terms are negotiated.** Asked about monthly payments, the FAQ says the sales team works out payment frequency with you.
- **The work after the export stays yours.** A database hands over contact records. Someone still decides who to contact, writes to them and follows up.

## 1. Pancake (our product): buyers with a reason, one price per workspace

ZoomInfo tells you who works where. Pancake tells you who should hear from you now, and why. It builds a GTM Brain from your website, then turns a one-sentence brief into a Play that runs on the schedule you pick, such as "Find finance leads at US companies with 50 to 500 staff that are hiring an accounts payable specialist."

Three signals are worth starting with: companies hiring for a role your product supports, [startups that recently raised](/plays/recently-funded-startups), and people engaging with a competitor's posts. Every lead comes with one line on why it fits, and you can see why others were set aside. The first message asks about the signal: "Saw you're hiring an AP specialist. Is that for invoice volume, or a backlog?" You can edit it before it goes out as a direct message from your own account, and a reply stops the sequence.

**Pricing (November 2026):** from [$99 a month per workspace](/pricing) with 60,000 credits, an estimated 120 to 600 leads a month. Teammates are free, and each extra sending account costs $39 a month. The 7-day free trial includes 30,000 credits, and a card is required to start it.

**How it differs from ZoomInfo:** the price is on the page and set per workspace, not per license. It isn't a database to browse, and it sends no email, makes no calls and identifies no website visitors.

**Pick it if** you're a team of one to five that wants conversations more than contact records.

## 2. Apollo: the closest swap, with a free plan

Apollo covers most of what small teams buy ZoomInfo for. Its database lists more than 240 million contacts and 30 million accounts, with buying signals for intent, job changes and website visits, plus sequences, a dialer and AI agents that research accounts and draft outreach.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Apollo's pricing page](https://www.apollo.io/pricing) starts with a Free plan of 900 credits per seat per year. Basic costs $49 per seat a month billed annually, or $65 monthly, Professional $79 billed annually, and Organization $119 per seat with a three-seat minimum. An email costs 1 credit and a phone number 8. Basic and Professional come with a 14-day trial.

**How it differs from ZoomInfo:** the prices are public and monthly billing is an option. ZoomInfo's homepage counts about twice as many contacts and goes deeper on hierarchies and buying groups. Our [Apollo alternatives guide](/blog/apollo-alternatives) covers the credit and seat limits.

**Pick it if** you want ZoomInfo's all-in-one shape at a price you can see before a call.

## 3. Lusha: seats bundled into small plans

Lusha sells verified emails, phone numbers and company data, with intent topics, lookalikes and signals on the higher plans. It works from a web workspace, a Chrome extension, an API and an MCP server for Claude and ChatGPT, and every plan lists automated email sequences.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Lusha's pricing page](https://www.lusha.com/pricing/) lists a Free plan with 40 credits a month, one seat and no card. Starter costs $37.45 a month billed yearly, or $49.90 monthly. Pro costs $52.45 billed yearly with two seats, and Premium $299.95 with five seats and 40,800 credits a year. An email costs 1 credit and a phone number 5, and monthly Pro and Premium plans roll unused credits over up to twice the monthly amount.

**How it differs from ZoomInfo:** seats come in small bundles with a price attached, and you can pay month to month. ZoomInfo's company data goes further, with hierarchies and org charts.

**Pick it if** your team of up to five calls prospects and wants direct dials at a known price.

## 4. RocketReach: lookups from $33 a month

RocketReach searches 700 million professionals and 60 million companies for verified work and personal emails and phone numbers. Every plan includes sequences with up to 500 emails a day, plus MCP and API access. Pro adds CRM integrations, and Ultimate adds intent data.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [RocketReach's pricing page](https://rocketreach.co/pricing) gives free accounts 5 lookups with no card. Essentials costs $33 a month billed annually ($399 a year) for emails only, or $69 monthly. Pro, with phone numbers, costs $75 a month billed annually or $119 monthly. Ultimate, with intent data, costs $142 billed annually. A lookup that finds no verified email or phone is refunded, and team plans start at $6,000 a year.

**How it differs from ZoomInfo:** self-serve and priced per seat on the page, with an email-only entry plan. ZoomInfo bundles more company intelligence into each license.

**Pick it if** one or two people need contact details more than account research, and annual billing suits you.

## 5. UpLead: month-to-month credits, no contract

UpLead sells contact data by the credit: one credit buys one contact, with a verified email and a mobile direct dial, ready to download or export to your CRM. Plus adds technographics, data enrichment and suppression list uploads.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [UpLead's pricing page](https://www.uplead.com/pricing/) offers a 7-day free trial with 5 credits. Essentials starts at $74 a month billed annually for 2,040 credits a year, or $99 on monthly billing for 170 credits a month. Plus costs $149 a month billed annually, or $199 monthly for 400 credits. Professional, with buyer intent and full API access, is annual-only and priced on request. Monthly plans carry no contract.

**How it differs from ZoomInfo:** you can cancel month to month and every credit maps to one contact. Essentials and Plus cover one user each, and multi-seat teams go to Professional, which is quoted.

**Pick it if** one seller wants verified mobiles for a few hundred contacts a month without a commitment.

## 6. Hunter: email only, unlimited users

Hunter is built around email: Discover, its B2B database, plus Domain Search, Email Finder, Email Verifier, sequences and signals from your existing leads. Its plans list no phone numbers. Every plan, the free one included, covers unlimited team members who share one pool of credits.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Hunter's pricing page](https://hunter.io/pricing) lists a Free plan with 50 credits a month. Starter costs $34 a month billed yearly ($408 a year), or $49 monthly, for 2,000 credits a month and three connected email accounts. Growth costs $104 billed yearly, or $149 monthly, for 10,000 credits. A found email costs 1 credit, a verification half a credit, and a search that finds nothing is free.

**How it differs from ZoomInfo:** no phones and no intent topics, but one price covers the whole team, and you can subscribe for a single month and cancel.

**Pick it if** your outreach runs on email and everyone on the team needs access.

## 7. Cognism: the Europe pick, for teams of five or more

Cognism sells B2B data with a European focus: phone-verified mobile numbers, emails, job titles and work history, technology usage, and signals for hiring, funding, job changes and intent. Its homepage says director-level data and above is refreshed on a 30-day basis, and MCP and API access are included.

**Pricing (November 2026):** [Cognism's pricing page](https://www.cognism.com/pricing) shows two prospecting packages, Standard and Pro, each with five seats included and pricing on request. One credit reveals one contact. Your team keeps using that contact for the rest of the contract, and it costs a credit again only if the person changes jobs. Pro adds Bombora intent data and on-demand mobile verification.

**How it differs from ZoomInfo:** stronger focus on Europe and on calling, but the same quote-based buying process. Its own FAQ says teams with at least five salespeople often see the most value.

**Pick it if** you have five or more sellers calling into Europe and a contract fits your budget.

## Five questions to ask before you sign any data contract

Small teams overpay when they compare entry prices instead of terms. Send these five questions to each vendor in writing, ZoomInfo included, before the demo.

1. **What does one credit buy?** A phone number costs 8 credits on Apollo and 5 on Lusha, while UpLead's credit includes both email and mobile.
2. **Do unused credits roll over?** Apollo's expire at the end of each billing cycle. Lusha rolls them over on monthly Pro and Premium plans.
3. **How are seats counted?** Apollo puts every seat on the same plan. Hunter counts no seats at all. ZoomInfo's homepage FAQ talks about consumption credits rather than fixed seat tiers, while its pricing page lists licenses as a price factor, so ask which applies to your quote.
4. **What happens to a contact you already paid for?** Cognism lets you reuse a revealed contact through the contract, and Hunter counts a repeated search once per billing period.
5. **What does leaving cost?** Apollo doesn't allow mid-cycle downgrades on annual plans, and ZoomInfo's integrations come with credit minimums. Ask for the renewal terms before you sign.
